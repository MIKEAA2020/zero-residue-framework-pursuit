# Spinozist Immanent Productivity Test Protocol v1
## Predeclared A0 test for finite modes, immanent causation, and necessitation

## Status

This protocol predeclares the next source reconstruction before its substantive verdict. It tests the productive/generative escape class identified in `Point-Sphere_A0_Escape_Class_Stress_Test_Primitive_and_Generative_v1.md`.

It does not assume that Spinoza is a successful account of actuality merely because he has explicit definitions of substance, attribute, mode, immanent cause, and necessity. It also does not classify the view as a failure merely because it has primitives or treats causation as necessary.

## 1. Source target

The reconstruction will be based principally on Spinoza’s *Ethics*, Part I:

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\mathrm{E1d3} &: \text{substance is in itself and conceived through itself};\\
\mathrm{E1d4} &: \text{attribute is what intellect perceives as constituting substance’s essence};\\
\mathrm{E1d5} &: \text{mode is an affection of substance, in another and conceived through another};\\
\mathrm{E1p15} &: \text{whatever is, is in God; nothing can be or be conceived without God};\\
\mathrm{E1p16} &: \text{infinitely many things follow from divine nature’s necessity};\\
\mathrm{E1p18} &: \text{God is immanent, not transitive, cause};\\
\mathrm{E1p25} &: \text{God causes both existence and essence of things};\\
\mathrm{E1p28} &: \text{a finite determinate thing requires another finite determinate cause, ad infinitum};\\
\mathrm{E1p29,33} &: \text{noncontingency and no-other-order claims.}
\end{array}
\]

The source inquiry must distinguish Spinoza’s own text from competing contemporary readings of whether particular finite modes are absolutely necessary, merely conditionally necessary, or necessary only as members of a whole modal system.

## 2. Strongest charitable candidate criterion

Let \(S\) be the one substance and \(a\) an attribute. The basic type rule is:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf{Substance}_{S}(x)&\iff
\mathsf{InItself}(x)\land\mathsf{ConceivedThroughItself}(x),\\
\mathsf{Mode}_{S}(x)&\iff
\mathsf{InAnother}(x)\land\mathsf{ConceivedThroughAnother}(x).
\end{aligned}
\]

The strongest productive actualization proposal is:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf{ActualFiniteMode}_{S}(x)\
\iff&\;
\mathsf{Finite}_{S}(x)
\land\mathsf{Mode}_{S}(x)
\land\mathsf{InAttribute}(x,a)\\
&\land\mathsf{NecessarilyDetermined}_{S}(x)\\
&\land\exists y\,[
\mathsf{Finite}_{S}(y)
\land\mathsf{InAttribute}(y,a)
\land\mathsf{Determines}_{S}(y,x)
].
\end{aligned}
\]

This must not be read as a verbatim Spinoza formula. It is a test reconstruction of the proposal that immanent causal necessity does more than classify an already actual finite thing: it makes that thing actual as part of the productive order of nature.

## 3. Central discriminating questions

The dossier must answer, separately:

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
Q_1 &: \text{Does E1d5 give a public type criterion for mode rather than substance/property?}\\
Q_2 &: \text{Does E1p25 plus E1p28 give an actuality criterion or only a causal condition on}\
&\text{already existing finite things?}\\
Q_3 &: \text{Does E1p29/33 establish that the particular finite profile is actual/necessary,}\
&\text{or only that the actual order is deterministic once given?}\\
Q_4 &: \text{Can immanent causation meet \(G_1\text{--}G_3\): actual base, actual productive law,}\
&\text{and realization of a finite metaphysical mode?}\\
Q_5 &: \text{Can the account decide particularity, symmetry, and re-encoding cases without}\
&\text{an unannounced exact-world selector?}
\end{array}
\]

## 4. Interpretive bifurcation: no forced verdict on necessitarianism

The dossier must preserve at least two live readings where the sources warrant them:

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
N &: \text{the complete finite modal order follows necessarily from God/Nature,}\
&\text{so particular finite modes are necessary in the relevant whole-order sense};\\
D &: \text{finite modes are determined through an infinite finite-cause nexus, but their}\
&\text{necessity does not follow directly from God’s absolute nature.}
\end{array}
\]

The A0 result may not be made to turn on a prematurely declared winner between \(N\) and \(D\). A success must meet A0 on either viable reading; a failure must be shared or clearly labelled reading-relative.

## 5. Predeclared adversarial battery

| Test | Required Spinozist answer |
|---|---|
| T0 | Can the view articulate a mode-empty finite order, and if not, does necessary productivity establish nonemptiness without asserting the desired profile? |
| T1 | Given one proposed finite candidate, what makes it an actual mode rather than a merely conceivable affection? |
| T2 | Given a symmetric finite pair, does causal determination or a mode definition decide one, two, or an orbit without names or imported histories? |
| T3 | Can `in another / conceived through another` separate a real mode from a property, proposition, or abstract description? |
| T4 | Can an allegedly independent finite candidate be classified without simply relying on substance monism as the conclusion? |
| T5 | Is there a profile extractor under which substance/mode and causal-necessity verdicts are conserved through a translation? |
| T6 | Does an additional candidate become actual by derivation from the productive order, and can the rule distinguish actual derivation from a merely formal description? |
| \(G_1\) | What makes the productive source/base actual? |
| \(G_2\) | What makes the immanent causal order/law actual rather than a formal rule? |
| \(G_3\) | What bridges determined formal/causal output to genuine finite metaphysical reality? |

## 6. Fixed rivals

The reconstruction must distinguish the target from:

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\mathsf{FormalClosure}(x) &: x\text{ is merely a member of a mathematically defined closure};\\
\mathsf{PropertyOnly}(x,S) &: x\text{ is a predicate of }S\text{, not a real finite mode};\\
\mathsf{DependenceOnly}(x,S) &: x\text{ is dependent but actuality is not explained};\\
\mathsf{IndependentFinite}(x) &: x\text{ is an independently real finite substance.}
\end{array}
\]

The dossier may not claim a rival defeat without separate truth conditions for the relevant alternatives.

## 7. Outcome rules

### Limited productive A0 pass

A pass requires an explicit, noncircular account joining:

\[
\mathsf{ModeType}(x)
\land\mathsf{Actuality}(x)
\land\mathsf{ProductiveNecessitation}(x),
\]

with determinate T0–T6 verdicts, a profile-invariance resource, a non-question-begging response to \(G_1\text{--}G_3\), and a public error condition.

### Structural generative failure

A failure is structural only if the proposal must either:

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
F_1 &: \text{take actual finite items as the causal relata before production can operate};\\
F_2 &: \text{treat the whole exact causal profile as given while using it to generate that profile};\\
F_3 &: \text{equate causal determination with actual existence without a realization bridge};\\
F_4 &: \text{rely on a mode/property/substance boundary that cannot be publicly applied};\\
F_5 &: \text{lack a profile-conservative interpretation of its causal and type predicates.}
\end{array}
\]

### Inconclusive result

An outcome is inconclusive if it rests on an unsettled translation or an interpretation-specific reading of the finite-mode necessity debate, unless the conclusion is explicitly conditional on that reading.

## Nonclaim

This protocol does not assume that Spinoza is a theist, a pantheist, a necessitarian about every finite thing, an adequate physical theory, or a correct whole-reality framework. It isolates a specific claim: that immanent productive necessity may provide what prior candidates lacked—a public route from a ground to actual finite modes.
