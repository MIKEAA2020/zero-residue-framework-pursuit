\[
\prec_C\subset C\times C,

## A tempting selector was tested and withheld

A stronger requirement would demand that every adjacent pair of contexts share at least two contents, allowing a nontrivial ordered record relation to be jointly present in every overlap.

In this finite test, that condition selects only the IAR-like diamond.

It was **not used**. At AP3 level, internal finite self-presentation does not independently require every pair of modes to share a two-content public record. Adding that condition would be IAR-biased unless it can be independently derived.

The condition is instead a legitimate candidate for the next layer, AP4, where publicly stable/redundant records—not merely finite self-presentation—are the explicit subject.

## AP3 result

The conditional aware-ground framework now determines the formal mode grammar only up to a structural family:

> bounded, immanently self-presenting, non-objectifying, nonexclusive, overlap-connected finite contexts, equipped where appropriate with internally coherent retentional order.

No exact compatibility topology has been derived. That is an honest structural-family result, not a failure and not an IAR theorem.

## Transition to AP4

AP4 can now begin at the family level. Its question is no longer which finite contexts are modes, but:

> Under what independently stated conditions do records become public, stable, and cross-perspectivally compatible among the qualified modes?

AP4 may test redundancy and shared-record structure across the five-family class. It must not retroactively smuggle a public-record requirement into AP3 merely to select IAR, and it must preserve the distinction between internal retentional order and background physical time.
