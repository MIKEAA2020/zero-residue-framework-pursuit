# AP3P2K Unified Self-Presentation Grammar-Derivation Audit v1 — Report
## The three debts share an interface, but the proposed four-feature derivation does not discharge them together

## What is right in the proposal

Grammar adequacy, valuation enrichment, and component boundaries all concern one broad interface:

> How does a finite formal grammar represent genuine self-presenting mode-content?

It is therefore correct to seek an integrated account rather than treating arbitrary notation, an arbitrary enrichment, and arbitrary component boundaries as unrelated issues.

The proposed finite-mode features are also appropriate constraints to test:

1. immanent reflexivity;
2. retentional thickness;
3. boundedness;
4. maximality.

## What needs correction

The four features do not all follow from the protected identity with the same force.

| Feature | Current standing |
|---|---|
| Immanent reflexivity | Conditional phenomenological/self-presentation typing constraint; does not select a formal graph. |
| Retentional thickness | Weakly motivated by temporal self-presentation; AP3N’s order is added, and AP3O proves it does not derive successor flow. |
| Boundedness | Has real force only in the proper-maximal-context implementation, where it excludes universal compatibility. |
| Maximality | An AP3G implementation choice for finite contexts, not a grammar-independent definition of every mode. |

Thus one may correctly say that these are constraints on an intended finite self-presentation programme. One may not yet say that all four are theorems of the circular core, or that they uniquely determine a grammar.

## The proposed family derivation has already been run

AP3L applied the relevant identity-derived constraints—immanence, boundedness/properness, nonobjectification, and real-boundary label quotienting—and obtained:
\[
37\ \text{labelled candidates}
\longrightarrow
5\ \text{structural grammar families}.
\]

AP3M's immanent-horizon constraints leave all five families viable. AP3N's endogenous retentional order leaves all five viable. AP3O then proves the decisive limit:

> Static retentional order does not derive a successor/continuation relation or an intrinsic unceasing flow.

Therefore the honest output of the four-feature programme is:
\[
\mathcal F_{\rm admissible}\quad\text{(a family)},
\]
not a uniquely derived current AP3P2 signature.

All audit checks pass.

## Why this does not automatically discharge Debt 1

A family of necessary structural conditions does not entail grammar adequacy for one selected member:
\[
\operatorname{GenuineContent}(x)
\Rightarrow
\operatorname{Represented}_{\Sigma}(x).
\]

A grammar can meet every current finite-self-presentation constraint while failing to represent some grounded intrinsic relation. To infer adequacy requires a further claim that the selected grammar exhausts the relevant mode-content. That is not obtained merely by calling it “minimal.”

## Why this does not automatically discharge Debt 2

The proposed enrichment test is too strong if it says:
\[
\neg\operatorname{RequiredForEveryFiniteMode}(J)
\Rightarrow
\neg\operatorname{GenuineContent}(J).
\]

A base grammar \(M\) can satisfy all four conditions, and an enriched grammar \(M+J\) can also satisfy them, with \(J\) a genuine content of that enriched mode without being constitutive of every finite mode.

So the proper distinction is:

- **constitutive/minimal grammar constraints:** what every member of a targeted class must instantiate;
- **admissible genuine enrichments:** additional content that may characterize some modes but not all;
- **mere notation:** structure not represented or warranted as content at all.

The four features can constrain the first class. They cannot exclude the second merely through nonnecessity. Excluding it would be a minimal-grammar policy, not a core theorem.

## Why this does not automatically discharge Debt 3

If a successor relation is already supplied, connected components are maximally connected subgraphs. But AP3L maximality concerns co-present phase contexts, while AP3P1 components concern a stipulated successor graph.

Hence:
\[
\text{maximal phase context}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{maximal successor component is a mode}.
\]

A component-to-mode identity bridge is still needed, as is independent warrant for the successor relation itself.

## Correct unified target

The best integrated research target is:
\[
\text{finite self-presentation features}
\Longrightarrow
\text{a family of admissible grammar constraints}.
\]

For each explicitly selected or derived grammar member, its coordinate quotients can be calculated. The AP3P2I/J result then says that its minimal lift presentations form one connected description groupoid under the local-fibre reading.

But no family constraint alone converts that description-class fact into a grammar-independent identity of genuine modes, rules out every optional genuine enrichment, or makes successor components non-stipulative.

## Conclusion

The proposal gives the correct architectural orientation—seek one account of the grammar/content interface—but overstates what the four features can derive. They have already been tested and yield a constrained family, not a single adequate grammar. The remaining debts are linked but not identical:

1. grammar adequacy: does a selected grammar exhaust relevant content?
2. enrichment: does a proposed added relation characterize some genuine modes?
3. boundary: does a specified successor component instantiate one genuine finite mode?

A new theorem could still address all three if it independently establishes a much stronger self-presentation adequacy principle. Until then, the programme must retain the family result and label each additional bridge explicitly.
