## 1. Exact result

Under strongest CI, an ultimate with real constituents and an unexplained unity fact fails:

\[
\boxed{
\mathsf{SCI}(F)
\land
\mathsf{RealConstituents}_F(G)
\land
\mathsf{ResidualUnity}_F(G)
\Rightarrow
\bot.
}
\tag{CR*}
\]

This is a genuine conditional result. A bare union of independently real ultimate constituents creates a named difference/unity fact that must be explained or is an SCI residue.

But it does not establish the stronger inference:

\[
\boxed{
\mathsf{SCI}(F)
\not\Rightarrow
\mathsf{AbsoluteSimplicity}(G).
}
\tag{CI3-NE}
\]

To reach absolute simplicity, one needs the additional composition-reflection bridge:

\[
\mathsf{RealConstituents}_F(G)
\Rightarrow
\mathsf{ResidualUnity}_F(G).
\tag{CR-strong}
\]

That bridge must show that every purported intrinsic explanation of ultimate unity is circular, inadequate, or a disguised primitive. SCI itself does not do that work automatically.

## 3. Consequences for leading framework families

### Simple-ground candidates

Classical simple-ground and nondual non-structural-ground candidates avoid the specific residual-unity problem if their simplicity is coherent. CI3 does not yet validate either their self-intelligibility, awareness, goodness, or finite-reality relation.

### Necessary totality and structural candidates

An articulated necessary totality or structural ultimate cannot pass merely by asserting “the structure is necessary.” But it is not eliminated merely for having internal articulation. It owes an account of why the articulation and its unifying principle are intrinsically intelligible rather than primitive.

### Nondual real-aspect candidates

Finite aspect articulation cannot be classified as composition in the ground merely because there are many finite contents. The candidate must still explain why aspects are genuine, real, non-gauge, and non-composite. That is a CI4 relation burden, not an answer granted by CI3.

### Participation, emanation, and creation candidates

External finite effects are not constituents of the ground by definition, but their source–effect relation, exact output, and selector rule still require CI4/CI5 explanation. Externality is not a free SCI pass.

## 4. Corrected use of “simplicity”

The transcript’s route

\[
\text{self-intelligible ultimate}
\Rightarrow
\text{absolutely simple pure act}
\]

is not yet established. It combines at least three claims:

1. ultimate real composition needs an explanation;
2. no intrinsic explanation of constituent unity can succeed; and
3. simplicity is coherent and explanatory rather than merely a terminus label.

CI3 supports only the first in the restricted residual-unity form. The latter two remain bridge questions.

The doctrine of radical divine simplicity is therefore a serious candidate theory, not a neutral restatement of complete intelligibility. Its own coherence problem must be addressed rather than bypassed.

## 5. Current strong-CI profile

After CI0–CI3, a framework must:

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\text{avoid a named ultimate brute fact;}\\
&\text{avoid an unexplained explanatory whole, whether finite, circular, or infinite;}\\
&\text{explain any real ultimate unity/difference it posits;}\\
&\text{not treat descriptive plurality as automatic ontological composition;}\\
&\text{not treat a claimed intrinsic unity as automatically successful.}
\end{aligned}
\]

This narrows the space without selecting a framework.

## Nonclaims

CI3 does not prove or disprove absolute simplicity, a simple aware ground, creation, participation, emanation, plenitude, real aspects, phenomenal consciousness, or any physical theory. It only closes the shortcut from “CI rejects a brute union” to “CI proves one exact simplicity doctrine.”
