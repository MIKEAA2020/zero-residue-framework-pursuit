# T6 Physical-Postulate Novelty Audit v1
## Executed audit of existing Point–Sphere formal resources

## Decision question

T1–T5 establish that clock selection cannot be obtained from an invariant record score, WKB decoherence, or the tested articulation proxy alone. T6 asks a narrower and decisive question:

> Does the existing Point–Sphere framework already contain a specified **new physical postulate** that can break the remaining symmetry and make a distinct problem-of-time prediction?

This is an audit of material already in `Point-Sphere_Consolidated_Framework.md`, the active Problem-of-Time protocol, and completed T1–T5 results. It does not demand that a metaphysical framework be an empirical theory by definition. It checks whether it presently is one in this particular application.

## Gate definition

A candidate physical addition must provide all six items below.

| Gate | Requirement |
|---|---|
| N1 | **Mathematical object:** a declared equation or rule—e.g. action/constraint modification, boundary-state measure, observable, or probability law—not only a label for a desired outcome. |
| N2 | **Physical variables and status:** variables, state space, dimensions/parameters, and transformation or gauge status are specified. |
| N3 | **Non-redundancy:** it changes the allowed physical histories, their weights, or an observable relative to a named baseline theory. |
| N4 | **Time relevance:** it supplies a physical orientation/clock result without assuming external time, already selected WKB branch, or preferred record factorisation. |
| N5 | **Recovery:** it states a semiclassical/GR and ordinary-QM recovery regime, including local proper-time interpretation. |
| N6 | **Discrimination:** it produces at least one quantitative result distinguishable from the baseline or an exact new no-go/recovery theorem. |

A candidate that misses any item is not rejected as metaphysics or heuristic. It simply is not yet a distinct physical solution of the Problem of Time.

## Conditional novelty lemma

Let \(T\) be a baseline physical theory and \(K\) a framework addition. If \(K\) introduces neither (i) a new/different equation or constraint, (ii) a state/boundary measure, (iii) an observable with operational content, nor (iv) a probability/selection rule, then it does not alter the physical projection of the solution space:
\[
\pi_{\rm phys}\!\bigl[\operatorname{Sol}(T\wedge K)\bigr]
=
\pi_{\rm phys}\!\bigl[\operatorname{Sol}(T)\bigr].
\]
This is conditional and deliberately modest. It does **not** say that interpretive or ontological claims are meaningless. It says that, without one of the listed additions, no changed physical prediction follows merely from adding those claims.

## Closest existing mathematical item: why it does not pass

The finite graph-field model is the only listed component with an explicit evolution equation. Its external parameter is visible:
\[
d\Psi=-P\nabla F\,dt+\sqrt{2/\beta}\,P\,dW.
\]
It can be useful for its stated task—testing bounded domains, record-like organisation, and candidate perspective conditions. But it cannot be repurposed as a solution of the Problem of Time without changing its foundations:

1. its \(t\) is already an external stochastic evolution parameter;
2. it is not a Hamiltonian-constrained, diffeomorphism-invariant system;
3. its graph locality is stipulated rather than derived from an emergent spacetime branch;
4. it supplies neither a Wheeler–DeWitt/Dirac constraint nor clock-change coherence; and
5. it has no GR proper-time recovery or discriminating quantum-gravity prediction.

Calling its stochastic parameter “emergent articulation time” would be relabelling, not derivation.

## T6 result

**No existing Point–Sphere component passes the physical-postulate novelty gate for the Problem of Time.**

The active framework currently contributes:

- a genuine philosophical commitment that created time must be real rather than illusory;
- the PITR evaluative requirement that viable time be internal, semiclassically recoverable, and publicly instantiated; and
- negative constraints established in T1–T5 concerning coordinate relabellings, interaction-relative records, symmetry, branch data, and articulation.

It currently does **not** contribute a new equation, boundary law, gauge-invariant time-odd observable, or probability measure that chooses a physical clock or temporal orientation. Therefore its existing physical projection is unchanged relative to whichever independently specified relational/semiclassical quantum-gravity model is used as baseline.

This is a **novelty failure**, not a falsification of the framework’s metaphysical hard core. It establishes the exact condition for any later physics claim: a new law must be supplied and tested rather than inferred from point/sphere terminology, public records, or the desire for a non-arbitrary world.

## T7 decision gate — no automatic invention

The programme may not fabricate a Point–Sphere dynamics to avoid this result. A successor can begin only after a candidate law is explicitly proposed in one of these forms:

### Route A — constraint/action modification
Specify \(\Delta S[g,\text{matter},\ldots]\) or a modified first-class constraint algebra; prove/recover diffeomorphism consistency and the GR limit.

### Route B — boundary-state/measure law
Specify a state space, boundary functional or measure \(d\mu[\Psi]\), and a reason it weights WKB orientations/branches; calculate a result that differs from standard boundary proposals.

### Route C — gauge-invariant time-odd observable
Specify an observable \(\mathcal O\) on the physical (constrained) state space with transformation properties, a nonzero source/dynamics, and an operational measurement protocol. It must not be merely \(\operatorname{sgn}p_a\) after a branch has been chosen.

### Route D — explicit probabilistic/selection law
Specify a probability rule on histories/records that reduces to the Born rule where tested, retains no-signalling, and differs quantitatively in an identified regime.

Until one route is supplied, T7 is **held**. This preserves the user’s requirement not to call a framework constraint or reinterpretation a derivation or solution without its formal result.
