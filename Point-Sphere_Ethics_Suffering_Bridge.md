# Ethics and Suffering Bridge v1
## Non-entailment, privation, source responsibility, and conditions for a serious theodicy

## Result in one sentence

Grounding, non-duality, plenitude, and a privation ontology do not by themselves entail that suffering is permissible, unreal, deserved, compensated, or justified; a benevolent-source claim needs further normative and counterfactual premises, while finite welfare and agency retain defeasible practical authority unless those premises are independently defended.

---

## 1. Three questions that must remain separate

Let \(M\) be the framework's descriptive/metaphysical claims, \(V\) its axiological claims, and \(D\) its deontic claims.

1. **Metaphysical dependence:** What grounds finite beings, events, capacities, and their actuality?
2. **Axiological status:** What is good or bad for a subject, and what kinds of value exist?
3. **Practical status:** What may an agent do, owe, permit, repair, or refuse?

The question whether reality is created, non-dual, simulated, derivative, or a manifestation is principally a question at level 1. It cannot settle levels 2–3 without bridge premises.

---

## 2. Practical non-entailment theorem

Let \(L_M\) be a non-normative language sufficient to express grounding, causal dependence, creation, non-duality, structural facts, and phenomenal occurrence. Let \(T_M\) be a consistent theory in \(L_M\). Extend the language with axiological/deontic predicates, including \(\operatorname{BadFor}(e,s)\), \(\operatorname{Wrong}(a)\), and \(\operatorname{Permitted}(a)\).

### Theorem 1 — No new practical verdict from metaphysics alone

If \(T_M\) contains no bridge axioms constraining the added normative predicates, then it entails no non-trivial claim of permission, obligation, or harmlessness.

#### Proof

Take a model \(M\models T_M\). Its non-normative reduct can be extended with differing interpretations of the normative predicates—for example, one in which a harmful act is wrong and another in which it is permitted—without changing any \(L_M\) fact. Both remain models of \(T_M\). Therefore the differing normative verdict is not entailed. \(\square\)

This result is not a proof that morality is autonomous, objective, or immune to metaphysical evidence. It says that anyone who derives a practical conclusion from metaphysics must state the normative bridge: a welfare principle, natural-law principle, divine-command premise, identity claim about good, or some alternative.

---

## 3. Why privation is not a theodicy

A privation view may define a defect as a lack of a good appropriate to a nature or capacity. This can illuminate why evil need not be a second independently created substance. It cannot by itself settle the status of felt agony, terror, loss, or wrongful violence.

### Proposition — Privation does not cancel badness

For an episode \(e\), \(\operatorname{Privation}(e)\) does not entail \(\neg\operatorname{BadFor}(e,s)\). Whether a lack is bad for a subject requires an axiological bridge concerning welfare, valence, flourishing, or reasons.

A blindness, betrayal, deprivation, or pain may be analysed as a failure, lack, or disorder and still be intensely bad for the one who undergoes it. The grammar “evil is a privation” never licenses “nothing bad happened.”

### Proposition — Non-creation of a defect does not settle responsibility

Even if a source does not create evil as an independently positive entity, it may create, sustain, choose, or permit the enabling conditions under which a privation occurs. Ontological dependence is not identical to moral responsibility, but neither does the former erase the need to assess the latter. Responsibility turns on agency, foresight, alternatives, authority, and reasons—further claims not supplied by privation language.

---

## 4. Benevolence requires a permission structure

Let \(E\) be an instance of severe suffering borne by subject \(s\). A claim that a morally perfect, purposive source permissibly allows \(E\) needs more than sourcehood or metaphysical goodness. At minimum, it owes a permission structure with the following elements:

1. **Moral-source premise:** the source has evaluative agency and non-arbitrary concern for finite welfare.
2. **Relevant good:** a specific good \(g\), not merely “the whole is good,” is at stake.
3. **Counterfactual necessity:** under the source's feasible alternatives and the relevant constraints, \(g\) requires \(E\), its risk, or a comparably serious cost.
4. **Proportionality:** \(g\) is weighty enough relative to the severity, duration, and distribution of the harm. Simple aggregation is insufficient where rights or non-instrumental constraints apply.
5. **Non-instrumental respect:** the sufferer is not merely a disposable means to benefits for others.
6. **Repair:** any compensation, healing, or restoration is a reason for repair, not a retroactive proof that the original harm was permissible.
7. **Epistemic discipline:** appeal to inaccessible goods cannot be unrestricted, or it defeats ordinary recognition of atrocity and blocks all moral inference.

These are necessary constraints on a serious theodical proposal, not jointly sufficient conditions. Some may be rejected only by openly revising the kind of benevolence asserted.

### Corollary — Necessary creation and plenitude intensify the burden

If every concrete history is necessary, or if every admissible mode is realised, then no suffering is avoided by non-actualisation. A benevolent-source proposal must explain why necessity or plenitude itself is good, why less harmful alternatives are unavailable or excluded by higher constraints, and how finite persons are not sacrificed to modal abundance. The explanatory burden increases; it does not disappear.

## 5. Compatibility, evidence, and explanation

Three verdicts are often confused.

- **Logical compatibility:** \(M\), a benevolent source \(B\), and suffering data \(S\) have at least one joint model.
- **Evidential support:** relative to stated auxiliary premises \(A\), compare \(\Pr(S\mid B,M,A)\) with \(\Pr(S\mid\neg B,M,A)\).
- **Explanatory adequacy:** a concrete permission structure meets the constraints in §4 without ad hoc rescue.

Compatibility is weak. Suffering refutes benevolence only if a benevolent-source theory gives it probability zero. But severe, apparently gratuitous suffering can be evidence against a particular strong benevolence model when that model predicts substantially less such suffering than its rivals. No numerical likelihood claim is warranted until the agency, freedom, regularity, soul-making, afterlife, and modal assumptions are specified.

The framework's current grounding or awareness claims do not entail the moral-source premise. Until they do, the framework must not cite them as an explanation of suffering.

---

## 6. Defeasible practical safeguards

The following are methodological constraints for the framework, not metaphysically exceptionless axioms.

- **CLND-N:** an ultimate-level description alone does not defeat ordinary welfare, consent, accountability, or anti-cruelty reasons.
- **CLND-E:** finite practical warrant begins with valence, vulnerability, agency, testimony, and reflection; ultimate speculation is not a blanket defeater.
- **No harm discount:** no claim of appearance, dependence, participation, future compensation, or cosmic unity may reduce the evidential seriousness of present pain without independent, person-respecting justification.
- **No local licence from global opacity:** unknown cosmic goods cannot authorise an ordinary agent to impose severe harm or disregard testimony.

These safeguards remain defeasible: an ethical theory may revise them, but only by exposing its normative premises and bearing their consequences, not by hiding the revision inside a metaphysical slogan.

---

## 7. What would advance or defeat this bridge

A successful ethical extension would need to publish:

- an explicit axiology of welfare, valence, dignity, and non-instrumental regard;
- a source-agency and moral-benevolence model distinct from bare grounding;
- counterfactual constraints specifying feasible alternatives and modal necessities;
- a treatment of animal suffering, natural evil, unequal distribution, and moral atrocity—not only voluntary human wrongdoing;
- stated limits on skeptical-theist appeals; and
- practical implications that remain recognisably protective of vulnerable subjects.

It fails if “privation,” “illusion,” “necessary expression,” or “the whole” is used to deny the reality of suffering without an axiological account; if unknown goods are invoked without limits; if compensation is treated as a permission to harm; or if the theory provides no difference between a benevolent and an indifferent source.

---

## 8. What has been achieved

This bridge establishes a conservative non-entailment result and a positive burden-of-explanation standard.

- It proves that metaphysical description alone does not yield permission or obligation.
- It distinguishes privation from absence of badness and sourcehood from benevolent agency.
- It gives a seven-part permission structure that any theodical extension must satisfy or openly revise.
- It preserves the possibility that metaphysical claims bear on ethics, while preventing them from silently overriding the welfare and testimony of finite persons.

It does not prove objective ethics, disprove theism or non-dualism, resolve the problem of evil, or establish any actual permission structure.
