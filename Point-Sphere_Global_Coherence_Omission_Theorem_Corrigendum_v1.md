# GC3 Corrigendum v1 — Omission Theorem and Remaining Aspect-Domain Debt

## Correction

`Point-Sphere_Global_Coherence_Maximal_Expression_Audit_v1_Report.md` correctly recorded the conditional identity theorem but incorrectly retained the **omission theorem** as an unproved bridge within the protected nondual framework.

That status is too weak.

The omission theorem is derivable **within** the framework once the protected identity is read non-representationally. It is not a premise-free theorem against every rival metaphysics, and it does not establish the independently typed genuine-aspect domain.

## 1. Exact premises

Let \(a\) range over genuine finite aspects, not merely formal descriptions or conceivable possibilities.

1. **Simplicity / non-representational self-knowledge.** There is no real knower–knowing–known gap in \(G\). For a genuine intrinsic content,
   \[
   \operatorname{ContentOfSelfKnowledge}_G(a)
   \Rightarrow
   R(a).
   \tag{I}
   \]
   This is the required formal reading of “to know is to be.” Simplicity alone, in a generic classical-theist or representational sense, would not supply \((I)\); here \((I)\) is part of the protected nondual identity package.

2. **Complete self-knowledge.** Every genuine determination of what \(G\) is is present to \(G\)’s self-knowledge:
   \[
   \operatorname{GenuineAspect}(a)
   \Rightarrow
   \operatorname{ContentOfSelfKnowledge}_G(a).
   \tag{E}
   \]

3. **Mode/aspect/content identity.** A genuine finite mode is neither an external created object nor a merely represented possibility:
   \[
   \operatorname{Mode}(a)
   \equiv
   \operatorname{Aspect}_G(a)
   \equiv
   \operatorname{ContentOfSelfKnowledge}_G(a).
   \tag{M}
   \]

These premises concern one reality described as simple ground and as finite aspect articulation; they do not introduce a second ontological level.

## 2. Omission theorem

### Theorem — P-internal maximal realization

\[
\forall a\,
\bigl(
\operatorname{GenuineAspect}(a)
\Rightarrow R(a)
\bigr).
\]

### Proof

Let \(a\) be an arbitrary genuine aspect. By \((E)\), \(a\) is a content of complete self-knowledge. By \((I)\), a genuine self-knowledge content is actual as self-presentation; hence \(R(a)\). Since \(a\) was arbitrary, every genuine aspect is realized. \(\square\)

The reductio form is equivalent. Assume a genuine aspect \(a\) is not realized. By \((I)\), it is not a self-knowledge content. By \((E)\), every genuine aspect is such a content. Contradiction.

## 3. What exactly does the work

The theorem does **not** follow from bare symmetry, no-preference, or the generic statement that a ground is simple.

Its load-bearing bridge is the non-representational identity \((I)\):

\[
\text{for }G,\quad
\text{self-knowing content}\neq\text{an unreal represented possibility}.
\]

Within the protected Point–Sphere identity package, this is not an additional selector or an arbitrary realization postulate. It is the meaning of mode = aspect = content of self-knowledge.

A classical creator countermodel blocks the theorem only by rejecting \((I)\): it retains a representational gap between divine knowledge of a possible item and the actuality of a distinct created item. That is a legitimate rival architecture, but not an internal countermodel to this theorem.

## 5. The remaining open task: \(\mathcal A_{\rm genuine}\)

The candidate direction is:

\[
\operatorname{GenuineAspect}(a)
\Longrightarrow
\operatorname{Coherent}(a)
\land
\operatorname{GroundCompatible}(a)
\land
\operatorname{NonGauge}(a)
\land
\operatorname{ContextAdmissible}(a).
\]

Coherence/non-contradiction is a necessary structural filter, but not yet sufficient: it does not by itself state what counts as a **determination of the non-structural ground’s nature**. The outstanding task is to give that phrase a formal, non-circular, target-relative aspect criterion without identifying the ground with a formal grammar.

The criterion must not be:

\[
\operatorname{GenuineAspect}(a)\iff R(a),
\]

nor:

\[
\operatorname{GenuineAspect}(a)\iff\text{any describable/formally coherent item}.
\]
