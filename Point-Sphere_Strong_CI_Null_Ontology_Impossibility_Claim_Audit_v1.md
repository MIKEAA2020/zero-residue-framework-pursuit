# Strong CI Adjudication — Null-Ontology Impossibility-Claim Audit v1
## Does strict grounding logic prove that no positive total account is possible?

## Status

**No formal impossibility theorem has been established.** The proposed trilemma yields a valid conditional incompatibility only after adding substantive assumptions that exclude terminal intrinsic explanation, non-well-founded closure, and non-grounding forms of explanation. It does not prove a “null ontology,” that reality is globally unintelligible, or that strongest CI is unsatisfiable.

## 1. The claimed formal setup

Let \(R\) be nonempty and let

\[
\mathcal E(x,y):
\quad
x\text{ strictly grounds/explains }y.
\]

The proposal assumes that \(\mathcal E\) is irreflexive, asymmetric, and transitive, then claims every total account faces an ungrounded terminus, infinite regress, or forbidden circle.

The setup already makes three non-logical choices:

1. it identifies complete explanation with one strict grounding relation;
2. it treats every ultimate terminus as brute unless grounded by a distinct item; and
3. it treats every non-terminating chain as globally unexplained.

Each is a substantive metaphysical premise, not a theorem of classical logic.

## 2. The genuine conditional theorem

Define:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf{InternalTotality}:&\quad
\forall y\in R\,\exists x\in R\,\mathcal E(x,y),\\
\mathsf{WF}_{\mathcal E}:&\quad
\text{every explanatory chain in \(R\) terminates in an \(\mathcal E\)-ungrounded item.}
\end{aligned}
\]

Then, for nonempty \(R\):

\[
\boxed{
R\ne\varnothing
\land
\mathsf{InternalTotality}
\land
\mathsf{WF}_{\mathcal E}
\Rightarrow
\bot.
}
\tag{STG-NoGo}
\]

This is a narrow structural point: a nonempty, well-founded strict intra-domain grounding order cannot also require that every member have a distinct intra-domain ground.

It does not yield the proposed global conclusion.

## 3. Why STG-NoGo does not establish a metaphysical impossibility theorem

### A. Strict grounding is not all explanation

\[
\mathsf{CompleteExplanation}
\not\equiv
\mathsf{DistinctStrictGrounding}.
\]

A candidate may propose an essence/existence account, a nonrepresentational identity, a global law, or intrinsic determination. Whether any succeeds is contested, but the trilemma cannot dismiss them by defining explanation as an irreflexive inter-item relation.

### B. Formal properties are not neutral axioms

Strict-order accounts of grounding are common, but transitivity, irreflexivity, asymmetry, and well-foundedness are themselves debated. Further, the question’s relation mixes formal logical explanation, metaphysical grounding, and causal/explanatory language.

\[
\mathsf{FormalProof}
\ne
\mathsf{Grounding}
\ne
\mathsf{MetaphysicalExplanation}.
\]

### C. An ungrounded terminus is not automatically brute

The argument needs:

\[
\forall g[
\neg\exists x\,\mathcal E(x,g)
\Rightarrow
\mathsf{Brute}(g)].
\tag{Terminal=Brute}
\]

This is exactly what a candidate self-intelligibility argument denies. It may be false, but it cannot be assumed in an argument intended to refute every self-intelligibility strategy.

### D. Infinite chains are not automatically globally unexplained

An infinite strict chain by itself may be explanatory failure, may have a global closure, or may be a candidate for principled infinitism. To infer failure requires a separate no-global-closure premise.

\[
\mathsf{InfiniteChain}(\mathcal E)
\not\Rightarrow
\mathsf{GloballyUnexplained}.
\]

No candidate presently has supplied such a global closure; that status does not convert its absence into a formal impossibility proof.

### E. Reality-as-a-whole is not automatically a member of \(R\)

The proof alternates between members of a domain and the total domain itself. Whether \(R\), its totality fact, or an ultimate ground is an item governed by the same relation must be separately typed. Otherwise the alleged “inside/outside” contradiction is a domain shift.

## 4. The positive claims in the proposed conclusion also fail

The following claims are not established by STG-NoGo:

\[
\text{only finite formal systems can be completely intelligible},
\]

\[
\text{reality as a whole is not a well-formed object of deduction},
\]

\[
\text{existence is not a predicate}
\Rightarrow
\text{no ultimate closure is possible},
\]

\[
\text{a selector/ground must be logically prior to the whole}.
\]

Each imports a substantive account of formal systems, ontology, existence, modality, or selection. None follows just from irreflexive strict grounding.

## 5. “Null ontology” is not residue-free

The proposed conclusion is itself a candidate profile:

\[
\mathsf{NO}:
\quad
\text{global reality admits no positive residue-free account; only local intelligibility is available.}
\]

It owes at least:

1. why local intelligibility is available but global intelligibility is impossible;
2. why the no-go assumptions apply to reality rather than only to one grounding formalism;
3. how this epistemic/metaphysical limitation is known without an illicit logic-to-ontology bridge; and
4. whether it accepts a brute global fact, revises SCI, or offers a positive global architecture under another name.

Thus \(\mathsf{NO}\) cannot be announced as “the correct account” without its own bridge dossier.

## 6. Exact result

\[
\boxed{
\mathsf{STG\text{-}NoGo}
\text{ is a conditional theorem about a particular strict-internal-grounding package.}
}
\]

But:

\[
\boxed{
\mathsf{STG\text{-}NoGo}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{no positive total account exists},
\quad
\mathsf{STG\text{-}NoGo}
\not\Rightarrow
\mathsf{SCI}\text{ is unsatisfiable},
\quad
\mathsf{STG\text{-}NoGo}
\not\Rightarrow
\mathsf{NO}\text{ is true}.
}
\]

The earlier strong-CI result remains: no public satisfier has yet been identified. That is not an impossibility theorem.

## 7. Source note

Contemporary grounding theory treats the strict-order and well-foundedness characterizations as widely discussed but contested; it distinguishes grounding from other relations of dependence and explanation. See https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/grounding/ and https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/dependence-ontological/.

## 8. Expiry condition

A genuine global impossibility theorem would need a profile-neutral argument that every possible complete explanation must instantiate the same strict relation and that every terminal, infinite, identity-based, whole-first, and nondual closure fails without simply defining it as forbidden. Absent that, the trilemma establishes only STG-NoGo.

## Nonclaims

This audit does not establish self-intelligibility, vindicate any positive candidate, endorse infinite regress, deny grounding, or reject a methodological least-residue revision. It blocks only the claim that the offered trilemma already proves a null ontology or the impossibility of all positive whole-reality accounts.
