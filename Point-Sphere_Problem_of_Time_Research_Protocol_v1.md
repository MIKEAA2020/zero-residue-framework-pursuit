# Problem of Time Research Protocol v1
## T1 — active physics-facing belt task

## Reset and scope

This protocol replaces the \(\lambda\phi^4_2\) tuning sequence as the active Q/F task. `F1` and the unexecuted `F2` extension are retained only as technical archive material; **F2 is not to be executed as a programme task.**

The target is the physical problem created by the tension between:

- ordinary quantum mechanics, whose Schrödinger formulation uses an external time parameter; and
- general relativity, in which proper time is local to worldlines, spacetime geometry is dynamical, and coordinate time has no preferred physical status.

The immediate technical focus is the canonical quantum-gravity problem of time—not a claim that all questions about the arrow of time, temporal phenomenology, or cosmology have thereby been solved.

## 1. Problem statement

For a constrained generally covariant system, physical states are commonly represented schematically by
\[
\widehat{\mathcal C}\,\lvert\Psi\rangle=0.
\]
There is then no external Schrödinger parameter for the closed system. A successful account must explain how effective temporal evolution, local proper-time physics, and ordinary quantum predictions are recovered without inserting a physically privileged background clock.

The relevant facets include at least:

1. frozen formalism/relational dynamics;
2. clock ambiguity or multiple choice—inequivalent internal-time choices can yield inequivalent quantum descriptions;
3. global-clock obstructions;
4. constraint closure, observables, and foliation dependence; and
5. recovery of local relativistic time and empirical records.

The arrow/low-entropy boundary is related but is a separate T-layer task; it must not be declared solved by a relational-clock construction.

## 2. Framework contribution stated as a testable constraint

The hard core assumes an atemporal ground and real created order. C1/C2 add that created temporal order must permit real finite causal roles, partial perspectives, and a public/error-correctable world.

Translated into a physics-facing constraint, this gives the **Public Internal-Time Requirement (PITR)**:

> Fundamental dynamics may be globally constrained and lack external time, but any emergent physical time must be internal to the physical system, covariantly recoverable in an appropriate semiclassical regime, and instantiated by records that are robustly shareable among finite subsystems.

PITR does **not** choose a clock merely because the framework says time is created. It yields a possible selection rule only if the following criteria actually distinguish candidate clock variables.

## 3. Candidate-clock criteria

Given a constrained system and candidate internal clock \(T\), define a candidate as physically admissible only if it satisfies all applicable conditions:

| Code | Requirement | Failure it is meant to exclude |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | **Constraint-internal:** \(T\) is a degree of freedom/relational observable of the constrained system; no external parameter is inserted. | Background-time substitution |
| P2 | **Branchwise monotonicity:** on a specified semiclassical history branch, \(T\) is monotonic over the relevant domain. | A clock that repeatedly reverses or fails globally |
| P3 | **Conditional dynamics:** conditioning on \(T=\tau\) yields an approximately unitary effective dynamics for the remaining degrees of freedom in the regime of use. | Merely relabelling a static state |
| P4 | **Low backreaction:** using/reading the clock does not invalidate the approximation that supports P3. | Ideal-clock fiction with uncontrolled clock-system coupling |
| P5 | **Public-record robustness:** correlations encoding \(T\) are redundantly accessible to distinct finite subsystems, with stable records adequate for error correction. | A private/formal clock with no physical temporal role |
| P6 | **Clock-change coherence:** overlapping admissible clocks agree on observable predictions to the required approximation, or a principled inequivalence is derived. | Unresolved multiple-choice ambiguity |
| P7 | **GR recovery:** in the semiclassical gravitational regime, the construction recovers local proper-time/time-dilation relations rather than a universal absolute time. | Conflict with general covariance/equivalence principle |

P5 is the distinctive programme hypothesis. Existing relational proposals use correlations and physical clocks; PITR adds the requirement that a candidate time be an objective public-record structure, not simply an allowed factorisation or formal parameter. This is a hypothesis, not an established result.

A possible quantitative research route is to define, in a specified model,
\[
\mathfrak T(T)=\mathfrak M(T)+\mathfrak U(T)-\lambda\mathfrak B(T)+\eta\mathfrak R(T),
\]
where \(\mathfrak M\) measures branchwise monotonicity, \(\mathfrak U\) conditional-unitary accuracy, \(\mathfrak B\) clock backreaction, and \(\mathfrak R\) redundant public-record stability. No use of this functional is licensed until all terms, the state, and parameters are independently defined; it must not be tuned to select a desired clock.

## 4. Comparison families—not premature pass/fail verdicts

| Family | Potential contribution | Open T1 question under PITR |
|---|---|---|
| Dirac/relational observables and reduced phase-space approaches | Define evolution through correlations/internal variables | Does the construction satisfy P6 across admissible clocks and P7 in gravity? |
| Page–Wootters / quantum-reference-frame approaches | Recover conditional dynamics from a globally constrained or stationary state | Does a physical clock-world split meet P4–P6 rather than remain factorisation-dependent? |
| Semiclassical/WKB quantum cosmology | Obtain an approximate Schrödinger time from a semiclassical gravitational background | Can its background/branch selection be internally justified and locally covariant? |
| Thermal-time proposals | Associate a flow with state/modular structure | What selects the state and connects modular flow to P5/P7? |
| Causal-set and causal-order approaches | Make causal order fundamental without an external time coordinate | Can proper durations, quantum dynamics, and public records be recovered? |
| Covariant/path-integral approaches | Avoid a preferred foliation in the formalism | How are physical observables and effective temporal records recovered? |

No family is marked as “passing” merely because it calls time emergent or relational. Conversely, a standard framework is not rejected simply because it uses a time parameter in an approximation. The question is whether it derives the regime in which that parameter is valid.

## 5. First executable research target: T1a → T1b

### T1a — comparison ledger

Construct a sourced technical ledger for the six families above. For each, specify:

- exact constrained/dynamical formalism;
- where its clock, state, foliation, and factorisation enter;
- status against P1–P7: established, conditional, open, or failed in the stated formulation;
- known toy-model successes and known no-go/ambiguity results; and
- one mathematical bottleneck rather than a metaphysical label.

**T1a success:** a reviewable ledger that corrects the common error of collapsing the multi-faceted problem into “the Wheeler–DeWitt equation has no time.”

### T1b — minimal discriminating model

Use a parametrised, constrained minisuperspace model (for example, FRW geometry plus a free scalar and inhomogeneous/record degrees of freedom) to compare at least two candidate clocks. Define all of P1–P7 sufficiently to calculate:

1. conditional evolution;
2. monotonic domain(s);
3. clock backreaction;
4. record redundancy/robustness; and
5. whether the candidate rankings are invariant under a permitted change of variables.

**T1b success:** PITR distinguishes clock candidates or proves an invariance/equivalence theorem.

**T1b failure:** it cannot be made non-arbitrary, changes under an equivalent description, or fails to recover a known relational/proper-time limit. That failure means the framework has added no usable clock-selection content.

## 6. Strict nonclaims

This programme does not yet derive the Wheeler–DeWitt equation, select a unique cosmological clock, explain temporal phenomenology, derive the thermodynamic arrow, resolve all quantum-gravity constraints, or predict a new measurable deviation from existing quantum gravity. It first tries to turn the framework’s “time is real, internal, and publicly instantiated” commitment into a discriminating mathematical criterion.
