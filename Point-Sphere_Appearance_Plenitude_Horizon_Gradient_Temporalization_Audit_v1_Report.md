# AP3P2M Horizon-Gradient Temporalization and Boundary Audit v1 — Report
## Horizon and clarity can be structural without time; therefore they do not by themselves derive flow

## Evaluation

The proposed horizon route is valuable because it separates finite perspectival structure from overt temporal vocabulary. Its first two steps are coherent:

\[
\text{finite context}
\Rightarrow
\text{nonempty horizon},
\]
\[
\text{horizon/context}
\Rightarrow
\text{possible focal–marginal–horizonal clarity rank}.
\]

A clarity gradient can be a purely structural relation. It need not contain a clock, an external time, or a successor parameter.

But that fact blocks the intended inference at the next step:
\[
\text{structural gradient}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{proceeding through gradient}.
\]

A static field, partial order, salience ranking, or focal/horizonal organization can have nonuniform grades without an act traversing them. To say that an act “lives through” or “proceeds through” the gradient introduces a directed relation. Unless independently defined, that is the successor relation in phenomenological wording.

## Exact finite countermodel

Take:
\[
S=\{a,b,c\},
\qquad C_0=\{a,b\},
\qquad C_1=\{a,c\}.
\]

Both contexts are finite and proper, overlap at \(a\), and have nonempty horizons:
\[
H(C_0)=\{c\},
\qquad H(C_1)=\{b\}.
\]

Give each a non-temporal focal/marginal/horizonal rank:
\[
\kappa_{C_0}(a,b,c)=(2,1,0),
\qquad
\kappa_{C_1}(a,c,b)=(2,1,0).
\]

This same static horizon-gradient structure permits all of the following:

| Successor choice | Resulting connected components |
|---|---|
| No successor | \(\{C_0\},\{C_1\}\) |
| \(C_0\rightsquigarrow C_1\) | \(\{C_0,C_1\}\) |
| \(C_1\rightsquigarrow C_0\) | \(\{C_0,C_1\}\) |
| Two-phase cycle | \(\{C_0,C_1\}\) |

It therefore fixes neither:

- whether there is a flow;
- its direction;
- terminality/nonterminality;
- acyclicity;
- a \(\mathbb Z\)-order; or
- the component partition relevant to mode-flow.

All executable checks pass.

## Relation to AP3M

AP3M already establishes that an immanent finite horizon is a correct typing condition:
\[
H(C)=S\setminus C\neq\varnothing.
\]

It also established that such horizon structure does not select among the five surviving grammar families. A clarity gradient enriches this picture but, unless it contains an independently warranted directed/integrative law, remains another static structural feature.

## Does horizon individuate a mode?

There is a limited affirmative result. For a fixed global vocabulary \(S\):
\[
H(C)=H(D)
\Longrightarrow C=D.
\]
So a horizon can extensionally describe an already supplied context.

But this does not make horizon prior to context individuation. Defining \(H(C)=S\setminus C\) already assumes \(C\), and a flow component contains multiple contexts with different horizons. The same horizon data can be grouped into different components by different successor relations.

Thus horizon difference can characterize differences between already given contexts. It does not yet determine which sequence of contexts is one act, one component, or one mode.

## Consequences for the proposed FT and ATG routes

### FT

The route can derive a candidate **nonuniform finite perspective structure**. It does not derive temporalization unless it adds:
\[
\mathsf{GradientProceeding}:
\quad
\text{a self-presentation act necessarily instantiates a directed traversal/integration of its clarity gradient.}
\]

This is a substantive new bridge. It must be defended independently of AP3P1 successor flow and remain acceptable if it excludes IAR.

### ATG

The proposed identification of an act’s proceeding with a successor relation becomes valid only after `GradientProceeding` or another directed act-integration relation is warranted. Component closure then needs a further theorem identifying maximal integrated horizon-units with modes. It cannot be read directly from \(H(C)\).

## Conclusion

The horizon route makes useful progress by isolating an atemporal structural layer of finitude. But it does not fill AP3O’s gap. The critical inference:
\[
\text{gradient of clarity}
\Rightarrow
\text{temporal proceeding}
\]
is invalid without an independently supplied relation of directed act-integration. The horizon is a descriptor of given contexts, not yet a generator of successor flow or mode boundaries.
