# Preregistration Protocol v2
## CFD selector: independent restricted-phenomenal calibration

**Protocol status:** This is a preregistration and contains no validation result. It supersedes the v1 calibration draft before any corpus has been examined, recruited, coded, or scored. It is timestamped **2026-10-21** in the workspace.

## 0. Question, scope, and nonclaims

The restricted question is whether the G3 complete-mode classifier tracks a predeclared, independently coded phenomenal target better than its matched rivals:

\[
\mathsf{Core}^{\rm cmp}(m)
\Longleftrightarrow
\mathsf{Complete}(m)
\land
\mathsf{Supported}(m)
\land
\mathsf{Unified}(m).
\]

The target is not truth about whole reality. A successful calibration would not establish:

\[
P_{\rm ND},\quad
\mathsf{CFD}^{\rm cmp,\Phi}_{G}\text{ as a metaphysical truth},\quad
\mathsf{Plenitude},\quad
\mathsf{Adeq}_{P},\quad
\text{or framework uniqueness}.
\]

It would test only the phenomenological adequacy claim that the frozen G3 profile criterion tracks a specified local-experience classification.

## 1. Frozen candidate and formal objects

### 1.1 Candidate classifier

The candidate is fixed as:

\[
\mathsf{Core}^{\rm cmp}_{A}(m)
\Longleftrightarrow
\mathsf{Complete}_{A}(m)
\land
\mathsf{Supported}^{\rm cmp}_{\Phi}(m)
\land
\mathsf{Unified}^{\rm cmp}_{\Phi}(m).
\tag{CFD-test}
\]

A complete diagram uses the frozen finite active frame:

\[
A=\langle S_A,E_A,Q_A\rangle
\]

and decides every active \(\mathsf{HasAt}\), equality/disequality, and \(\mathsf{Before}_{\Phi}\) atom under the frozen theory \(T_A\).

The profile scored by the classifier is:

\[
\Pi(m)=\langle\bar S,\bar E,Q,H,B\rangle.
\]

No post-registration change to the atom basis, support rule, connectivity rule, profile extractor, or active-frame completion rule is allowed in the confirmatory analysis.

### 1.2 Formal matched rivals

The two operational formal rivals are frozen as:

\[
R_{\rm syn}(m)
\Longleftrightarrow
\mathsf{Gen}_{\mathcal G_3}(m),
\]

and:

\[
R_{\rm atom}(c)
\Longleftrightarrow
\mathsf{FormalCtx}(c)
\land
\forall j\,\mathsf{Core}^{\rm cmp}(m_j).
\]

The atomistic rival omits the global context-unity condition.

## 2. Corpus source and selection

### 2.1 Admissible source

The primary corpus must consist of **prospectively elicited first-person local-experience reports**, collected after this timestamp from participants who are not told about CFD, nondual appearance, plenitude, graph connectedness, or the candidate/rival predictions.

Third-person observations may be retained as metadata but cannot supply phenomenal content or certification. Framework-generated examples, the attached transcript, and cases supplied by a person already familiar with the framework are excluded from the confirmatory corpus.

Archival reports may be used only in an exploratory appendix, unless they were independently collected before this protocol using the same neutral prompts and contain the required completion information.

### 2.2 Sampling rule

The intended sampling frame is a predeclared participant pool maintained independently of this framework project. Once a pool is selected, reports are enrolled in chronological order of eligibility until each stratum reaches quota; there may be no replacement based on apparent fit with any classifier.

No report may be selected because it appears likely to confirm or challenge CFD.

### 2.3 Neutral prompts

Before any coding, each participant receives only these neutral prompts:

1. “Describe one brief, immediate experience in ordinary words.”
2. “What qualities, if any, were present?”
3. “Did anything change or occur in an order? Describe the order if so.”
4. “Did this seem like one local experience, several separate experiences, or are you unsure?”
5. “What in your report is uncertain or omitted?”

The prompts must not mention aspects, grounds, modes, nonduality, participation, support, nodes, edges, graphs, or selector predictions.

### 2.4 Inclusion and exclusion

Include a report only if all conditions hold:

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
I1 &: \text{it is a first-person local-experience report using the neutral prompt set};\\
I2 &: \text{it contains enough information for a unique complete coding under the frozen manual};\\
I3 &: \text{it contains no framework-specific terminology supplied by the study materials};\\
I4 &: \text{it is independently classified and independently diagram-coded under §4}.
\end{array}
\]

Exclude from confirmatory scoring:

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
E1 &: \text{transcript examples, philosophical thought experiments, or framework-generated cases};\\
E2 &: \text{reports that cannot receive a unique complete diagram without adding unreported content};\\
E3 &: \text{reports with unavailable independent classification or coding};\\
E4 &: \text{duplicate reports of the same predeclared episode};\\
E5 &: \text{reports disclosed after coding to have been composed with knowledge of CFD predictions.}
\end{array}
\]

Exclusions must be logged with the applicable criterion before any classifier verdict is viewed.

### 2.5 Target sample

The target is:

\[
N=80\text{ independently eligible reports},
\]

stratified into 20 reports each by the phenomenal classifier's frozen raw categories:

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\mathcal S_1 &: \text{single sustained qualitative presentation};\\
\mathcal S_2 &: \text{reported qualitative succession};\\
\mathcal S_3 &: \text{reported multi-item or multi-quality one-episode presentation};\\
\mathcal S_4 &: \text{reported list, separate episodes, or no qualitative presentation}.
\end{array}
\]

These categories are assigned before diagram coding and do not use the terms support, connectivity, Core, aspect, or mode.

The purpose of \(N=80\) is calibration coverage rather than population estimation: it reserves 20 predeclared opportunities per category and requires at least 16 valid scored items per category after exclusions. If any stratum has fewer than 16 valid items, its stratum result and the full confirmatory verdict are inconclusive.

## 3. Coding manual and completeness

### 3.1 Diagram coding

A diagram coder converts raw reports to \(\langle S_A,E_A,Q_A\rangle\) using a versioned manual that fixes:

- subject-slot introduction;
- phase-slot introduction;
- rigid quality vocabulary;
- equality/disequality conventions;
- attribution and order conventions;
- the finite active atom base; and
- the criteria for a unique complete coding.

The coder may encode only content supported by the raw report and fixed coding conventions.

### 3.2 Completion and out-of-scope rule

Unreported material may not be inserted to force connectedness or disconnectedness.

If the report fails to determine a unique completion, it is not coded as a negative field. It is marked:

\[
\mathsf{OutOfScope}_{\rm incomplete}.
\]

This protects the complete-mode semantics from relapsing into a partial-diagram test.

### 3.3 Alternative grammars

An alternative/reified encoding is admissible only if a profile extractor is frozen before coding:

\[
\eta_L:
\mathsf{CompleteModes}(L)
\to
\langle\bar S,\bar E,Q,H,B\rangle.
\]

It must be profile-conservative under the G3 representation-invariance condition. Otherwise its result is exploratory only.

## 4. Independence and blinding

### 4.1 Separated roles

Each item must have distinct people in these roles:

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
R_1 &: \text{phenomenal classifier};\\
R_2 &: \text{diagram coder};\\
R_3 &: \text{verdict/statistical analyst}.
\end{array}
\]

No person may occupy two roles for the same item.

### 4.2 Blindness

- \(R_1\) is blind to all diagrams, Core results, and rival predictions.
- \(R_2\) is blind to the phenomenal classification, Core results, and rival predictions.
- \(R_3\) receives frozen classification and coding tables only after both are locked.
- Participants are blind to the framework and the classifier's predicted verdict.

If any role separation or blinding fails, the item is retained only in an exploratory appendix.

### 4.3 Reliability

For a randomly selected 25% duplicate subsample, two independent phenomenal classifiers and two independent diagram coders code the same materials under the same blindness rules.

Reliability criteria are:

\[
\kappa_{\rm phen}\ge .80,
\qquad
\kappa_{\rm diagram}\ge .80.
\]

The diagram coefficient is computed over predeclared finite coding decisions, not over the downstream Core verdict. If either criterion fails, the confirmatory result is inconclusive regardless of apparent fit.

Disagreements are not resolved by consensus for confirmatory scoring. A third blinded coder supplies a tie-break coding only for a separately labelled sensitivity analysis.

## 5. Independent phenomenal certification

The phenomenal classifier records these answers without using framework vocabulary:

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
K1 &: \text{Was there a reported qualitative presentation?}\\
K2 &: \text{Was it reported as one local presentation rather than a mere list?}\\
K3 &: \text{If distinguishable moments/items occurred, were they reported as jointly present in that episode?}\\
K4 &: \text{Was the report too indeterminate to answer K1--K3?}
\end{array}
\]

Define:

\[
\mathsf{CertField}_{\alpha}(d)=+
\Longleftrightarrow
K1=\mathrm{yes}\land K2=\mathrm{yes}
\land K3\in\{\mathrm{yes},\mathrm{n/a}\}
\land K4=\mathrm{no}.
\]

Define:

\[
\mathsf{CertField}_{\alpha}(d)=-
\Longleftrightarrow
K1=\mathrm{no}
\lor
(K2=\mathrm{no}\land K4=\mathrm{no}).
\]

All other reports are indeterminate and are not counted as evidence for either classifier.

This is an independent calibration label, not a definition of metaphysical aspecthood and not a proof of \(P_{\rm ND}\).

## 6. Broader metaphysical rivals

The protocol includes three required architecture-level rivals:

\[
R_{\rm part}:\text{classical participation},
\qquad
R_{\rm whole}:\text{whole-first articulation},
\qquad
R_{\rm null}:\text{null/deflationary account}.
\]

Names alone do not operationalize a rival. Before the corpus lock, each rival must submit or be assigned a written, independently justified prediction function:

\[
V_R(d)\in
\{+, -, \bot\},
\]

where \(+\) predicts the independently certified field label, \(-\) predicts non-field, and \(\bot\) declares no prediction at this restricted phenomenal interface.

The function may not invoke CFD, profile connectivity, or the outcome labels after they are known.

If a rival has only \(\bot\) predictions, it is retained as a broader metaphysical rival but cannot be counted as a discriminated empirical competitor in this protocol. This prevents a strawman operationalization of participation, whole-first, or deflationary frameworks.

## 7. Mismatch, comparative, and decision criteria

For each valid in-scope item, compute after all locks:

\[
\mathsf{Core}^{\rm cmp}(m),
\quad
R_{\rm syn}(m),
\quad
R_{\rm atom}(c)\text{ where applicable},
\quad
V_R(d)\text{ where supplied}.
\]

A CFD mismatch is:

\[
\mathsf{Mismatch}_{\rm CFD}(d)
\Longleftrightarrow
[
\mathsf{CertField}_{\alpha}(d)=+
\land
\neg\mathsf{Core}^{\rm cmp}(m)
]
\lor
[
\mathsf{CertField}_{\alpha}(d)=-
\land
\mathsf{Core}^{\rm cmp}(m)
].
\]

Indeterminate or incomplete reports are excluded, never counted as half-mismatches.

### 7.1 Success

The restricted phenomenological adequacy claim earns a provisional pass only if all conditions hold:

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
S1 &: \text{each stratum has at least 16 valid items};\\
S2 &: \kappa_{\rm phen}\ge .80\text{ and }\kappa_{\rm diagram}\ge .80;\\
S3 &: \text{overall CFD match rate}\ge 85\%;\\
S4 &: \text{no stratum has CFD mismatch rate}>20\%;\\
S5 &: \text{CFD is not outperformed by every operational matched rival on the same valid items};\\
S6 &: \text{at least one predeclared disagreement item exists for }R_{\rm syn}\text{ or }R_{\rm atom}.
\end{array}
\]

### 7.2 Failure

The restricted adequacy claim fails if either holds:

\[
F1:\quad
\text{overall mismatch rate}>20\%,
\]

or:

\[
F2:\quad
\text{a predeclared stratum has mismatch rate}\ge 30\%
\text{ with at least five valid items.}
\]

Failure withdraws the claim that this classifier tracks the predeclared phenomenal target. It does not deductively falsify a metaphysical postulate that could reject the calibration target.

### 7.3 Inconclusive

Every result not meeting Success or Failure is inconclusive, including inadequate recruitment, low reliability, absence of a rival-disagreement item, unresolved rival operationalization, or match rates between the stated thresholds.

## 8. Expiry and archival rule

The protocol is archived in the workspace on **2026-10-21**, before any corpus is examined. The confirmatory public-selector claim expires on:

\[
d_\star=\text{2026-12-31}.
\]

It expires if, by \(d_\star\):

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
X1 &: \text{no independently collected corpus satisfies the eligibility and quota rules};\\
X2 &: \text{no valid disagreement item for }R_{\rm syn}\text{ or }R_{\rm atom}\text{ exists};\\
X3 &: \text{the broader-rival prediction functions are not fixed before corpus lock};\\
X4 &: \text{reliability coding cannot meet the preregistered requirements.}
\end{array}
\]

Expiry means only that CFD may not be presented as a publicly calibrated selector. It does not establish \(\neg\mathsf{CFD}\), \(\neg P_{\rm ND}\), or the truth of a rival.

## 9. Final interpretation boundary

A protocol pass would support only:

\[
\text{restricted calibration of one finite phenomenal profile classifier.}
\]

It would remain insufficient for:

\[
\mathsf{PubDet}_{G}\text{ globally},
\quad
\mathsf{Adeq}_{P},
\quad
\text{unrestricted plenitude},
\quad
\text{or a unique correct-framework conclusion}.
\]

## Nonclaims

This preregistration does not claim a neutral participant pool presently exists, that any cases have been reviewed, that a predeclared corpus will be collected by the expiry date, that first-person reports are infallible, or that a successful calibration settles metaphysics.
