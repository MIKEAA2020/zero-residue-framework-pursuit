# The Point–Sphere Framework
## A consolidated metaphysical framework and scientific research programme

### Working name
**Asymmetrical Non-Duality with Participatory Plenitude**

This is a programme for relating a demanding metaphysics of ultimate explanation to consciousness, physics, quantum theory, and empirical inquiry. It is neither a completed proof of classical theism nor an established physical theory. Its strongest form is a **structured research programme**: it states what an ultimate account must be like, proposes an ontology capable of meeting those conditions, interprets existing science through that ontology, and specifies the formal and empirical work required for the proposal to earn more than philosophical status.

Its governing ambition is bidirectional. From below, it takes the world disclosed by physics, mathematics, consciousness, and ordinary life seriously. From above, it asks what sort of ultimate reality could make the whole intelligible without treating the physical order, consciousness, or its own existence as an unexplained residue. A successful framework must let the two directions constrain one another.

---

## 1. Standards of adequacy

The programme begins from five methodological standards.

1. **Ontological rather than temporal explanation.** The relevant question is not what happened first in time. Time is itself part of the reality to be explained. The question is what is ontologically non-derivative: what does not borrow its intelligibility or actuality from a more basic condition.

2. **No merely verbal stopping point.** “It just is” can mark the end of a question, but it does not by itself state a positive account of why that stopping point is fit to be ultimate. An ultimate account should be self-contained rather than merely unexplained.

3. **No unexplained emergence of phenomenality.** A theory that describes only relations, dispositions, functions, and information-processing has not thereby explained why anything is felt from within. It may explain reports, access, and behaviour; it still owes an account of phenomenal presence.

4. **Non-competition between metaphysics and science.** Metaphysics cannot veto successful measurement, quantum field theory, relativity, evolutionary biology, neuroscience, or ordinary causal practice. Its role is to articulate their place, scope, and ontological interpretation—not to replace their methods with declaration.

5. **Public discipline.** The framework must eventually be expressible as definitions, axioms, mathematical structures, recovery results, and discriminating predictions. Elegance, symmetry, and felt intelligibility are discovery-guides, not substitutes for derivation.

These standards distinguish a complete explanation from a mere refusal to continue explaining, and a research programme from a vocabulary of suggestive metaphors.

## 2. Controlled vocabulary

Several terms must be kept stable. Much apparent disagreement comes from allowing one word to shift between different jobs.

| Term | Controlled meaning |
|---|---|
| **Ground / Absolute** | The unique non-derivative reality: not one object within a larger inventory, but that on which every derivative reality depends. |
| **Aseity / self-subsistence** | Dependence on nothing external or prior in ontological order. It does **not** mean self-causation, nor merely being uncaused. |
| **Topological or ontological cause** | A relation of grounding, constitution, or dependence; not an earlier event in time. |
| **Simple** | Not assembled from ontologically independent parts, principles, or conditions. Simplicity is not mere smallness or geometric point-likeness. |
| **Complete** | Lacking no condition needed to be what it is. Completeness does not automatically imply that no derivative effects can exist; it rules out an external complement needed to complete the ground. |
| **Infinite / unlimited** | Not bounded by an external determiner or constitutive lack. It is not merely infinitely large in one spatial coordinate. |
| **Created / derivative reality** | Reality with genuine but non-independent being: it is sustained by the ground and not a second ultimate. |
| **Perspective** | A locally bounded mode of access, information, or manifestation. It need not be a separate substance or a second ultimate subject. |
| **Structure** | Relations, symmetries, dispositions, quantities, dynamics, and formal organisation—the domain that physics and mathematics can represent. |
| **Experience** | Intrinsic phenomenal presence: what it is like, if anything, for a concrete mode to be that mode. |
| **Polarity** | A generative contrast or dual aspect within derivative reality: e.g. unity/multiplicity, timeless/temporal, simple/complex, or positive/negative. It is not automatically a pair of independently existing substances. |
| **Grade** | A level or mode of derivative organisation defined by a precise structural relation once the model is formalised. “Higher” must not remain a purely poetic label. |
| **Plenitude** | The hypothesis that every admissible, internally coherent mode of derivative reality is realised within the total landscape, subject to a still-to-be-defined admissibility criterion and measure. |
| **Fundamental** | Context-sensitive. A prime number can be fundamental within arithmetic; a particle can be fundamental within a physical model; neither fact makes it the ontological ground of all reality. |

Two distinctions are especially important.

- A **brute fact** is ungrounded in the weak sense that no further explanation is supplied. A **self-subsistent ground** is non-derivative in the stronger sense that its lack of external dependence follows from its intelligible nature. The two must not be conflated.
- “Only the Absolute exists” can mean either (a) there is only one **independent** reality, or (b) literally nothing derivative is real. The framework requires (a). If it adopts (b), then creation, physics, and finite subjects must be treated as merely illusory, which conflicts with the programme’s commitment to genuine derivative reality.

## 3. The core grounding argument

### 3.1 The strongest conditional argument

The framework can be stated as a conditional argument.

1. **Derivative realities are not ultimate merely by being temporally eternal.** An eternal physical order may still depend on laws, symmetries, a field structure, modal facts, or a more basic condition. Eternity in time is not aseity.
2. **A complete ultimate explanation cannot consist only of borrowed or mutually deferred explanation.** A chain of derivative items can account for how each member depends on another without yet explaining why the total dependence-structure obtains.
3. **A genuinely ultimate ground cannot be externally conditioned, internally assembled from independent constituents, or limited by a condition that it does not itself contain.** Otherwise its supposed ultimacy would be displaced to those conditions or constituents.
4. **The physical description of the world presents organised structure—fields, laws, parameters, symmetries, relations, dynamics—not an evident proof that this entire structure exists by necessity.** Physics describes what a physical world is like conditional on its laws and constants; it does not currently derive that there must be precisely such a world.
5. **Mathematical necessity, even if real, is not obviously concrete actuality.** Mathematics supplies constraints, identities, and formal possibility. It does not by itself explain why a concrete, experiential, law-governed order is actual.

**Conditional conclusion:** If one requires that reality admit a complete non-arbitrary ontological explanation, then it must terminate in a non-derivative ground sufficient for the actuality of derivative reality. If one further accepts that any composite requires a unifying condition not reducible to independent constituents, then the ground must be simple. If physical reality belongs to the dependent totality rather than constituting a demonstrated necessary base, then the ground is non-physical in the relevant ontological sense.

This is an argument for an **aseitic ground**, with conditional routes to simplicity and non-physicality. It is not yet an argument for every feature of a personal God, for a particular religion, or for a fully specified cosmology.

### 3.2 Brute fact and regress: what survives

The critique of brute fact is methodological and metaphysical, not a formal contradiction.

When the answer to “Why does this exist?” is “It just is,” the answer identifies the object at issue; it does not yet show that the object is self-explanatory. Saying “this is the stopping point” is different from showing that its nature makes external explanation inapplicable. A brute-fact view may therefore be **weakly self-contained**—it stops the search—but not **strongly self-intelligible**.

Likewise, an infinite regress can explain local dependence indefinitely. Treating the regress as a whole as ultimate is a coherent option, but it does not automatically establish that the whole is simple, necessary, or self-subsistent. Its explanatory force comes from the unboundedness of the sequence; a further argument is required to show that unboundedness yields aseity.

Accordingly:

- Brute fact and infinite regress are not refuted merely by naming them.
- They fail the programme’s demand for complete intelligibility unless they can show why their stopping condition or total structure is necessary.
- If they can do so, they have ceased to be mere brute fact or mere regress and have become candidates for an ultimate ground.

This is the real sense in which alternative stopping points are “ground in disguise”: not that logic proves them identical to a simple ground, but that any adequate version must acquire the positive features—non-derivativeness, internal intelligibility, and sufficiency—that the ground proposal makes explicit.

### 3.3 Terminus taxonomy and rational stability

At the highest level, an ontological explanation either does not terminate, or it terminates.

- A **non-terminating family** includes infinite regress and circular dependence. These may account for local relations while leaving open whether the total structure is self-sufficient.
- A **terminating family** ends in a base reality. That base is either treated as primitive and unexplained (**brute termination**) or as non-derivative in virtue of its nature (**necessary or self-explanatory termination**).

This taxonomy is usefully exhaustive at the level of *termination form*. It does not settle the specification of a terminus. A necessary physical base, a necessary mathematical/structural base, a plurality of necessary bases, pure awareness, and strict non-duality are genuine competing specifications. They belong to the same termination family only in the thin sense that each claims some non-derivative basis. They cannot be identified merely by being placed in that family.

The strongest criticism of brute termination is a criticism of **rational stability**, not a formal reductio. It says that a primitive stopping point cannot say why this feature rather than another is fundamental, why the fundamental has the character it does, or why consciousness, order, law, and normativity arise within it. At best it marks where explanation ends. The simple-ground thesis offers a more ambitious terminus: a nature whose non-derivativeness, unity, and explanatory scope are supposed to be internally connected.

Several stronger claims do **not** follow.

- “This fact needs no further explanation” does not make the fact its own explanation. It can express a refusal of a strong Principle of Sufficient Reason rather than an assertion of self-existence.
- An epistemic policy against unconstrained metaphysical inference does not lose all authority merely because the policy itself is not ontologically grounded. Epistemic justification, ontological grounding, and normative authority are different questions.
- The mere availability of a primitive stopping point does not prove it irrational; it identifies an explanatory cost. The comparative force of that cost depends on whether a rival genuinely explains more without importing equal or greater unexplained commitments.

The “switching” concern survives in a more modest form. If there is no criterion for why one fact is primitive rather than another, brute termination is explanatorily arbitrary. A brute-fact theorist may answer that fundamentality itself is the criterion, or that the total base is primitive. The framework must show why that answer is inadequate; it cannot simply redescribe the base as self-explaining.

### 3.4 Limits and comparative status

The argument is not premise-free. The principle that every derivative fact must have a sufficient ontological ground is a substantive rational commitment. A defender of brute fact can reject it without formal inconsistency. A defender of necessary physical reality can insist that physical structure is necessary, although no known physical theory currently demonstrates this. A defender of an infinite totality can regard the whole as primitive.

Nor does the word **ultimate** settle the matter by definition. It can mean “most fundamental or explanatory terminus”; it does not, without further argument, mean “morally perfect,” “devoid of every defect,” or “self-explaining in the strongest Thomistic sense.” The observed incompleteness, change, and suffering of the physical universe make it a poor candidate for a *perfect* ultimate. They do not by themselves prove that it cannot be a primitive ultimate base. To infer a further ground requires the framework’s grounding and perfection premises.

The strongest honest result is therefore:

> The aseitic-ground thesis is the best candidate **given the programme’s norm of complete intelligibility and its demand that an ultimate terminus be internally sufficient rather than merely primitive**. It is not a theorem derivable from logic alone.

This supports a conditional transcendental and abductive case, not an unconditional deduction. Its comparative advantage must be earned by delivered explanations—especially of consciousness, normativity, and the relation of ground to world—not simply by promises of future explanation. Making these standards explicit turns a diffuse intuition into a disciplined philosophical position.

## 4. The ontological architecture

### 4.1 Asymmetrical non-duality

The framework rejects both a flat dualism and a flat monism.

- It rejects **dualism** if that means two independent ultimate realities—mind and matter, God and world, subject and object—whose relation would itself require a deeper ground.
- It rejects **eliminative monism** if that means dismissing physics, bodies, pain, individuality, or moral life as simply unreal.

Its positive position is **asymmetrical non-duality**:

1. There is one reality with aseity: the ground.
2. Derivative modes are real, but their reality is participatory rather than self-standing.
3. The ground and its modes are not rival occupants of a shared space. Space, time, matter, and finite subjecthood belong to the derivative order.
4. The dependence of the derivative on the ground is not a process that occurs before or after something else. It is an asymmetric relation of being.

This preserves a central non-dual insight—nothing stands independently alongside the Absolute—without requiring the denial that finite reality is real in a derivative sense.

### 4.2 Creation without temporal change in the ground

“Creation” must not be imagined as an earlier divine event followed by a later universe. Such a picture makes time external to the ground and makes the ground seem to acquire a new state.

The clean formulation is:

> Creation is the timeless dependence of finite, structured, and relational reality on an unconditioned source.

The ground does not become more complete by creating. Derivative reality does not fill a lack in it. If creation is necessary in this framework, its necessity is not coercion by an external force; it is the proposed identity between complete actuality and non-arbitrary plenitude. That identity still requires defence. It should be treated as a central axiom, not smuggled in as a conclusion.

### 4.3 Point, sphere, and grades

The **point** is a limiting symbol for the ground: zero extension, no spatial position, no parts, no boundary, no temporal succession. It is not a physical point located in a pre-existing space.

The **sphere** is a symbol for derivative manifestation: extension, differentiation, relation, boundary, and articulated order. Nested spheres represent grades of existence or increasingly articulated relational structures. The image is valuable because a sphere can be generated from a centre without placing the centre as one more surface point, and because it foregrounds symmetry, boundary, and perspectival location.

The image must not be reified. There is no literal spatial expansion of a timeless point into a universe. In a mature theory, “point-to-sphere” should be represented by a formal relation such as:

- a transition from an unstructured limiting object to a family of structured models;
- a partial order of ontological dependence rather than temporal evolution;
- a hierarchy of algebras, state spaces, or categories;
- an invariance-breaking or information-localising operation that yields finite perspectives.

The image therefore functions as a **research constraint**: the model should show how unity can underwrite multiplicity without making the ground a composite object or treating multiplicity as a second independent substance.

### 4.4 Polarity

Polarity is the framework’s candidate grammar of derivation. It operates at two levels.

**Transcendental polarities** articulate the contrast between ground and derivative order:

- simple / complex
- unlimited / finite
- timeless / temporal
- spaceless / spatial
- non-relational / relational
- universal awareness / local perspective
- unity / multiplicity

**Constitutive polarities** organise physical and informational grades:

- positive / negative
- attraction / repulsion
- symmetry / symmetry-breaking
- possibility / actual record
- inside / outside
- signal / constraint
- order / entropy gradient

The programme proposes that physical polarities are not arbitrary additions but graded expressions of a deeper structural principle. This is a fruitful hypothesis, not yet a derivation. A successful formalisation must state the relevant symmetry group, involution, duality, adjunction, or invariant; merely listing opposites is not theory.

### 4.5 Plenitude

The plenitude hypothesis is the framework’s response to the question of arbitrary selection. If the ground is complete and perfectly non-preferential, why would it realise one admissible structure rather than another equally admissible structure?

The strong answer is:

> Every admissible coherent structure is realised, not as a rival to the ground, but as a derivative grade or branch within the total landscape.

This has several consequences.

- A single universe need not bear the burden of being uniquely selected from all imaginable alternatives.
- Apparent fine-tuning can become indexical: observers necessarily find themselves in a grade compatible with observers.
- Many-worlds, modal possibility, mathematical landscape, and multiverse ideas become partial descriptions of a larger plenitude.
- Physical constants in our universe may be locally fixed without being metaphysically unique.

But plenitude is not entailed by “symmetry” alone. It needs at least three further components:

1. an **admissibility condition** distinguishing coherent or realisable structures from contradictions and arbitrary descriptions;
2. a **realisation principle** explaining why admissibility entails actuality rather than bare possibility;
3. a **measure or weighting rule** explaining probabilities, typicality, and observer selection.

Without these, “all possibilities exist” is too unconstrained to do explanatory work. With them, plenitude becomes a potentially fertile landscape programme.

### 4.6 Defect, limitation, and suffering

A finite mode is defined partly by what it is not. Limitation, exclusion, error, failure, loss, and defect are therefore structurally connected to finitude. On this view, defect is not a positive substance competing with good; it is a privation or failure of a possible fulfilment. Yet it is not unreal. A hole can be a lack and still cause a fall; pain can be derivative and still matter absolutely within the life in which it is felt.

Plenitude and finitude can explain why a totality containing finite modes includes privation. They do **not** show that every concrete instance of suffering was necessary, good, deserved, educational, or morally insignificant. The framework should reject facile claims that the problem of suffering, wasted time, or divine hiddenness has been “dissolved.” At most, it relocates the problem:

- the ground need not choose defects as a separate object;
- finite modes make defects structurally possible, and a plenitude thesis makes such modes unavoidable if all admissible modes are realised;
- the ethical reality of suffering within a finite perspective remains non-negotiable.

This yields compassion rather than dismissal. A derivative perspective is not metaphysically independent, but its injury is not thereby trivial.

### 4.7 Agency and concurrence

The appropriate causal model is **graded concurrence**.

- The ground is the primary ontological condition of every derivative mode and every grade-level causal process.
- Grade-level causes—neurons, organisms, choices, fields, institutions—remain real causes at their own level. They are not competitors with the ground.
- This avoids both deism, which makes the ground absent after an initial act, and crude occasionalism, which turns all created causal structure into fiction.

Finite agency is real as a capacity for representation, deliberation, learning, control, and response to reasons within conditions not chosen by the agent. The framework does not require libertarian freedom understood as an uncaused choice outside all prior structure. Neuroscience, genetics, development, trauma, and social context show that agency is deeply conditioned; they do not by themselves prove a total deterministic universe. The defensible conclusion is **nested or compatibilist agency**, not moral fatalism.

At the level of the ground, “will” and “necessity” should not be projected from ordinary human choosing without qualification. A necessary plenitude model treats actualisation as flowing from the ground’s identity; a classical theistic model treats creation as freely willed. These are distinct theological models. They cannot be made identical merely by calling necessity “freedom.”

### 4.8 Normativity, goodness, and the desire for perfection

The framework aims to ground normativity without treating moral laws as disconnected primitive commands. The proposed route is participatory: if derivative creatures have real natures, their flourishing can be objectively better for them than their frustration; if the ground is metaphysically good, finite goods are derivative participations in that goodness.

That route has several distinct steps that must not be collapsed:

1. **Metaphysical goodness:** being or actuality is good in the sense of being an intelligible fulfilment rather than a privation.
2. **Creaturely goods:** a kind of creature can flourish or be damaged relative to its capacities and form of life.
3. **Reasons:** agents capable of representing goods have reasons to respond to them.
4. **Moral standing:** other centres of experience and agency are not merely instruments.
5. **Obligation:** duties arise only with further principles governing non-arbitrary regard, accountability, agency, conflict, and proportional ordering of goods.

The first step does not by itself entail the fifth. A viable normative extension therefore needs explicit principles of responsiveness to basic goods, equal or non-arbitrary regard for comparable subjects, respect for rational agency, accountability for avoidable harms, and proportional adjudication where goods conflict. This is a unified participatory normative blueprint, not a deduction of morality from bare existence.

The experience of longing for truth, beauty, wholeness, or perfection is compatible with this picture: a finite perspective can experience its incompleteness as orientation toward fuller participation in the good. It is not a proof of a perfect ground. Natural selection, culture, imagination, and social aspiration also bear on why humans have such desires. At most, the desire is a datum for comparative explanation, not a decisive transcendental premise.

Privation theory remains a metaphysical account of defect, not a theodicy. It neither justifies particular suffering nor converts the structure of finitude into moral permission.

## 5. Consciousness: from explanatory gap to structural–experiential identity

### 5.1 The challenge to a posteriori identity

The phenomenal-concept challenge remains a central starting point. A physicalist may say that pain is identical with some physical state and that the identity is discovered a posteriori, like water’s identity with H₂O. The difficulty is that the phenomenal concept of pain seems to disclose pain as hurtfulness—as something intrinsically felt—not as a neural or functional description.

A phenomenal-concept strategy can explain why first-person concepts fail to reveal neural implementation. It may solve a meta-problem: why consciousness seems resistant to physical explanation. It does not automatically answer the hard problem: why any physical or functional process is accompanied by experience at all.

### 5.2 The structural limitation of physics

Physics is extraordinarily successful at describing structure: dynamical relations, symmetries, fields, state transitions, probabilities, conserved quantities, and information-bearing interactions. Even a complete physical theory may leave open the intrinsic character of whatever instantiates that structure.

This motivates the framework’s central move:

> Concrete reality has two inseparable poles: a structural pole available to third-person description and an experiential pole available from within.

The claim is not that structure and experience are two substances. Nor is it that human consciousness creates the physical world. It is a constitutive or dual-aspect thesis: the same concrete mode can be structurally describable and experientially present.

This resembles forms of Russellian monism, dual-aspect monism, panexperientialism, and cosmopsychism, but the framework gives it a distinctive hierarchy: fundamental awareness belongs to the unbounded ground; finite modes have derivative, graded experiential interiors rather than being tiny self-sufficient minds.

### 5.3 Why pure awareness is proposed

The move from an aseitic ground to pure awareness is not a strict deduction from logic. It rests on a cumulative argument.

1. Structural accounts specify relations and roles.
2. Relations alone appear insufficient to account for the intrinsic fact of felt experience.
3. Experience is the only directly known candidate for intrinsic presence rather than merely relational role.
4. If all derivative modes ultimately depend on one ground, and experience cannot emerge from utterly experience-less being without an unexplained bridge, then the ground is plausibly experiential in the strongest, non-finite sense: pure awareness.

This is a serious argument, but it has explicit bridge premises. A reductive physicalist may deny that there is any further intrinsic fact to explain; an illusionist may deny robust phenomenal consciousness; an emergence theorist may accept unexplained emergence; a neutral monist may posit a non-experiential neutral base. The framework should state that it **prefers** pure awareness because it avoids emergence of phenomenality from an allegedly wholly non-phenomenal base, not because it has disproved every alternative.

### 5.4 Meaning, thought, and world

The intuition that “a universe of matter with no mind is absurd” contains two different claims.

1. **Epistemic-transcendental claim:** facts, meanings, concepts, and theories are intelligible only in relation to awareness. A tree as known, described, or meaningful presupposes a knower.
2. **Ontological claim:** a tree could not exist at all except as present in or sustained by awareness.

The first claim is strong and difficult to deny. The second requires an additional bridge principle. The framework supplies one: concrete actuality requires an intrinsic aspect, and awareness is the candidate for that aspect. But semantic dependence alone cannot prove ontological idealism; otherwise one mistakes the conditions for describing an object for the conditions of the object’s existence.

The mature position is therefore not solipsism. It is **grounded ideal realism**: physical objects are real, stable, public, and law-governed in the derivative order, but their ultimate being and intelligibility are sustained by the ground’s awareness rather than by an independent material substance.

### 5.5 Perspectives and the decombination problem

The central consciousness problem is not only combination—how many micro-experiences make one macro-experience. It is also **decombination**: how one unbounded awareness can be present as many bounded, apparently separate perspectives.

The proposed solution schema is informational and relational:

- the ground is not divided into pieces;
- a perspective is a local, bounded organisation of information and causal access within a grade;
- a finite subject is not a second substance but a self-locating mode with restricted access to the total structure;
- the whole need not be ignorant even if a local perspective lacks information.

An analogy is a complete formal system containing many partial views, though no analogy should be mistaken for a mechanism. The framework must still construct a precise individuation map: what makes one local perspective numerically distinct from another, what determines its memory boundary, and how its experiential unity is preserved through change.

Until such a map exists, decombination is not solved; it is cleanly posed.

### 5.6 Personhood and theology

Pure awareness alone does not logically entail personhood in the robust sense of intellect, intentionality, will, value, and interpersonal responsiveness. Nor does metaphysical completeness by itself prove moral goodness. A theistic reading is possible if the ground is further argued to include intelligibility, self-knowledge, purposive agency, and goodness; those are additional philosophical tasks.

Similarly, claims that death necessarily dissolves finite perspective, restores omniscience, or reveals a full divine identity are not consequences of the present framework. They may be religious hopes or doctrinal claims, but they are not derivable from the point–sphere model alone.

## 6. Physics as the structural pole

### 6.1 What physics can and cannot settle

Physics constrains the framework decisively, but it does not by itself choose among all ultimate ontologies. Standard quantum mechanics, relativity, field theory, and evolutionary biology are compatible with multiple metaphysical interpretations. No current experiment directly selects classical theism, non-dual idealism, neutral monism, materialism, or plenitude.

The framework treats physics as the study of the **structural pole** of derivative reality. This does not mean physics is “merely conventional” or unreal. Its structures are precisely the stable, public, predictive grammar through which finite modes encounter one another.

The claim that whatever physics studies is structural is useful. The stronger claim that everything non-structural is therefore experience is a substantive metaphysical axiom, not a logical consequence. It must be defended by the consciousness argument above.

### 6.2 The tiered architecture

A disciplined model separates four layers.

1. **Ground layer:** the non-derivative source; not a physical system, not a quantum state, and not an object in Hilbert space.
2. **Landscape layer:** the space of admissible grades or world-structures, together with a realisation principle and measure.
3. **Physical layer:** the structural dynamics within a particular grade—our quantum fields, spacetime, particles, constants, thermodynamics, biology, and cosmology.
4. **Perspective layer:** local informational boundaries, records, observers, agency, and experiential interiors.

Confusion follows when a theory appropriate to one layer is asked to answer another. Quantum mechanics describes the physical layer; it cannot by itself be the ground. A first-person experience occurs in the perspective layer; it need not cause a special physical collapse.

### 6.3 Quantum theory: an interpretative fit map, not a synthesis

The framework does not derive Hilbert-space quantum mechanics, select an interpretation, or merge incompatible interpretations into one theory. It should not be presented as a quantum synthesis. The ground is not a quantum system, the point-to-sphere image does not generate the Born rule, and plenitude is not identical to Everettian branching.

What it can provide at this stage is an **interpretative fit map**. It asks which features of an interpretation would be congenial to the framework if that interpretation were independently supported.

- A relational account of facts fits a perspective-sensitive ontology.
- Decoherence and environmental records fit the idea of stable grade-level structure.
- Everettian branch structure is an analogy for certain forms of derivative plurality, not a derivation of plenitude.
- QBist emphasis on updating and experience may illuminate perspective, provided a shared world is retained.
- Transactional or boundary-value formalisms may be useful models of global consistency, not established retrocausal metaphysics.
- Bohmian, collapse, and superdeterministic theories remain distinct physical hypotheses with distinct formal and empirical obligations.

Any physical implementation must obey no-signalling, relativistic constraints or a tested replacement, Bell data, and the Born rule. Consciousness has no privileged collapse-causing role merely because the framework regards experience as intrinsic. Whether quantum evolution is universally unitary, objectively collapsing, guided by hidden variables, or otherwise structured remains an open empirical and theoretical question.

The legitimate conclusion is modest: quantum interpretations can be located as possible local grammars of a derivative grade. The framework may constrain their metaphysical reading, but it neither proves nor replaces their physics.

### 6.4 What is retained from major interpretations

| Interpretation or programme | Surviving contribution | Constraint or rejection |
|---|---|---|
| **Copenhagen** | Complementarity and operational discipline. | Insufficient as a final ontology if it refuses every account of what exists. |
| **Relational quantum mechanics** | Facts are relative to interactions; no privileged external observer is required. | A relational account still needs a precise consistency rule across overlapping observers. |
| **QBism** | Quantum states can encode agent-relative expectations; experience and updating matter. | Agents must be universalised beyond humans, and the shared structural world cannot be reduced to private belief. |
| **Everett / many-worlds** | Unitary evolution, decoherence, and branch plurality. | “Worlds” are not independent substances; probability and the Born rule still require derivation. |
| **Many minds** | Takes the experiential side of branching seriously. | Rejects a dualist multiplication of separate immaterial minds. Perspectives are derivative modes of one ground. |
| **Transactional interpretation** | Time symmetry and interaction as an actualising relation. | Advanced/retarded waves cannot be added as literal ontology without a relativistically rigorous model; one global actualised outcome conflicts with unrestricted Everettian branching unless reformulated. |
| **Consistent histories / modal approaches** | Decoherent histories, stable record sets, and modal structure. | A choice of consistent set or a probability measure cannot remain unexplained. |
| **Bohmian mechanics** | A clear reminder that quantum theory may admit a realist ontology and guidance structure. | A preferred foliation, configuration ontology, and relation to relativity need justification; it is not automatically the framework’s preferred model. |
| **Objective collapse** | Makes a sharp empirical alternative possible. | The framework does not posit collapse without evidence. Collapse models remain testable rivals, not ingredients to combine indiscriminately. |
| **Superdeterminism** | Highlights that correlations may be global rather than independent local events. | It earns explanatory status only with a concrete causal model, a non-conspiratorial measure, and distinctive predictions; global necessity alone is not an explanation of Bell data. |
| **Quantum Darwinism** | Environmental redundancy and record formation help explain the emergent classical world. | It does not settle the ontology of quantum states or consciousness. |
| **Valentini-style non-equilibrium** | Offers a genuinely testable beyond-quantum possibility through Born-rule deviations. | It is speculative and cannot be identified with another “grade” unless a precise bridge is supplied. |
| **Biological Darwinism** | Gives a local structural grammar for variation, inheritance, selection, and adaptation. | It neither proves materialism nor decides the ultimate ontology. |

The table is a map of resonances and tensions, not a composite interpretation. Relational facts, participatory interaction, unitary/decoherent structure, branch-level plurality, and experiential interiors may be jointly explored in a specific formal model, but no such model has yet been constructed. Literal collapse, unmodelled superdeterminism, and incompatible hidden-variable additions cannot simply be retained together.

### 6.5 Probability

A plenitude or branch ontology faces an unavoidable probability question. If multiple admissible outcomes or histories are real, why should a local observer use Born weights rather than counting branches, weighting by complexity, or some other measure?

Any completed theory must provide one of the following:

- a derivation of the Born rule from symmetry, decision theory, envariance, typicality, or information geometry;
- a measure on the landscape and on decoherent histories that reduces to Born weights in our grade;
- an empirically distinguishable alternative probability law.

Without this, plenitude remains an ontological picture rather than a quantum theory.

## 7. Formal research programme

The programme should be formalised in layers rather than by prematurely translating metaphysical terms into physics vocabulary.

### 7.1 A minimal formal skeleton

Let \(\mathcal{L}\) be the landscape of admissible grades and \(g\in\mathcal{L}\) a particular grade.

1. **Grade structure.** Model grades as objects of a category, higher category, or partially ordered class \(\mathcal{G}\), where the ordering represents ontological dependence or structural refinement, not earlier and later times.

2. **Physical structure.** Associate to each physically articulated grade \(g\) a functorial quantum theory, for example an algebraic quantum field theory
   \[
   \mathsf{A}_g: \mathsf{Loc}_g \rightarrow \mathsf{Alg}_g,
   \]
   assigning local regions or operational contexts to observable algebras. This gives a rigorous home for locality, causality, symmetry, states, and interactions.

3. **Perspectives.** Define a perspective \(p\) not as a ghostly observer but as a bounded information-bearing subsystem or subalgebra together with its accessible records:
   \[
   p=(\mathsf{A}_{\mathrm{local}},\rho,\mathcal{R},\partial p).
   \]
   Here \(\rho\) is the relevant state, \(\mathcal{R}\) the record structure, and \(\partial p\) the information boundary.

4. **Relational facts.** An interaction channel
   \[
   \Phi_{ab}: \mathsf{A}_a\otimes\mathsf{A}_b\rightarrow \mathsf{A}_{ab}
   \]
   yields a relative fact when it produces a robust, decoherence-stable record. The theory must define compatibility conditions for facts shared by overlapping perspectives.

5. **Experiential assignment.** Introduce an explicit bridge map or functor
   \[
   \mathsf{E}: \mathsf{Persp}\rightarrow\mathsf{Phen},
   \]
   from a category of perspectival structures to a category of phenomenal organisations. The existence and character of \(\mathsf{E}\) is the core open problem; writing the symbol does not solve it.

6. **Coherence constraints on \(\mathsf{E}\).** At minimum it should respect structural isomorphism, composition/decomposition, temporal continuity of memory, and no-signalling. It must explain why a unified perspective does not decompose into unrelated micro-subjects, and why multiple perspectives do not require a literal fragmentation of the ground.

7. **Polarity.** Represent polarity through specified dualities, involutions, adjunctions, order reversals, or symmetry-breaking maps. The framework must identify which mathematical operation corresponds to each claimed polarity rather than treating all contrasts as one vague principle.

8. **Plenitude.** Define the landscape as a moduli space, stack, class of models, or constrained solution space:
   \[
   \mathcal{L}=\{M\mid M\text{ satisfies admissibility, consistency, and realisability conditions}\}.
   \]
   The next indispensable object is a measure \(\mu\) over \(\mathcal{L}\) and its observer-indexed subspaces.

9. **Grounding relation.** The ground should not be represented as another node in \(\mathcal{L}\). At most, mathematics can model a limit condition, universal property, or dependence relation that expresses how every derivative structure is non-independent. The ground itself is not thereby captured exhaustively by a formal object.

### 7.2 The decombination programme: from one ground to bounded perspectives

The central technical problem is to make “one ground, many bounded perspectives” more than a slogan. It is important to state the target exactly. A model of domain formation is **not yet** a model of subject formation, and a structural model of subject formation is **not yet** a derivation of phenomenality. The programme therefore has three distinct tasks:

1. construct stable, bounded, internally coherent domains;
2. show that those domains possess the structural conditions proposed for a perspective;
3. defend the bridge principle on which such a structure has an experiential interior.

The following is a rigorous candidate for tasks 1 and 2. It begins by postulating an experiential/order field; it does not derive that field from physics alone.

#### 7.2.1 Constrained graph-field model

Let \(G=(V,E)\) be a finite connected graph with \(|V|=N\), graph Laplacian \(L\), and field values \(\Psi_i\in\mathbb{R}^d\). Impose a zero-mean constraint,
\[
\sum_{i\in V}\Psi_i=0,
\]
with projection onto the constrained subspace
\[
P=I-\frac1N\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^\top.
\]

Count each undirected edge once, so \(\sum_{(i,j)\in E}\|\Psi_i-\Psi_j\|^2=\Psi^\top L\Psi\). Define
\[
F[\Psi]=\sum_{i\in V}\frac g4\big(\|\Psi_i\|^2-a\big)^2+\frac J2\Psi^\top L\Psi+U_{\mathrm{aniso}}[\Psi],
\qquad g,a,J>0.
\]
The quartic term makes \(F\) coercive and gives a well-defined finite-graph Gibbs measure
\[
p_\beta(\Psi)=Z_\beta^{-1}e^{-\beta F[\Psi]}.
\]
A constrained stochastic dynamics that preserves the mean exactly is
\[
d\Psi=-P\nabla F(\Psi)\,dt+\sqrt{2/\beta}\,P\,dW.
\]
Equivalently, the constraint can be imposed with a time-dependent Lagrange multiplier.

For \(d>1\), the unmodified potential has a continuous Mexican-hat vacuum manifold, not a set of discrete phases. If discrete phase labels are needed, an anisotropy term is required, for example
\[
U_{\mathrm{aniso}}(x,y)=\kappa x^2y^2
\]
or, for \(K\) phases represented by \(z\in\mathbb C\),
\[
U_{\mathrm{aniso}}(z)=-\kappa\operatorname{Re}(z^K).
\]

#### 7.2.2 What the bifurcation actually establishes

On the zero-mean subspace, the quadratic operator about \(\Psi=0\) is
\[
H=JL-gaI.
\]
If the Fiedler eigenvalue \(\lambda_2\) is simple, the first local instability occurs at
\[
J_c=\frac{ga}{\lambda_2}.
\]
Near this point, a branch in the Fiedler direction \(v^{(2)}\) has, under the stated normalization, amplitude
\[
c^2=\frac{ga-J\lambda_2}{g\sum_i(v_i^{(2)})^4},
\qquad c\propto\sqrt{J_c-J}.
\]

This is a **local deterministic bifurcation result**, not a proof that subjects divide. It has several limits.

- The onsite potential controls local amplitude: it favours \(\|\Psi_i\|\approx\sqrt a\). It is not correct to say that large \(J\) creates local ordering.
- \(J\) controls spatial coherence and domain-wall cost. Coherent, low-noise domains depend on the competition between \(J\) and thermal noise \(1/\beta\); very weak coupling can produce independently chosen local phases rather than coherent domains.
- The Fiedler branch is only a local guide. Competing eigenmodes and metastable patterns must be compared using the full nonlinear free energy.
- For finite \(V\), the Gibbs measure remains symmetry-invariant and can mix among equivalent patterns. The precise claim is bifurcation of deterministic gradient flow plus metastable domain states. Literal spontaneous symmetry breaking requires a thermodynamic limit, an infinitesimal symmetry-breaking field, or conditioning on an attractor.

The model therefore supplies a candidate **domain-formation mechanism**, not a decombination law by itself.

#### 7.2.3 Candidate perspectives: four jointly necessary structural tests

For a candidate interior \(A\subset V\), choose a disjoint graph blanket \(B\subset V\) and exterior \(R=V\setminus(A\cup B)\), with \(B\) separating \(A\) from \(R\) in \(G\). Keep this static graph blanket distinct from a dynamic boundary state \(B_t\).

A domain is a *candidate perspective* only when all of the following are jointly met.

**D — Dissociation / screening.**
\[
I(\Psi_A;\Psi_R\mid\Psi_B)\le\varepsilon.
\]
For a positive nearest-neighbour Gibbs field, graph separation supplies exact conditional independence in the ideal model. In real data it must be estimated and bounded rather than assumed.

**I — Integration.** For every non-trivial bipartition \(A=A_1\sqcup A_2\), let \(B_{12}\) be an appropriate separator within the candidate domain. Require
\[
\Phi(A)=\min_{A=A_1\sqcup A_2}
I(\Psi_{A_1};\Psi_{A_2}\mid\Psi_{B_{12}})\ge\theta.
\]
The threshold and estimator must be preregistered and compared with matched nulls: a shared global mode can otherwise inflate static mutual information without creating a unified domain.

**S — Intervention-validated self-model.** Add an internal model state \(M_t\), inputs \(u_t\), and dynamics such as
\[
M_{t+1}=f_\theta(M_t,\Psi_{A,t},u_t)+\eta_t,
\qquad
B_{t+1}=h_\phi(B_t,M_t,\Psi_{R,t})+\xi_t.
\]
A structural self-model must improve prediction over a matched null:
\[
\Delta_{\rm self}=I(B_{t+1};M_t\mid B_t)-I(B_{t+1};M_t^{\rm null}\mid B_t)>\delta.
\]
The advantage must disappear when \(M_t\) is shuffled, lesioned, or otherwise causally disrupted. A static field cannot satisfy this requirement.

**P — Stable, intervention-robust closure.** The internal loop must have a stable attractor and resist direct exterior dependence except through its boundary and inputs:
\[
\rho\!\left(D\mathcal T_A\big|_{\mu_A^*}\right)<1,
\qquad
I(M_{t+1};R_t\mid M_t,B_t,u_t)\le\varepsilon,
\]
with the second inequality tested under interventions on \(R_t\), not only passive observations.

Under a separately defended structural–experiential bridge principle, a domain satisfying D, I, S, and P is a **candidate subject**. The equations do not prove that a candidate subject is phenomenally conscious; they formulate the exact correlates that the framework proposes to associate with perspective.

#### 7.2.4 Research obligations

The decombination programme must now:

1. specify graphs, potentials, noise levels, phase structure, estimators, and thresholds before simulation;
2. demonstrate D, I, S, and P jointly on the same \((A,B,R)\), not one condition at a time;
3. compare against null models with matched global modes and connectivity;
4. establish finite-size, noise, and metastability scaling;
5. give identity conditions for a candidate perspective through time, memory disruption, and branching;
6. map \((J,g,a,\beta,\kappa,\theta,\delta)\) to neural or other measurable observables before making claims about anaesthesia, split-brain phenomena, psychedelics, dissociation, or disorders of consciousness.

This is a productive point of vulnerability. It supplies a mathematically meaningful domain-and-boundary mechanism and an operational subject criterion, but it does not yet solve decombination, establish a neural mapping, or derive phenomenality. A framework that cannot meet these obligations should not claim to have solved consciousness.

### 7.3 Mathematical resources

Several fields can contribute, each with a limited role.

- **Algebraic and categorical quantum field theory:** local observables, functoriality, compositional processes, covariance, and the structural description of quantum systems.
- **Category theory and higher categories:** relations between grades, dualities, composition, universal properties, and maps between structural descriptions.
- **Information theory:** accessible information, entropy, records, causal boundaries, coding, and observer-relative updates. Shannon information, semantic information, and phenomenal meaning must not be conflated.
- **Quantum information:** entanglement, reference frames, decoherence, error correction, and operational notions of subsystem.
- **Geometry, topology, and moduli theory:** spaces of admissible structures, invariants, phase transitions, and symmetry-breaking.
- **The Langlands programme:** a source of deep correspondence and duality ideas, not an established route from metaphysics to particle physics. It should be used only where a concrete mathematical correspondence can be specified.
- **Computational search and quantum computing:** useful for exploring candidate model spaces, solving bounded optimisation problems, and testing consistency. They cannot manufacture a derivation that has not supplied its axioms, measure, or selection rule.

### 7.4 Constants and law selection

Symmetry and parsimony are powerful constraints, but they do not alone determine observed dimensionless constants. Many highly symmetric theories contain continuous parameters, discrete choices, or multiple vacua. To claim derivation of constants, the programme must provide:

1. a unique mathematical structure or sharply limited family;
2. a theorem linking that structure to effective low-energy physics;
3. a dynamical or measure-theoretic selection principle;
4. numerical outputs for dimensionless constants with error bars or exact values;
5. a route from the model to observation that does not fit parameters after the fact.

Until then, point-to-sphere, polarity, plenitude, and consciousness provide a conceptual search heuristic, not a derivation of the Standard Model, general relativity, or cosmological parameters.

## 8. Empirical programme and falsifiability

### 8.1 Three levels of claim

The programme must distinguish three levels.

| Level | Claim type | Standard of success |
|---|---|---|
| **I. Metaphysical interpretation** | The ground, structural–experiential identity, and participatory creation offer the most intelligible ontology. | Conceptual coherence, explanatory compression, and comparison with alternatives. |
| **II. Formal unification** | A precise mathematical architecture connects grades, perspectives, quantum structure, and phenomenality. | Definitions, proofs, recovery theorems, internal consistency, and parameter reduction. |
| **III. Empirical theory** | The architecture yields novel measurable consequences. | Quantitative predictions that can fail and outperform rival theories. |

A Level I framework should not present itself as a Level III discovery merely because it is compatible with known science.

### 8.2 Non-negotiable recovery tests

Before offering novel effects, any formal model must recover or preserve:

- Lorentz covariance or a demonstrated empirically equivalent replacement;
- no superluminal signalling;
- the observed Bell-inequality violations without illicit local hidden variables;
- the Born rule or a tested replacement;
- thermodynamic consistency and the observed arrow of time;
- the empirical content of quantum field theory, general relativity, chemistry, biology, and neuroscience in their validated regimes;
- stable public records and intersubjective agreement among overlapping perspectives.

Failure at any of these points disqualifies a proposed formal implementation.

### 8.3 Concrete research tracks

**Track A — Quantum relational record theory**

- Construct an AQFT or quantum-information model of interaction-generated records.
- Define when a record is stable relative to a perspective.
- Derive conditions under which different observers’ records agree.
- Recover decoherence and classical histories.
- Derive or test the probability rule.

**Success criterion:** a mathematically exact relational measurement model that makes no use of a special consciousness-triggered collapse and reproduces standard quantum predictions.

**Track B — Perspective and phenomenology**

- Define operational proxies for perspectival organisation: causal integration, access structure, memory, self-model stability, and information boundary.
- Compare these with psychophysics, neural perturbation, anaesthesia, split-brain data, disorders of consciousness, and report-independent markers.
- Test invariance claims: if two systems preserve a proposed relevant structure, should they preserve the same phenomenal organisation or report profile?

**Success criterion:** non-trivial, preregistered structure–phenomenology relations that are not post hoc redescriptions of known neural correlates.

**Track C — Parameterised experiential coupling**

The base framework need not posit a new physical influence of consciousness. If it does posit one, it must be explicit. Introduce a parameter \(\lambda\) linking a formally defined perspectival quantity \(I(p)\) to a physical observable such as decoherence, interference visibility, or noise:
\[
\Delta O = \lambda f(I(p),\text{control variables}).
\]

- Specify \(f\) before data collection.
- Compare systems with controlled physical complexity and isolation.
- Pre-register a null and alternative model.
- Let existing and new experiments bound or estimate \(\lambda\).

**Success criterion:** a reproducible non-zero effect not already explained by uncontrolled physical complexity. If \(\lambda=0\), the metaphysical dual-aspect thesis may remain, but it loses this route to empirical distinctiveness.

**Track D — Beyond-equilibrium and global-constraint models**

- If a Valentini-like non-equilibrium extension is adopted, derive a quantitative deviation from the Born rule and identify laboratory, astrophysical, or cosmological tests.
- If a superdeterministic/global-constraint model is adopted, write its causal graph, probability distribution, and distinctive forecast. Cosmic Bell tests and setting-generation experiments constrain naive versions but do not test an undefined “global necessity.”

**Success criterion:** an actual alternative distribution, not the claim that any observed correlation could have been globally fixed.

**Track E — Landscape and constants**

- Define admissible grades and a measure over them.
- Derive observer-selection effects rather than invoking them after the fact.
- Compare predicted distributions of dimensionless constants, entropy conditions, or cosmological observables with alternatives.

**Success criterion:** prospective constraints that narrow possible observations.

### 8.4 What would count against the programme

The framework becomes scientifically vulnerable only through its specific implementations. Relevant failures include:

- no coherent formal account of individuation and phenomenal unity;
- failure to derive or match quantum probabilities;
- violation of relativistic no-signalling or observed covariance;
- a landscape measure that is undefined, arbitrary, or unable to generate observations;
- no parameter reduction and no novel predictions after sustained formal development;
- a rival theory that explains consciousness, physics, and their connection with fewer primitive assumptions and equal or greater empirical power;
- a derived prediction contradicted by measurement.

These conditions protect the programme from becoming an all-purpose reinterpretation immune to evidence.

## 9. Interpretive guardrails

### 9.1 Mathematics

Mathematical truths are strong examples of apparently non-temporal and non-spatial necessity. They support the thought that not all reality need be spatiotemporal. But their ontological status is disputed, and even mathematical Platonism does not automatically yield a concrete causal ground. Equations express identities and relations; their atemporality does not by itself turn them into creators.

### 9.2 Infinity and symmetry

A bi-infinite line is invariant under translations along its own axis, illustrating a kind of symmetry. It is not therefore free of all relations, perfectly simple, or metaphysically unlimited: it has a structure, dimensionality, and possible embedding relations. The lesson is analogical: genuine ultimacy should not depend on an external boundary. It is not a proof from geometry to God.

Likewise, perfect symmetry does not automatically entail plenitude. The transition from no arbitrary preference to all admissible actualities is a major thesis that requires its own formal statement.

### 9.3 Light and photons

Light is an important theological and philosophical symbol of intelligibility, presence, and disclosure. Physics does not establish that light exists “outside spacetime.” A photon travels on a null spacetime interval, but a photon has no valid inertial rest frame and therefore no literal first-person “photon perspective.” Physical statements about massless particles cannot prove claims about a timeless ground. Symbolic or scriptural resonances should be kept distinct from physical inference.

### 9.4 Gödel

Gödel’s incompleteness theorems show limitations of sufficiently rich formal systems under particular assumptions. They do not prove that ultimate reality is unknowable, that non-dualism is true, or that a metaphysical impasse is unavoidable. They are relevant as a warning against premature claims of final formal closure, not as a shortcut to metaphysics.

### 9.5 Simulation language

“Simulation” is useful only if stripped of the standard picture of an external computer, programmer, hardware substrate, and simulated inhabitants. Within this framework it can mean an internally rule-governed informational realisation: a derivative grade represented and sustained within the ground. It must not reintroduce a higher material world and merely relocate the explanatory problem.

### 9.6 Theology and scripture

The framework can resonate with themes of divine light, continuous dependence, degrees of being, and a world sustained by divine knowledge. It does not by itself settle doctrinal questions, identify nested grades with scriptural heavens, or establish the exact relation between necessary plenitude and a tradition’s account of divine freedom. Those require textual, theological, and historical scholarship in addition to metaphysical argument.

## 10. Consolidated insights

The following claims survive as the framework’s strongest core.

1. **The ultimate question is ontological, not chronological.** A beginning in time is neither necessary nor sufficient for a ground.
2. **Eternal physical structure is not automatically self-subsistent.** It needs either a proof of necessity or an acceptance of brute ultimacy.
3. **Brute fact and regress are live logical options but weak ultimate explanations unless endowed with a positive account of their necessity.**
4. **The most demanding explanatory norm points toward an aseitic, simple, non-derivative ground.** This is a conditional metaphysical conclusion, not a premise-free theorem.
5. **Physics describes relational structure with exceptional success but does not obviously disclose the intrinsic character of its relata.**
6. **Phenomenal consciousness is the decisive pressure point for reductive physicalism.** Explaining why consciousness seems mysterious is not identical to explaining why it exists.
7. **A structural–experiential identity is a serious alternative to both dualism and reductive materialism.** It avoids treating experience as a causal afterthought or a second substance.
8. **The framework is cosmopsychist rather than micropsychist.** Fundamental awareness belongs to the ground; finite experiential modes are derivative and graded.
9. **Asymmetrical non-duality preserves one independent reality while allowing derivative physics, individuality, and suffering to be real rather than dismissed.**
10. **Point-to-sphere and polarity are promising generative metaphors only insofar as they become explicit formal structures.**
11. **Plenitude can explain non-arbitrary diversity only if it acquires an admissibility criterion, realisation principle, and measure.**
12. **Quantum theory supplies an interpretative fit map, not a metaphysical synthesis.** Relational facts, decoherence, and stable records are congenial resources; no quantum interpretation is derived or selected, and consciousness need not cause collapse.
13. **Existing interpretations of quantum mechanics contribute partial insights; they cannot simply be stacked together without resolving their incompatible mechanisms.**
14. **Finite agency is nested and conditioned, but it need not be unreal.** Moral seriousness and compassion remain appropriate even if libertarian freedom fails.
15. **Defect is plausibly the privative shadow of finitude, but this does not erase concrete suffering or settle theodicy.**
16. **A participatory account of normativity needs further bridge principles.** Metaphysical goodness, flourishing, reasons, standing, and obligation are distinct levels; none is automatically deduced from bare being.
17. **The longing for perfection is a relevant datum, not a proof.** It fits a participatory account of finite orientation toward the good, while remaining compatible with rival psychological and naturalistic explanations.
18. **The programme becomes science only where it yields formal recovery results and risky empirical predictions.** Until then it is a disciplined metaphysical research framework.

## 11. Compact statement of the framework

1. The framework posits one non-derivative ground: simple, self-subsistent, and not located in space or time.
2. The derivative order is real but non-independent; it is created or participatory being, not a second absolute.
3. The ground is proposed to be pure awareness because structural description alone does not explain phenomenal presence and awareness is the only directly known intrinsic mode.
4. Every derivative mode is hypothesised to have a structural pole and an experiential pole. Neither is reducible to an independently complete substance.
5. Finite perspectives are proposed to arise through bounded information, relation, and grade-level organisation, not through literal fragmentation of the ground.
6. Point-to-sphere expresses the non-temporal articulation of unity into structured multiplicity; polarity expresses the candidate grammar of finite differentiation.
7. Plenitude is the hypothesis that every admissible coherent grade is realised; it requires a formal landscape and measure.
8. Our universe is one grade with its own physical laws, constants, decoherent histories, and finite perspectives.
9. A candidate quantum reading treats measurement as relational record formation and perspectival actualisation rather than consciousness-triggered global collapse; it does not yet select among physical interpretations.
10. The ground concurs with every grade-level cause without cancelling the reality of grade-level causal powers.
11. Finitude makes privation and defect structurally possible; finite suffering remains ethically real.
12. The programme’s decisive unfinished tasks are the formal map from ground to grades, the individuation of perspectives, the structure–experience bridge, the probability measure, the recovery of known physics, and novel testable predictions.

This is the stable form of the project: ambitious without pretending that its open obligations have already been discharged.
