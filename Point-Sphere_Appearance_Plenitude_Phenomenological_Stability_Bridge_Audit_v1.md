# AP4C3C1 Phenomenological Stability-Bridge Audit v1
## Result: temporal self-presentation supports retentional coherence, not AP4C1 error correction

## Question

After AP4C3A, the remaining possible route was not a bare derivation from core identity but an independently warranted phenomenological bridge:

> Does prereflective self-manifestation and temporal retention require a finite mode to preserve/correct an identifiable trace under a minimal perturbation?

A positive answer would have to supply the four bridge layers B1–B4: a perturbation relation, an error-versus-genuine-change distinction, a preservation rule, and a mapping to the AP4C1 witness code.

## Sources consulted

- [Phenomenological Approaches to Self-Consciousness, SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/self-consciousness-phenomenological/) — prereflective self-consciousness is presented as non-objectifying, first-order self-manifestation. Its temporal discussion describes primal impression, retention, and protention; each passing moment remains in “intentional currency,” producing experienced diachronic coherence, while retention is not a literal replay of a past note.
- [Temporal Consciousness, SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/consciousness-temporal/) — surveys competing retentional, extensional, hybrid, and anti-realist accounts. It describes Husserlian retention as presenting a past phase *as past*, and discusses temporal/postdictive illusions in which later information can alter a reported temporal organization.
- [Self-Consciousness, SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/self-consciousness/) — distinguishes immunity to error through *misidentification* of the subject from a general guarantee of correctness about represented content; it also presents prereflective self-awareness as an implicit mode rather than an explicit self-object.
- [Edmund Husserl, SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/husserl/) — presents primal-impression/retention/protention as a unified field whose contents change progressively, and warns against treating a method of quantification as the measure of being.

These sources support an interpretive phenomenological route, not a public proof of the Point–Sphere core.

## Audit

| Requirement | Phenomenological result | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| **B1 — minimal perturbation relation** | Retentional accounts offer an endogenous ordering of experienced phases and continuity across change. They do not provide a counterfactual adjacency metric, a non-gauge notion of one minimal alteration, or a Hamming distance on modes. | **not supplied** |
| **B2 — witness error versus genuine modal change** | Prereflective self-awareness concerns the givenness of lived experience. Retention presents prior experience as past; it is not a separate witness reading whose deviation can be classified as error. Immunity to error through misidentification, where accepted, concerns who the subject is, not whether an experiential/record content is correct. | **not supplied** |
| **B3 — recovery/preservation across perturbation** | Retention gives a limited rationale for retentional coherence: the just-past remains intentionally available as just-past. But it is intrinsically modified, and phenomenological accounts allow changing contents, temporal illusions, and postdictive reorganization. No source entails recovery of an unchanged trace after an arbitrary local alteration. | **weak support only for endogenous coherence; no AP4C1 stability law** |
| **B4 — AP4C1 code architecture** | The sources supply neither multiple independent witnesses, a witness-name gauge, a diagonal code, a redundancy criterion, an error ball, nor a decoder. Rival models of temporal consciousness remain live; phenomenology does not choose a three-witness repetition code. | **not supplied** |

## Positive residue

The strongest defensible phenomenological bridge is weaker than AP4C1:

\[
\mathsf{RetentionalCoherence}:
\quad
\text{finite self-presentation has a non-objectifying endogenous reference to its just-past phase.}
\]

This is consistent with the AP3N retentional-order result, but AP3N already showed that endogenous coherent order leaves all five grammar families viable. It therefore adds no derivation of public redundancy, error correction, IAR, or physical objectivity.

The very phenomenological details that make the route attractive also block the stronger inference: retention is not numerical preservation of an unaltered token; it is a modified presentation of a past phase as past. A changing lived mode cannot be equated without argument to a corrupted witness configuration.

## Anti-confirmation-bias result

The conclusion does not depend on selecting IAR or AP4C1. A retentional-coherence requirement is compatible with multiple grammar/record-law implementations, and the continuing plurality of retentional, extensional, and hybrid accounts prevents elevating one formal coding architecture into a phenomenological theorem.

## Conclusion

Phenomenology gives **partial, independent motivation for endogenous retentional coherence**, but it does not supply B1–B4 and hence does not warrant AP4C1's arbitrary-one-witness error model, three-witness code, or decoder.

The honest ledger is now:

\[
\text{core identity} + \text{phenomenological retention}
\not\models
\text{AP4C1 stability/error law}.
\]

AP4C1 remains a transparent conditional formal law. The remaining route to a stronger status requires either a new independently argued stability/error principle or a physical theory that supplies an explicit noncircular record mechanism; neither may be smuggled in as “retention” or “self-presentation.”
