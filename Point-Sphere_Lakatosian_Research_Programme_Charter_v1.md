# Point–Sphere Lakatosian Research Programme Charter v1
## Adopted operating architecture: protected hard core, revisable belt, external rivals

## Adoption and scope

The programme now adopts the hard-core/belt architecture reconstructed in `Point-Sphere_Hard-Core_Lineage_Contextual_Adjudication_v1.md`. This is an organising decision about how research proceeds. It does **not** recast the protected hard core as a conclusion of the public conditional grounding arguments, and it does not exempt belt claims from formal, empirical, historical, or ethical criticism.

## 1. Protected hard core

For internal programme direction, the following are protected working/faith commitments:

1. an ultimate ground that is simple, complete, fully aware, good, unchanging, and non-temporal;
2. real but asymmetrically dependent creation rather than mere appearance; and
3. temporal/spatial order as internal to the created domain.

“Creation under computational description” is a permitted **interpretive vocabulary**, not an additional ontological theorem. It may not import an external physical computer, Bostrom-style observer ratios, a quantum interpretation, or a realization rule by definition.

The hard core is stated, cited, and not re-litigated in ordinary belt work. In public-facing work it remains clearly marked as a protected commitment rather than a premise-free proof.

## 2. Protective belt

The belt contains proposals required to connect the hard core to an explanatory and testable research programme:

Full C-Nec-P plenitude is **deferred**, not a hard-core claim. It can re-enter the belt only with a formal admissibility/realization/measure proposal that is not fitted after the fact.

## 3. Negative heuristic

The programme must not:

- promote a hard-core commitment into a public deduction by semantic relabelling;
- protect belt claims by changing thresholds, measures, candidate boundaries, or interpretations after an adverse result;
- use geometry, scripture, simulation language, historical resonance, or quantum vocabulary as a substitute for a bridge;
- discard external rivals merely because they conflict with the hard core; or
- call failure evidence for mystery, confirmation, or hidden success.

## 4. Positive heuristic

Every belt document must state:

1. the protected commitments it assumes;
2. the single belt question it addresses;
3. its formal objects, data/model inputs, and independently fixed comparator;
4. relevant external rivals and a discriminating criterion;
5. a success condition, a failure condition, and what gets revised if failure occurs; and
6. which claims remain out of scope.

## 5. Appraisal: progressive versus degenerative work

A belt development is **progressive** only if it does at least one of the following without ad hoc rescue:

- derives a new constraint from already declared commitments;
- reduces primitive/selection freedom while preserving or improving empirical fit;
- recovers a hidden synthetic answer under locked rules;
- distinguishes the programme from a stated rival on data or a formal theorem; or
- repairs a prior failure by a separately frozen architecture and survives independent validation.

It is **degenerative** if it repeatedly absorbs failures through retrospective redefinition, adds unconstrained selectors or measures, treats every rival result as a partial confirmation, or produces no new formal/empirical constraint.

## 6. Core review policy

Ordinary belt failure revises the belt, not the protected core. A deliberate hard-core review is warranted only by a demonstrated formal inconsistency internal to the commitments, a sustained programme-level degeneracy judgment, or a decision by the holders of the commitment to reopen it. External rivals remain documented regardless.

## 7. Immediate operating order

1. B-C: execute the frozen Tier-0 robustness/count-recovery grid under unchanged rules.
2. B-F: repair the \(\lambda\phi^4_2\) mass-tuning estimator using pooled observables and chain-level replication, then independently validate it.
3. B-R: formalize a minimal comparison language for C-Nec-D/C-Nec-O/C-Cont before attempting measure claims.
4. B-M: undertake measure work only when B-R supplies a specific admissible structure and invariance candidate.
5. B-P and real-data work remain downstream of B-C.

This ordering respects both topological dependency and feasibility. It protects the hard core without granting the belt immunity from failure.
