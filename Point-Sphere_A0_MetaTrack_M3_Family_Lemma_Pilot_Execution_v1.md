# Part I — Pilot S: Internal semantic classification

## 1. Frozen class and premises

Let \(C\in K_{\rm sem}^{\rm int}\). By the frozen scope, it has:

\[
C=\langle L,\mathcal M,\varphi,\tau\rangle,
\qquad
\mathcal M=\langle D,I\rangle,
\tag{S-Class}
\]

where \(L\) is ordinary first-order logic under standard Tarskian semantics, \(\varphi(x)\) is parameter-free, and \(\tau\) is a type formula or finite type partition. The class excludes a domain-generating semantics, a non-model-theoretic actualization base, a primitive actuality rule with independently stated application conditions, and a public selector of the intended domain.

The proof assumes exactly the frozen premises:

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
S_1 &: \mathsf{Adm}_{C}(a)\iff\mathcal M\vDash\varphi(a)\land\tau(a)=\mathsf{Mode};\\
S_2 &: \mathsf{Act}_{C}(a)\text{ is claimed to follow from those same in-model satisfaction facts alone};\\
S_3 &: \mathsf{NoExternalDomainBase}(C);\\

\end{array}
\tag{S-Premises}
\]

No premise is strengthened to say that all semantic theories fail, that all models have rivals, or that formal classification is valueless.

## 2. Semantic locality lemma

For a standard first-order structure \(\mathcal M=\langle D,I\rangle\), the satisfaction relation is recursively defined relative to \(D\), its interpretation, and assignments ranging over \(D\). Hence:

\[
\mathcal M,s\vDash\varphi(x)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
s(x)\in D.
\tag{Locality-1}
\]

Likewise, when \(a\in D\) is evaluated:

\[
\mathcal M\vDash\varphi(a)
\]

classifies \(a\) only relative to the already supplied pair \(\langle D,I\rangle\). It does not, merely as a satisfaction fact, state that \(D\) is the actual candidate field rather than a relevant rival domain or interpretation.

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal M\vDash\varphi(a)
\not\Rightarrow_{\,\text{Tarski alone}}
\mathsf{ActualDomain}(D).
}
\tag{Locality-2}
\]

This is not an attack on truth in a model. It is the distinction between:

\[
\text{classification within a supplied structure}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\text{selection of that structure as actuality-bearing}.
\tag{Distinction}
\]

---

## 3. Required rival-domain audit

The preregistration requires a distinction among domain extension, domain replacement, and mere renaming.

### 3.1 Domain extension

Consider the illustrative standard first-order case:

\[
L=\{P\},
\qquad
\varphi(x):=P(x),
\qquad
\tau(x):=\mathsf{Mode}.
\]

Let:

\[
D=\{a\},
\qquad
P^{\mathcal M}=\{a\},
\]

and let \(\mathcal M^{+}\) have:

\[
D^{+}=\{a,b\},
qquad
P^{\mathcal M^{+}}=\{a,b\}.
\]

Then the original satisfaction result remains:

\[
\mathcal M\vDash P(a),
\qquad
\mathcal M^{+}\vDash P(a),
\tag{Extension}
\]

while \(\mathcal M^{+}\) supplies an additional classified candidate \(b\). This is an illustration of domain/inventory underdetermination; it is **not** a claim that every formula is preserved under every arbitrary domain extension.

### 3.2 Domain replacement

A domain replacement is a distinct interpretation \(\mathcal M'=\langle D',I'\rangle\) proposed as the relevant candidate field, not merely a relabeling of \(D\). Whether it preserves a given \(\varphi\)-verdict depends on the fixed interpretation and formula. The semantic-locality point does not require universal preservation: if a criterion claims that in-model facts alone decide cross-domain actuality, it needs a rule deciding why its own \(D\), rather than such a relevant \(D'\), is actuality-bearing.

Under \(S_3\), no public external base or semantic rule supplies that selection. Under the scope exclusion of \(K_{\rm sem}^{\rm int}\), neither a domain-generating semantics nor an independently applicable actuality rule may be invoked silently.

### 3.3 Mere renaming or isomorphism

If \(f:\mathcal M\cong\mathcal N\) is only a structure-preserving renaming, then standard isomorphism invariance yields the corresponding truth preservation:

\[
\mathcal M\vDash\varphi(a)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathcal N\vDash\varphi(f(a)).
\tag{Rename}
\]

This is not itself a rival-inventory objection. It is a profile-preserving re-encoding, and it should be treated as equivalent rather than as a second ontology. It shows only that bare carrier labels cannot supply an actuality selector.

## 4. Proof of the frozen semantic lemma

Assume \(C\in K_{\rm sem}^{\rm int}\) and \(S_1\)--\(S_5\).

By \(S_1\), admission is determined by:

\[
\mathcal M\vDash\varphi(a)
\land
\tau(a)=\mathsf{Mode}.
\tag{S1-use}
\]

By \((\mathrm{Locality\text{-}1})\), these are facts whose semantic evaluation presupposes the supplied domain \(D\). By \((\mathrm{Locality\text{-}2})\), they do not themselves select \(D\) as the actual candidate field.

By \(S_2\), the claimed actuality conclusion has no source other than those in-model facts. By \(S_4\), however, the conclusion claims actual finite-mode membership rather than merely relative classification inside \(\mathcal M\). That cross-domain claim requires either:

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\mathrm{(i)} & \text{a public, noninventory basis selecting \(D\) or its intended interpretation; or}\\
\mathrm{(ii)} & \text{a domain-generating / independently applicable actuality rule.}
\end{array}
\tag{Required-Bridge}
\]

Option \((\mathrm{ii})\) leaves \(K_{\rm sem}^{\rm int}\) by its frozen scope. Option \((\mathrm{i})\) is denied by \(S_3\). Consequently the claimed cross-domain transition cannot be supplied by the stated resources.

Relative to the fixed A0 meanings, the result is:

\[
\neg\mathsf{NoInventoryPresupposition}(C)
\ \lor\ 
\neg\mathsf{PubliclyAdjudicable}(C)
	ag{S-Fail}
\]

for the claimed cross-domain actuality verdict. Thus:

\[
\mathsf{BridgeFail}(C;R_{\rm sem}),
\qquad
R_{\rm sem}=\mathsf{NoActualDomainBridge}.
\tag{S-Conclusion}
\]

The role of \(S_5\) is not to assume the conclusion away. It fixes the target standard: the criterion purports to provide the A0-eligible public admission interface. The derivation shows that the stipulated internal-only route does not meet that target for its cross-domain actuality claim.

Therefore the preregistered conditional is established:

\[
\boxed{

## 8. Adversarial counterexample to the frozen modal lemma

The direct proof route stated in the preregistration would be valid only if the unique selected world were required to belong to the symmetric pair supplied by \(M_4\). But \(M_4\) requires only that some distinct profile-equivalent pair exists somewhere in \(W\). It does not require either member of that pair to satisfy \(\Sigma\).

The following finite criterion remains inside the frozen class and satisfies every premise. Let:

\[
W=\{w,v,u\},
\qquad
w\ne v,
\qquad
w\equiv_{\Pi}v,
\qquad
u\not\equiv_{\Pi}w.
\tag{CE-Worlds}
\]

Let all declared modal and profile data be invariant under the transposition of \(w\) and \(v\), while \(u\) has a distinct declared \(\Pi\)-profile. Define the nonindexical selector solely by that declared profile:

\[
\Sigma(z)
\iff
z\equiv_{\Pi}u.
\tag{CE-Selector}
\]

Then:

\[
\Sigma(u),
\qquad
\neg\Sigma(w),
\qquad
\neg\Sigma(v).
\tag{CE-Values}
\]

The fixed premises are all satisfied:

\[
\begin{array}{rcl}
M_1&:&\text{the criterion claims A0 eligibility;}\\
M_2&:&\exists!z\in W\,\Sigma(z),\text{ namely }u;\\
M_3&:&x\equiv_{\Pi}y\Rightarrow[\Sigma(x)\leftrightarrow\Sigma(y)];\\
M_4&:&w\ne v\land w\equiv_{\Pi}v;\\
M_5&:&\Sigma\text{ uses only the declared }\Pi\text{-profile, with no extra selector.}
\end{array}
\tag{CE-Premises}
\]

The symmetric pair is not arbitrarily distinguished: it is jointly unselected. The unique selected world is selected by its different declared profile. Therefore neither the frozen invariance condition nor the stated no-extra-selector condition is violated:

\[
\mathsf{ProfileInvariant}(C)
\land
\mathsf{NoSelectorResidue}(C).
\tag{CE-NoFail}
\]

Accordingly:

\[
\neg\mathsf{BridgeFail}(C;R_{\rm mod}).
\tag{CE-Conclusion}
\]

This is a counterexample satisfying every fixed premise while falsifying the preregistered conclusion. Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\mathsf{Outcome}(\Lambda_{\rm mod}^{\rm sym})
=
\mathsf{RefutedPilot}.
}
\tag{M-Outcome}
\]

The defect is narrow and cannot be repaired inside M3: \(M_4\) does not state that the symmetric pair exhausts \(W\), contains the selected world, or is the only profile class. Adding any of those conditions would revise the preregistration and would require a fresh protocol.
