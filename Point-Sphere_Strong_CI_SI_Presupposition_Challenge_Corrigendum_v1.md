# Strong CI Adjudication — SI Presupposition-Challenge Corrigendum v1
## Reframing the self-intelligibility deliverable

## Correction

The SI dossier was right to reject terminal labels as explanations. Its phrase “public non-circular semantics of self-intelligibility” was too narrow and invited an avoidable regress objection.

A candidate need not satisfy a further semantic criterion as though self-intelligibility were an ordinary predicate waiting for external certification. Ultimate closure is a metaphysical issue. The required deliverable is instead a **publicly examinable metaphysical argument** that a purported further contrastive question either rests on a false distinction or has already been answered by the candidate’s stated nature.

“Public” here means assessable by shared argument, declared premises, and rival challenge. It does not mean that the ultimate is reducible to a formal semantics or that a new ontological criterion must ground the ultimate.

## 1. Why the criterion regress objection has force

If every explanation had to be certified by a further independent criterion, then that criterion would itself demand certification. A chain of criteria would either regress, become circular, or stop arbitrarily.

Strong CI should not be formulated as requiring an external explanatory object beyond every ultimate nature. It requires that a candidate distinguish genuine explanatory closure from a merely renamed brute fact.

The distinction is made by argument about the alleged contrast, not by a separate ontological item called “the criterion of self-intelligibility.”

## 2. Corrected self-intelligibility pass condition

Let \(Q(U)\) be a proposed ultimate contrastive question, such as:

\[
Q(U):
\quad
\text{“Why does \(U\) exist rather than fail to exist?”}
\]

or:

\[
Q(U,D):
\quad
\text{“Why does this exact derivative domain \(D\) obtain rather than another?”}
\]

A candidate may answer that \(Q\) is ill-posed only if it supplies a **presupposition challenge**:

\[
\mathsf{PC}(F,Q) :=
\exists d\,[
\mathsf{Presupposes}(Q,d)
\land
\mathsf{Derives}_F(\neg d)
\land
\mathsf{NonCircular}_F(\mathsf{Derives}_F)
\land
\mathsf{NoReappearance}(F,Q,d)
].
\tag{PC}
\]

The four conditions are:

1. **Locate the presupposition.** Identify the real distinction, alternative space, component relation, or contingent fact assumed by the question.
2. **Derive its absence or resolve it.** Show from independently stated candidate premises that the presupposition does not obtain, or that the candidate already supplies the relevant explanation.
3. **Avoid circularity.** Do not use “\(U\) is self-intelligible” as the premise that proves the question is illegitimate.
4. **No reappearance.** Show that the same unexplained exactness is not merely relocated one level up—for example, from “why this output?” to “why this essence/law/identity?”

This is a metaphysical dialectical test, not a semantic predicate imposed on the ultimate.

## 3. Corrected candidate applications

| Candidate | Legitimate presupposition challenge | What remains to be shown |
|---|---|---|
| Essence–existence identity / simple ground | The question “why is existence added to this essence?” presupposes a real essence/existence distinction | Coherent account of the identity; why the identity has existential force rather than merely conceptual status; no reappearance at “why this identity?” |
| Neoplatonic One/Good | “Why does the Good have an additional external productive act?” may presuppose an act/source distinction rejected by the candidate | BGD: why goodness itself entails diffusion; why diffusion gives this exact hierarchy rather than merely some derivative relation |
| Necessary totality | “Why is an external item added to the totality?” may be malformed | The ordinary question “why this exact internal totality rather than another?” remains unless its determinate profile is derived |
| Nondual aware ground | “Which external selector chooses aspects?” may presuppose an external selection relation rejected by the candidate | The internal question “why these all-and-only genuine contents?” remains until public/domain semantics is supplied |
| Principled infinitism | “What external cause begins the series?” may presuppose a beginning or external cause | Why this exact global law/pattern is self-intelligible rather than a primitive totality |

“No outside” or “no alternative space” is not enough by itself. Contrastive alternatives can be stated internally or modally; the candidate must show why the relevant alternative space is incoherent under its own independently specified ontology.

## 4. Corrected result

The SI dossier’s valid core conclusion is retained in revised form:

\[
\boxed{
\mathsf{SCI}
\Rightarrow
\text{every candidate owes a non-brute terminal-closure argument.}
}
\]

The corrected non-result is:

\[
\boxed{
\mathsf{SCI}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{a neutral external criterion that mechanically certifies a terminal closure.}
}
\]

A candidate passes only conditionally by satisfying \(\mathsf{PC}(F,Q)\) for the relevant ultimate and exact-output questions, not by a terminal label.

## 5. Consequence for the research programme

The next work is not a search for another generic criterion. It is candidate-specific presupposition analysis:

1. test essence–existence identity against the reappearance condition;
2. test BGD and exact hierarchy for necessary emanation;
3. test the all-and-only genuine-content question for nondual identity;
4. test exact-totality claims for necessary totalities, structures, and infinitist laws.

If no candidate can meet the presupposition challenge without reappearance, then strongest CI has no supplied satisfier in the audited space. Only then would revising it to a least-residue standard become a live methodological option.

## Nonclaims

This corrigendum does not establish any candidate’s self-intelligibility, declare the ultimate beyond rational assessment, accept brute facts, or conclude that strongest CI is unsatisfiable. It corrects the type of argument required to address the deepest terminal burden.
