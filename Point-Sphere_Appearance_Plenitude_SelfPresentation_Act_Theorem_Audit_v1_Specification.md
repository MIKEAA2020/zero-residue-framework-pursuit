# AP3P2L Self-Presentation Act-Theorem Audit v1
## Can act-structure derive intrinsic flow, component boundaries, and partial grammar adequacy?

## Candidate theorem

The proposed route says that finite self-presentation is an act rather than a static state. It should be tested by independently characterizing act-structure before treating it as a successor/flow law.

The intended conclusion must be factored:
\[
\mathsf{FiniteSelfPresentation}(m)
\Rightarrow
\mathsf{TemporalAct}(m)
\Rightarrow
\mathsf{FlowGrammar}(m).
\]

The first arrow is the load-bearing **finite temporalization bridge**. The second requires a representation theorem linking retention/protention to a concrete successor grammar.

## Act ambiguity

“Act” has two non-equivalent readings:

1. **Metaphysical actuality / eternal act:** compatible with atemporal self-knowledge. It does not entail succession.
2. **Phenomenological temporally articulated act:** its ongoing experience includes retention, primal presence, and protention. It supports a temporal field, but not a particular graph, successor type, or bi-infinite order.

The ground/mode contrast itself shows that act alone does not imply temporal flow: the proposal calls ground self-knowledge atemporal while calling mode self-presence temporal. The additional claim must be:
\[
\mathsf{FiniteDetermination}
+\mathsf{SelfPresentationAct}
\Rightarrow
\mathsf{TemporalAct}.
\]

## Independent resources and limits

- The protected core identifies mode/aspect/content of self-knowledge and supplies nonrepresentational actuality conditionally; it does not formally assert that every finite mode is a temporal act.
- Husserlian phenomenology supports retention/impression/protention as a unified, largely passive structure of ongoing intentional experience. It supplies a conditional resource for finite temporal phenomenality, not an entailment of the aware-ground package, bi-infinite flow, or a particular successor graph.
- Henryan auto-affection supports immanent self-appearing but is tied to radical passivity; it cannot simply be equated with a self-initiated temporally sequential act.

## Tests

### Flow

Even granting a temporal act, distinguish:
\[
\text{temporal field/coherence}
\not\Rightarrow
\mathbb Z\text{-ordered nonterminal successor flow},
\]
\[
\text{retention/protention}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{IAR P/Q alternation, phase-origin gauge, or a unique continuation relation}.
\]
AP3O/AP3P1 already establish these gaps.

### Boundaries

An act can support a candidate internal integration condition, but “where self-affection stops” presupposes a prior individuation of the act and excludes neither nested nor overlapping self-presenting units. A boundary theorem requires an independently stated act-integration/closure relation.

### Partial adequacy and enrichments

An act theorem could yield necessary act-determined grammar features. It cannot infer:
\[
\neg\operatorname{RequiredByEveryAct}(x)
\Rightarrow
\neg\operatorname{GenuineContent}(x).
\]
Optional genuine act-contents remain possible. It can require explicit representation of whatever the theorem shows necessary; it cannot turn nonnecessity into mere notation.

## Expiry condition

The act theorem succeeds only if it supplies both, noncircularly:

1. an independently warranted finite temporalization bridge; and
2. a mapping from act structure to formal flow/integration relations that remains defensible if it excludes IAR.

Otherwise “act” either means atemporal actuality (no flow result) or includes temporal succession by definition (circular flow result).
