# Point–Sphere Focused Literature Audit v1
## Orientation sources for the foundation, creation, and consciousness comparison modules

## Scope and use rule

This is a targeted orientation audit, not a claim of exhaustive scholarship and not an appeal to authority in place of argument. Each source is used only for the limited purpose stated. A source may show that a distinction, doctrine, or objection is live in the literature; it does not validate the Point–Sphere conditional premises.

The audit services the first feasible post-foundation tasks:

- formal grounding/essence/necessity vocabulary;
- creation, simplicity, and modal-cost comparison;
- consciousness and intrinsic-nature rival comparison; and
- historical restraint concerning Leibniz.

## 2. Consequences for the foundation stack

### 2.1 Grounding cannot be used with unpriced ambiguity

The grounding literature reinforces the master’s decision to distinguish full from partial grounding and to list closure/substitution premises. If a theorem needs a sole fact to be a **complete** ground of every fact in a domain, it must say what makes a set a full ground and how substitution works. Well-foundedness is not a harmless notation convention; it excludes regressive models.

This supports the stress-test repair but does not validate its governing norms. In particular:

- globality still needs a declared fact domain and closure principle;
- uniqueness still needs a single-foundation premise;
- transfer from facts to entities still needs bearer semantics; and
- strong aseity still needs MI\*, EL\*, EC\*, ECmp\*, and RIG.

### 2.2 Essence and necessity must not be conflated

Classical divine-simplicity traditions supply a family of proposals in which essence and existence are connected very tightly. They show that the Point–Sphere concern is philosophically recognisable, not that its conditional theorem is automatic. The literature also foregrounds exactly the costs already registered here: the coherence of concrete beings with property/attribute identity, divine freedom, and modal consequences for creatures.

The framework must therefore continue to use **strong aseity** in its narrow formal sense—\(\mathcal E_G\mathrel{\mathsf{FG}}\Box_R\operatorname{Ex}(G)\), conditional on the stated premises—rather than silently importing every doctrine of classical divine simplicity.

## 3. Consequences for the creation comparison

The literature audit gives three guardrails.

1. **Necessary existence is underdetermining.** A necessary being can be abstract, impersonal, or theistic; its necessity alone does not choose a creation architecture.
2. **Simplicity and creation are a real pressure point.** The C-Nec/C-Cont distinction tracks a recognisable philosophical problem. A necessary productive rule creates a modal-cost issue; a contingent selector creates a simplicity/selection issue. Neither can be settled by a verbal appeal to “expression.”
3. **Leibniz is a comparison case, not a shortcut.** His view has created, dependent, active monads. It therefore cannot be responsibly imported as evidence that finite centres are simply divine self-perspectives, or that dependence abolishes creaturely distinction.

The correct next scholarly task is a focused primary-and-secondary reading on divine simplicity, concurrentism, modal collapse, and creation/emanation. It should test whether specific proposed C-Nec or C-Cont models have an internally consistent semantics—not merely list historical labels.

---

## 4. Consequences for the intrinsic-nature comparison

The sources confirm that the comparison map is appropriately plural.

- Chalmers provides a canonical articulation of the hard problem, but his own response is non-reductive rather than a proof of theism or cosmopsychism.
- Frankish supplies a clear version of the illusionist alternative; its task is explanation of the appearance of phenomenality, not a simple denial that reports occur.
- Russellian monism is a live direct alternative: it shares the structural/intrinsic distinction while withholding identification of intrinsic nature with one pure awareness.
- Panpsychism and cosmopsychism make clear why combination and decombination are not interchangeable labels. A global consciousness thesis does not automatically give a theory of finite subjects.

The framework’s modest result survives intact: a purely structural theory needs an explicit phenomenal bridge. The question of which bridge is best remains comparative and abductive.

## 5. Reading sequence and closure conditions

### Topological reading sequence

1. **Grounding vocabulary:** Bliss & Trogdon; then selected work on full grounding, essence, and well-foundedness.
2. **Creation/simplicity:** divine simplicity and necessary-beings material; then primary texts and contemporary work on modal collapse, divine freedom, and concurrence.
3. **Consciousness rivals:** Chalmers and Frankish; then Russellian monism and panpsychism/cosmopsychism literature.
4. **Historical comparison:** Leibniz’s metaphysics and causation, with primary texts kept distinct from modern reconstructions.

### Closure condition for this audit

This v1 audit is complete as an orientation map. It will be superseded only by a **claim-indexed** literature review that, for each master premise, records: (a) the exact claim; (b) primary source support or objection; (c) the interpretation adopted; (d) unresolved controversy; and (e) whether the source changes the framework’s status label. Until then, these sources constrain terminology and prevent false novelty claims; they do not alter the conditional ledger.
