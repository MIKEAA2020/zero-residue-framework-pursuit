# Realization-Rule Minimal Model Families v1
## R1b; frozen formal diagnostic under the active hard-core + belt programme

## Scope box

**Hard core assumed, not re-argued:** one simple, complete, fully aware, good, unchanging ultimate ground; real dependent creation.

**Belt question:** what additional formal structure is indispensable for C-Nec-D, C-Nec-O, or C-Cont to fix a created totality?

**Scope:** finite toy families over a common state space plus one non-compact measure counterexample. The models diagnose formal costs; they do not derive a real created order, prove a metaphysical rule, or decide quantum probabilities.

## Common finite domain

Let
\[
\Omega=\{0,1,2,3\}.
\]
It represents four complete toy totalities. A common symbolic ground parameter \(E\) is held fixed in every family. The exercise deliberately does not encode the hard core as a finite object.

## Frozen families

### D — determinate necessary expression

\[
\mathcal R_D(E)=\{f(E)\}.
\]
The implementation fixes a displayed representative \(f(E)=0\) only to make the toy model executable. It must record that the map \(f\), rather than the numeral 0, is the indispensable exact-totality bridge. Renaming or permuting \(\Omega\) must expose whether a supposedly canonical output relies on an unacknowledged labeling convention.

### O — orbit-restricted expression

Let the finite cyclic group \(C_4\) act transitively on \(\Omega\) by \(g_k\cdot x=(x+k)\bmod4\):
\[
\mathcal R_O(E)=C_4\!\cdot\!0=\Omega.
\]

Compute the unique finite-group-invariant uniform probability. Then evaluate two readings:

1. **Gauge reading:** all orbit members are descriptions of one physical totality, so the quotient has one member and O reduces to D-like uniqueness.
2. **Substantive reading:** orbit members are distinct totalities, so the orbit gives genuine plurality; this requires an independent physical/metaphysical distinctness criterion.

### C — contingent/non-necessitated selector

\[
\mathcal R_C(E,q)=\{q\},\qquad q\in\Omega.
\]

Hold \(E\) fixed and vary \(q\). The model must show that \(q\) is an indispensable additional parameter. It does not decide whether an actual selector can be non-arbitrary and non-collapsing; that is the next belt debt.

### Non-compact warning model

Let \(\mathbb Z\) act on itself by translation. Establish that no countably additive normalized translation-invariant probability gives equal weight to every integer: equal point mass is either zero, giving total mass zero, or positive, giving divergent total mass. This prevents the finite \(C_4\) uniform measure from being misreported as a general orbit-measure solution.

## Required outputs and failure interpretation

Report realized-set sizes, invariant measure where it exists, quotient size under the gauge reading, selector variation, and the non-compact normalisation result. A coding or logical failure is retained. A successful toy diagnostic shows only the listed formal costs:

- D needs a derivation/bridge \(f\);
- O needs a substantive non-gauge orbit plus a normalisable relevant invariant measure;
- C needs an extra selector \(q\) and a non-arbitrariness account.
