# Modal Monism — Intrinsic Limitation Test v1
## Application of the preregistered IML formulation

## Scope

This document applies, without alteration, the sole formulation in:

`Point-Sphere_Modal_Monism_Intrinsic_Limitation_Test_Protocol_v1.md`

\[
\mathsf{IML}_{G}(m)
\iff
\exists\lambda\in\mathcal L_G[
\mathsf{Admissible}_G(\lambda)
\land m\cong m_\lambda
\land\mathsf{NonTrivial}(\lambda)
\land\mathsf{Finite}(\lambda)
].
\]

It does not prove or disprove modal monism as such. It tests whether this one intrinsic partial-presentation account closes the theory-of-limitation gap.

## 1. What the formulation genuinely improves

The IML proposal makes three previously implicit moves visible:

\[
\begin{array}{lll}
\mathcal I_G &: & \text{a determinate intelligible interface of the ground};\\
\lambda &: & \text{an alleged intrinsic finite partial-presentation map};\\
m_\lambda &: & \text{a finite structured retained profile.}
\end{array}
\]

It also distinguishes an external limiter from an intrinsic articulation and requires a finite profile, an identity relation, an admissibility condition, an error condition, and reformulation control. These are genuine gains over the slogan:

\[
\text{“the One limits itself.”}
\]

But making a missing bridge visible is not yet crossing it.

## 2. ML0 — intelligibility of \(\mathcal I_G\) and \(\lambda\)

The initial expression is mathematically intelligible:

\[
\lambda:\mathcal I_G\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal I_\lambda.
\]

Its metaphysical interpretation remains three-way ambiguous.

\[
\begin{array}{lll}
A &: & \mathcal I_G\text{ contains real differentiated content intrinsic to }G;\\
B &: & \mathcal I_G\text{ is only a conceptual interface supplied by a finite knower};\\
C &: & \mathcal I_G\text{ is determined only through the already existing finite modes.}
\end{array}
\]

Under \(A\), the proposal owes an account of how genuinely differentiated intrinsic content is compatible with the stipulated simplicity of \(G\). Under \(B\), the quotient maps describe finite cognition, not an ontological finite mode. Under \(C\), the theory defines limitations through the modes it was meant to generate.

Therefore:

\[
\mathsf{MapWellFormed}_{\rm math}
\not\Rightarrow
\mathsf{OntologicalLimitation}_{G}(\lambda).
\]

**Result:** ML0 is unresolved. The formula does not yet determine whether \(\lambda\) is ontological rather than conceptual or circular.

## 3. ML1 — internal generation of \(\mathcal L_G\)

The natural candidate rule is:

\[
\mathcal L_G=
\{\lambda:\mathcal I_G\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal I_\lambda
\mid
\mathsf{Finite}(\mathcal I_\lambda)
\land\mathsf{Admissible}_G(\lambda)
}.
\]

This does not generate \(\mathcal L_G\) until \(\mathsf{Admissible}_G\) is given independent content. Three attempted readings fail to close that gap:

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\text{All finite quotients} &: \text{includes arbitrary, incompatible, or merely formal coarse-grainings};\\
\text{All coherent quotients} &: \text{requires a prior coherence/compossibility semantics and its actual basis};\\
\text{The ground's own admissible quotients} &: \text{relabels the desired modal roster.}
\end{array}
\]

A closure rule must say why a particular quotient preserves the kind of intelligible determination relevant to finite actuality rather than merely generating another formal interface.

**Result:** no non-roster \(\mathsf{Admissible}_G\) rule has been recovered. ML1 remains open.

## 4. ML2 and ML3 — external limitation and simplicity

The proposal correctly avoids positing a second being that physically constrains \(G\). But an abstract quotient map has not thereby become an intrinsic ontological operation.

If \(\lambda\) is real enough to constitute a finite mode, then one of the following must be explained:

\[
\begin{array}{lll}
D_1 &: & \lambda\text{ is a real intrinsic differentiation of }G;\\
D_2 &: & \lambda\text{ is an extrinsic/conceptual presentation of an undifferentiated }G;\\
D_3 &: & \lambda\text{ is actual only by a further actualizing principle.}
\end{array}
\]

\(D_1\) does not automatically contradict every theory of simplicity: a defender may distinguish composition from virtual, modal, or formally distinct articulation. But IML has not supplied such a theory. It cannot simply assert:

\[
\mathsf{DifferentiatedIn}(G)
\land
\mathsf{Simple}(G)
\]

without an account of the relevant distinction.

\(D_2\) preserves a very strong simplicity at the cost of making limitation a fact about descriptions or perspectives. It then does not establish real finite plurality.

\(D_3\) raises the further question IML was meant to answer: what actualizes this \(\lambda\), and why this actualized articulation rather than another?

**Result:** IML avoids an explicit external limiter but has not established an intrinsic ontological limiter compatible with simplicity. ML2 and ML3 are unresolved.

## 5. ML4 — plurality and identity

A workable profile identity convention can be stated:

\[
m_\lambda\simeq m_\mu
\iff
\rho_\lambda\cong_{\Pi}\rho_\mu,
\]

for a declared profile-conservative isomorphism \(\cong_{\Pi}\). This can prevent differences of name or merely redundant encoding from multiplying modes.

But this only supplies a conditional identity rule. It leaves two questions open:

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\text{Why are the retained profiles }\rho_\lambda\text{ real modal profiles rather than formal quotients?}\\
&\text{What makes two non-isomorphic profiles distinct actual modes rather than distinct descriptions?}
\end{aligned}
\]

The equal-profile case can be handled by quotienting; the existence and actual modehood of the quotient classes cannot.

**Result:** ML4 receives a partial formal resource but no actual plurality bridge.

## 6. ML5 — modal availability is not actuality

This is the decisive failure of the present formulation.

From:

\[
\lambda\in\mathcal L_G
\]

one can derive at most a kind of admissible modal availability:

\[
\mathsf{Admissible}_G(\lambda)
\Rightarrow
\mathsf{ModalCandidate}(m_\lambda).
\]

It does not follow that:

\[
\mathsf{ActualFiniteMode}(m_\lambda).
\]

There are only three evident ways to add that conclusion:

\[
\begin{array}{lll}
A_1 &: & \text{one or some \(\lambda\)'s are selected as actual};\\
A_2 &: & \text{all admissible \(\lambda\)'s are actual};\\
A_3 &: & \text{actuality of \(m_\lambda\) is primitive.}
\end{array}
\]

\(A_1\) requires the very selector IML was meant to avoid.

\(A_2\) is a plenitude postulate. It removes selection among admissible maps but does not derive either the admissible domain or its actuality:

\[
\forall\lambda\in\mathcal L_G\,\mathsf{Actual}(m_\lambda)
\]

is new content, not a consequence of surjectivity or simplicity.

\(A_3\) moves the problem to primitive actuality and requires independent public application and error conditions.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\mathsf{IML}_{G}(m)
\not\Rightarrow
\mathsf{ActualFiniteMode}(m).
}
\]

**Result:** ML5 fails for this formulation. This is a targeted actuality-interface failure, not a disproof of modal monism.

## 7. ML6–ML8 — type, invariance, and an extra candidate

### Type boundary

A quotient or partial presentation may be:

\[
\text{a concept},\quad
\text{a property},\quad
\text{a perspective},\quad
\text{a possible profile},\quad
\text{or a genuine finite mode}.
\]

IML offers no independent rule deciding among these. Dependence on \(G\) cannot do the work because concepts, properties, and modes can all be described as dependent in a monist ontology.

### Reformulation invariance

The proposed relation \(\rho_\lambda\cong_\Pi\rho_\mu\) is useful only after \(\Pi\) is independently fixed. Changing the interface \(\mathcal I_G\), choosing different quotient coordinates, or reifying relations can alter the quotient lattice. No invariant decoder or equivalence theorem has been supplied across such presentations.

### Extra candidate

A proposed \(m_\nu\) is admitted exactly when \(\nu\in\mathcal L_G\) and \(\mathsf{Admissible}_G(\nu)\). Since that predicate is unresolved, the rule either leaves the case undecided or admits it by stipulation.

**Result:** ML6–ML8 remain open; the formulation has not met the A0-level type, invariance, or extra-candidate burdens.

## 8. ML9–ML10 — phenomenal and physical bridges

No phenomenal realization law follows from IML:

\[
\mathsf{IML}_{G}(m)
\not\Rightarrow
\mathsf{CreaturelyExperience}(m).
\]

A separate psychophysical selector would be required, with its own subject boundary, profile, determinacy, and anti-zombie burdens.

Likewise, no quantum-gravity or physical recovery theory follows:

\[
\mathsf{IML}_{G}(m)
\not\Rightarrow
\mathsf{EmergentTime}
\land\mathsf{EmergentSpace}
\land\mathsf{EmergentLocality}
\land\mathsf{EffectiveCausation}.
\]

The WDW/WKB and holographic results in the attached evolving chat are at most partial physics templates under their own assumptions; they are not derived from modal limitation or from any theological premise.

**Result:** ML9 and ML10 are outside the reach of IML as stated.

## 9. Rival audit

| Rival | What it makes explicit | IML comparison result |
|---|---|---|
| External limiter \(R_{\rm ext}\) | a distinct principle provides restriction | IML avoids this only formally; it has not yet shown that \(\lambda\) is intrinsically ontological. |
| Creator–creature concurrence \(R_{\rm conc}\) | finite beings are genuinely distinct but dependent | IML does not refute it; it relocates, but does not solve, the admission/actuality problem. |
| Appearance-only monism \(R_{\rm abs}\) | finite plurality is non-ontological appearance | IML has not yet established more than this, because \(\lambda\) may remain conceptual/presentational. |
| Bare plenitude \(R_{\rm plen}\) | all eligible profiles obtain | IML needs an additional plenitude postulate to take this route. |
| Modal availability \(R_{\rm mod}\) | admissible possibilities need not be actual | This is the present IML result unless an additional actualization rule is added. |

## 10. Fixed-outcome adjudication

The preregistered pass requires ML0–ML8 to be supplied noncircularly. They are not.

A universal structural failure of modal monism has also not been shown: the tests leave open, among other possibilities, a non-composite account of intrinsic distinction, a public primitive-actuality rule, or an independently defended actualization/plenitude principle.

Therefore the required result is:

\[
\boxed{
\mathsf{IntrinsicLimitationResult}_{\rm IML}
=
\text{Inconclusive, with a targeted ML5 actuality-interface failure.}
}
\]

More explicitly:

\[
\mathsf{IML}_{G}
\text{ provides a formal modal-candidate generator but no noncircular actual finite-mode admission rule.}
\]

## 11. Expiry recorded

The project’s one permitted formulation and its ML0–ML10 application are complete.

No modified quotient rule, revised meaning of \(\mathcal I_G\), additional selector, or successor theory of limitation is authorized within this standalone project.

A future project would require a new preregistration and an independently stated interface, not a relabelling of the missing actuality rule.

## Nonclaim

This test does not show that modal monism is false, that real distinction is a deficiency, that creator–creature concurrence is true, that all limitations are external, that plenitude is impossible, or that no theory of intrinsic limitation can be articulated.
