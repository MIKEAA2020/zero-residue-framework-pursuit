# Strong CI Adjudication — TB Classical Transcendentals-Conversion Bridge Audit v1
## Can being–truth–intelligibility convertibility supply a profile-neutral ontological selector?

## Status

**No public selector has been supplied.** A weak convertibility claim is compatible with several live ultimate profiles; a strong classical convertibility package imports contested classical metaphysics. The route does not yet satisfy the direct-condition controls.

## 1. The proposed condition

The classical route may propose:

\[
\mathsf{Conv}_{\rm weak}:
\quad
\forall x[\mathsf{Real}(x)\Rightarrow\mathsf{AptForIntelligibility}(x)].
\]

This must be distinguished from:

\[
\mathsf{Conv}_{\rm strong}:
\quad
\text{being, unity, truth, and goodness are metaphysically convertible in a}\
\text{classical account of act, form, intellect, and analogical being.}
\]

And both must be distinguished from:

\[
\mathsf{FullInt}:
\quad
\text{the exact ultimate profile and finite order are fully non-circularly intelligible.}
\]

The route fails if it silently substitutes \(\mathsf{Conv}_{\rm strong}\) or \(\mathsf{FullInt}\) for \(\mathsf{Conv}_{\rm weak}\).

## 2. Semantic and ontological disambiguation

“Intelligibility” may mean at least:

| Sense | Content | Does it select an ultimate profile? |
|---|---|---:|
| Conceptual thinkability | A content can be entertained without contradiction | no |
| Cognitive aptness | A real item can in principle manifest/be known by an intellect | no, not by itself |
| Truth relation | A judgment can conform to or be grounded by how a thing is | requires a substantive truth account; no profile selection shown |
| Full explanatory intelligibility | The item’s exact profile has a non-brute closure | this is the contested strongest-CI target, not a derived result |

No inference among these senses is free.

## 3. The direct-condition controls

### Profile neutrality

\(\mathsf{Conv}_{\rm weak}\) does not contain essence–existence identity, divine simplicity, an external creator, emanation, nondual aspect identity, or whole-first structure. It is potentially profile-neutral.

\(\mathsf{Conv}_{\rm strong}\), by contrast, brings a specific classical account of being, act, truth, and intellect. It cannot be treated as neutral merely because the terms are transcendental.

### Typed bridge

\[
\mathsf{Conv}_{\rm weak}
\not\Rightarrow
\mathsf{FullInt}.
\tag{No Full-Int Bridge}
\]

Cognitive aptness or truth-relatedness does not explain why there is this exact reality, this ground, or this finite domain.

### Adversarial cases

| Candidate | Can it accept \(\mathsf{Conv}_{\rm weak}\)? | Is it excluded? |
|---|---:|---:|
| Classical essence–existence ground | yes | no |
| Necessary emanation/plenitude | yes | no |
| Nondual genuine-aspect identity | yes: genuine content is self-presentable | no |
| Whole-first/structural totality | yes: its intrinsic structure can be intelligible | no |
| Deflationary/brute-remainder rival | may allow local/objective intelligibility while reject global full intelligibility | no |

The weak claim does not discriminate.

## 4. The strong-package dilemma

\[
\boxed{
\mathsf{Conv}_{\rm weak}
\Rightarrow
\text{too weak to select a framework};
\qquad
\mathsf{Conv}_{\rm strong}
\Rightarrow
\text{too profile-laden to serve as an independent selector.}
}
\]

To escape this dilemma, a defender must derive the classical act/form/intellect package from profile-neutral premises, then show why alternative accounts of truth and intelligibility fail. Neither derivation is presently supplied.

## 5. Relation to TB1

A correspondence-oriented truth theory can support a limited claim that truth is constrained by how reality is. This supports neither full convertibility nor a classical ultimate profile:

\[
\mathsf{TruthGroundedInReality}
\not\Rightarrow
\mathsf{Conv}_{\rm strong},
\qquad
\mathsf{TruthGroundedInReality}
\not\Rightarrow
\mathsf{FullInt}.
\]

Conversely, a nondual or whole-first candidate can offer an internal account of truth/intelligibility without conceding classical essence–existence identity.

## 6. Exact result

\[
\boxed{
\mathsf{Conv}_{\rm weak}
\not\Rightarrow
\mathsf{UniqueProfile}(P).
}
\]

\[
\boxed{
\mathsf{Conv}_{\rm strong}
\text{ is a substantive candidate package, not an independently established}\
\text{condition for selecting that package.}
}
\]

Thus the classical transcendental strategy does not yet provide the missing CI0B typed bridge or break the candidate symmetry.

## 7. Source note

Medieval transcendental theories are plural rather than a single neutral doctrine. Their accounts of the relation among being, unity, truth, goodness, cognition, and God differ; the relation between transcendental truth and propositional truth is itself a recognised tension. See https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/transcendentals-medieval/.

## 8. Expiry condition

This route advances only if it provides:

1. a profile-neutral derivation of a sufficiently strong convertibility claim;
2. a typed bridge from that claim to exact ontological/explanatory closure;
3. adverse treatment showing why nondual, whole-first, emanation, and brute-remainder profiles do not meet the same condition; and
4. a non-circular argument for the classical act/form/intellect package.

Absent those deliverables, the weak/strong dilemma closes the transcendental-conversion route as a selector.

## Nonclaims

This audit does not deny transcendental doctrines, reject classical metaphysics, deny that being is intelligible, or establish a non-classical rival. It evaluates only the claim that convertibility currently furnishes an independently grounded selector of the correct framework.
