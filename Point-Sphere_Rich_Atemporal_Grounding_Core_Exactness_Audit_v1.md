# Rich Atemporal-Grounding Core Exactness Audit v1
## What the Point–Sphere core commitment does and does not settle

**Scope:** audit of the rich atemporal-grounding reading within the strict pure-negation Appearance candidate.  
**Nonclaim:** this audit does not reject that core reading, replace it with a rival, derive appearance from a thin Absolute, or treat a core commitment as a framework-selection result.

---

## 0. Fixed candidate-core claim

The rich Point–Sphere core states:

\[
\mathsf{Core}^{\exists}_{\rm PAG}
:=
\mathsf{Core}_{\rm NP}
\land
\exists\sigma\,
\mathsf{AG}_{\rm PS}(G,\sigma),
\tag{Existential-Core}
\]

where \(G\) is simple, spaceless, and timeless; \(\sigma\) is a code/profile of the whole appearance-field; and \(\mathsf{AG}_{\rm PS}\) is atemporal, asymmetric grounding rather than temporal production, a part-relation, or a relation between independent beings.

This is a legitimate way to mark a candidate's protected starting point:

\[
\mathsf{Core}^{\exists}_{\rm PAG}
\not\equiv
\mathsf{ThinDerivationFromSimplicity}.
\tag{No-Derivation-Claim}
\]

The question here is narrower: does the existential rich core determine the **exact** whole appearance-field that it claims is grounded?

---

## 1. Exactness is not supplied by existential grounding

Let \(\simeq_{\rm app}\) be a declared profile-conservative equivalence relation on candidate whole-field codes. Suppose that two candidate fields are not equivalent:

\[
\sigma_0\not\simeq_{\rm app}\sigma_1.
\tag{NonEquivalent-Fields}
\]

The bare existential core permits a realization in which \(G\) atemporally grounds \(\sigma_0\), and another in which it atemporally grounds \(\sigma_1\):

\[
\begin{array}{rcl}
\mathfrak M_0&\models&
\mathsf{Core}_{\rm NP}
\land
\mathsf{AG}_{\rm PS}(G,\sigma_0),\\
\mathfrak M_1&\models&
\mathsf{Core}_{\rm NP}
\land
\mathsf{AG}_{\rm PS}(G,\sigma_1).
\end{array}
\tag{Model-Pair}
\]

Nothing in the existential statement itself excludes either model. Hence:

\[
\boxed{
\mathsf{Core}^{\exists}_{\rm PAG}
\not\models
\exists![\sigma]_{\simeq_{\rm app}}\,
\mathsf{AG}_{\rm PS}(G,\sigma).
}
\tag{Existential-Underdetermination}
\]

This is an exactness result, not a denial that one field might be grounded in fact.

---

## 2. Three distinct rich-core positions

The framework must keep the following options distinct.

\[
\begin{array}{c|l|l}
\textbf{position} & \textbf{core claim} & \textbf{status}\\
\hline
\mathsf{PAG}_{\exists} & \exists\sigma\,\mathsf{AG}_{\rm PS}(G,\sigma) & \text{grounds some appearance-field, not its exact profile}\\
\mathsf{PAG}_{!} & \exists![\sigma]_{\simeq_{\rm app}}\,\mathsf{AG}_{\rm PS}(G,\sigma) & \text{uniqueness is additionally core-stipulated}\\
\mathsf{PAG}_{\Omega} & \mathsf{AG}_{\rm PS}(G,\sigma)\iff\Omega_{\rm PS}(G,\sigma) & \text{requires an independent grounding rule }\Omega_{\rm PS}
\end{array}
\tag{Core-Options}
\]

### 2.1 Existential rich core

\(
\mathsf{PAG}_{\exists}
\) is the least committal version. It avoids pretending to derive appearance from the thin Absolute but leaves the exactness question open.

### 2.2 Unique rich core

\(
\mathsf{PAG}_{!}
\) adds a unique whole appearance-field modulo declared equivalence:

\[
\mathsf{Core}^{!}_{\rm PAG}
:=
\mathsf{Core}_{\rm NP}
\land
\exists![\sigma]_{\simeq_{\rm app}}
\mathsf{AG}_{\rm PS}(G,\sigma).
\tag{Unique-Core}
\]

This is permissible only if stated honestly as a **richer core commitment**. It is not a theorem of point–sphere polarity, simplicity, or atemporality:

\[
\mathsf{Core}_{\rm NP}
\land
\mathsf{PSContrast}
\not\models
\mathsf{Core}^{!}_{\rm PAG}.
\tag{Uniqueness-Not-Derived}
\]

### 2.3 Rule-governed grounding

\(
\mathsf{PAG}_{\Omega}
\) is the public-bridge aspiration. Its rule cannot define grounding by already actual appearance:

\[
\Omega_{\rm PS}(G,\sigma)
\not\iff
\mathsf{ActualAppearance}(\sigma).
\tag{No-Circular-Rule}
\]

It requires a candidate-independent field, output conditions, positive and negative cases, equivalence, error conditions, and expiry. No such \(\Omega_{\rm PS}\) is presently supplied.

---

## 3. Consequences of making uniqueness core content

Stipulating \(\mathsf{PAG}_{!}\) does not automatically make the whole appearance-field a second being or a positive part of \(G\). Under the protected reading, the field remains a dependent appearance profile:

\[
\mathsf{AG}_{\rm PS}(G,\sigma)
\land
\mathsf{PAG}_{!}
\not\Rightarrow
\mathsf{IndependentEntity}(\sigma)
\lor
\mathsf{Part}(G).
\tag{No-Composition-by-Uniqueness}
\]

But uniqueness as core content carries four remaining burdens.

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
E_1 &: \text{state the equivalence relation }\simeq_{\rm app}\text{ rather than confuse notation with distinct fields};\\
E_2 &: \text{state what makes }\sigma\text{ a whole appearance-field rather than a code or a bare assertion};\\
E_3 &: \text{provide a finite-locus/type account without silently turning appearance into}\
&\text{independent substances or mere labels};\\
E_4 &: \text{meet the truth/error, privation, phenomenal, normative, and physical adequacy}\
&\text{requirements without treating core status as automatic confirmation.}
\end{array}
\tag{Core-Residuals}
\]

Thus:

\[
\mathsf{PAG}_{!}
\not\Rightarrow
\mathsf{ZRPass}(F_{\rm PAG}).
\tag{Unique-Core-Not-ZR}
\]

---

## 4. The anti-brute condition applies symmetrically

A protected core can be an explicit conditional starting point. It cannot receive a free public exemption from the exactness burden merely because it is called “core”:

\[
\mathsf{CoreCommitment}(q)
\not\Rightarrow
\mathsf{PublicNoBruteResidue}(q).
\tag{No-Core-Exemption}
\]

The correct symmetric comparison is:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\textbf{candidate form} & \textbf{public burden}\\
\hline
\text{contingent creation} & \text{nonbrute selector/difference-maker for an exact output}\\
\text{necessary emanation} & \text{noncircular essence-to-domain/output map}\\
\text{Point--Sphere rich core} & \text{why this grounding content and, if asserted, this exact field are not}\
&\text{merely protected stipulation in a public comparison}
\end{array}
\tag{Symmetric-Burden}
\]

This does not make all candidates identical or equally promising. It blocks only an asymmetric inference from “our claim is core” to “our exactness is explained while rivals’ core content is brute.”

---

## 5. Disposition

The core distinction may now be stated without equivocation:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&\mathsf{PAG}_{\exists}:\quad
\text{a conditional core commitment to some atemporally grounded appearance};\\
&\mathsf{PAG}_{!}:\quad
\text{a still richer conditional core commitment to a unique appearance field};\\
&\mathsf{PAG}_{\Omega}:\quad
\text{the unachieved public grounding-rule form.}
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{Core-Classification}
\]

No option is a thin derivation from simplicity. The first two are not refuted by this audit, but neither is a zero-residue pass or a correct-framework verdict.

\[
\boxed{
\mathsf{GroundingBridgeStatus}(F_{\rm PAG})
=
\mathsf{ConditionalCoreArticulation};
\quad
\neg\mathsf{PublicGroundingPass}(F_{\rm PAG}).
}
\tag{Final-Status}
\]

\[
\exists![F]_{\simeq}\,\mathsf{ZeroResidue}(F)
\quad\text{remains unproved.}
\tag{Open-Objective}
\]
