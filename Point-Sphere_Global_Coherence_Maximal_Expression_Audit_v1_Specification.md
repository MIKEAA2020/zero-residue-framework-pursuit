# GC3 — Maximal-Expression Hinge Audit v1
## Formal specification

## Decision question

Does complete self-knowing awareness entail realization of all and only independently typed genuine finite self-aspects, or does the claimed result still require aspect-domain, non-representational-identity, compossibility, and non-composition bridges?

GC3 builds on AP2B/B1, AP2B/B4, AP2A, and GC1. It does not revisit symmetry-only derivations or treat formal grammar as ground structure.

## Conditional identity theorem under test

Let:

- \(C(G)\): the ground lacks no perfection intrinsic to its fully actual nature;
- \(W(G)\): the ground is self-knowing awareness;
- \(\mathcal A\): independently typed genuine finite intrinsic aspect-content;
- \(E(G,\mathcal A)\): self-knowledge is exhaustive over \(\mathcal A\);
- \(I(G,\mathcal A)\): knowledge of \(a\in\mathcal A\) is non-representational actual self-presentation of \(a\);
- \(R(a)\): \(a\) is realized;
- \(\mathsf{Comp}(\mathcal A)\): incompatible finite contents are assigned declared proper contexts, not jointly asserted within one inconsistent finite context;
- \(\mathsf{NC}(G,\mathcal A)\): aspect articulation does not add parts, changing states, or ground-level structure to \(G\).

The conditional result is:

\[
C(G)\land W(G)\land E(G,\mathcal A)\land I(G,\mathcal A)
\Rightarrow
\mathsf{MEC}(\mathcal A),
\]

where

\[
\mathsf{MEC}(\mathcal A):\quad
\forall a\in\mathcal A\;R(a).
\]

The conclusion is P-internal immanent plenitude only. It is not unrestricted all-possible-world plenitude.

## Required bridge tests

### GC3a — independent aspect criterion

\(\mathcal A\) must be fixed independently of realization, bare coherence, arbitrary grammar, and gauge copies. A domain selected only by the realized support fails the test.

### GC3b — omission bridge

The audit must distinguish:

\[
\text{known possible content}
\]

from

\[
\text{genuine non-representational self-knowledge content}.
\]

Only the latter permits \(I\). The audit must test whether \(C\land W\) alone entails either \(E\) or \(I\).

### GC3c — compossibility

The framework must not realize mutually incompatible finite contents in one finite proper context. It may use maximal proper contexts or a successor context architecture only when the relevant context relation is declared rather than silently presumed.

### GC3d — non-composition

The aspect account may not be read as a set of parts/properties possessed by \(G\) qua \(G\). This is a typing condition; no toy structure is a model of the ground itself.

## Finite audit families

1. **Bare self-presence:** \(C\land W\) without a nonempty finite aspect domain or exhaustivity bridge.
2. **Representational knowledge:** exhaustive knowledge of a candidate domain but no identity of knowing with realization.
3. **Full non-representational aspect model:** independent full \(\mathcal A\), exhaustivity, identity, full realization, declared compossibility/non-composition.
4. **Restricted-domain model:** MEC is true relative to a selected proper subdomain, exposing the aspect-domain debt.
5. **Unresolved-compossibility model:** full realization but incompatible finite contents lack a proper-context assignment.

## Required outputs

The executable audit must verify:

1. The conditional identity theorem is valid when \(E\) and \(I\) are explicitly present.
2. \(C\land W\) alone does not entail a nonempty finite \(\mathcal A\), \(E\), or \(I\).
3. Exhaustivity of represented possibilities does not entail MEC without \(I\).
4. MEC relative to a restricted \(\mathcal A\) does not establish independent aspect typing.
5. MEC does not by itself settle compossibility or non-composition.

## Success condition

GC3 passes as a rigorous **closure audit** if it states the valid conditional theorem, identifies each non-entailment, and gives an exact agenda for proving the four bridges. It passes as a positive plenitude proof only if a later argument independently establishes GC3a–d without circularity.
