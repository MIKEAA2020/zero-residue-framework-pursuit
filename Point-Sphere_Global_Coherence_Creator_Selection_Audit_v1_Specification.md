# GC2 — Classical Creator-Selection Lemma Audit v1
## Formal specification

## Decision question

Can a strictly simple ultimate ground contingently actualize one of non-equivalent candidate created totalities while satisfying the GC1 no-brute-differentiation standard, without concealing a primitive or circular selector?

This is an ideal-type analysis of explanatory structures. It is not an adjudication of a historical religious tradition, an assessment of scripture, or a claim that every classical theist endorses identical modal premises.

## Frozen language

Let \(G\) be an ultimate ground and \(W=\{w_0,w_1\}\) a pair of non-equivalent candidate created totalities. Let \(\mathsf{Act}(G,w)\) denote actualization of \(w\).

### Strict simplicity (SSS)

For this audit, \(\mathsf{SSS}(G)\) means no independently variable intrinsic differentiating component, state, or property in \(G\) is available to explain why one of \(w_0,w_1\) is actual rather than the other. This is deliberately a strong analytic reading. A rival may reject it or distinguish real from merely conceptual distinctions; that is a substantive response to test, not a forbidden move.

### Contingent exclusive actualization (CEA)

\[
\mathsf{CEA}(G;w_0,w_1):
\quad
\mathsf{Act}(G,w_0)\land\neg\mathsf{Act}(G,w_1)
\]

with both actualization alternatives held to be genuinely open relative to the same complete intrinsic nature of \(G\).

### Weak NBD

An asymmetric actualization needs some declared differentiator.

\[
\mathsf{NBD}_{w}:
\mathsf{CEA}(G;w_0,w_1)
\Rightarrow
\exists\delta\,\Delta(w_0,w_1;\delta).
\]

A primitive selector or an extrinsic act relation may count formally as \(\delta\) at this weak level.

### Strong NBD / CI condition

A difference-maker must be non-circular and non-primitive with respect to the very asymmetry it is invoked to explain:

\[
\mathsf{NBD}_{s}:
\mathsf{CEA}(G;w_0,w_1)
\Rightarrow
\exists\delta\,[
\Delta(w_0,w_1;\delta)
\land\mathsf{NonCircular}(\delta)
\land\mathsf{NonPrimitive}(\delta)
].
\]

This is the relevant standard if global coherence means no unexplained terminal selection. It is stronger than GC1 S2 and must be defended at GC4; it is not bare logic.

### Necessary determination

\[
\mathsf{ND}(G,w_0):
\quad
f(G)=w_0
\]

for a non-circular exact-totality bridge \(f\). This is not CEA: it gives up the stipulated contingency of the alternative outcome.

## Rival replies to test

1. **Intrinsic differentiator.** An intrinsic fact in \(G\) distinguishes \(w_0\) from \(w_1\).
2. **Extrinsic single act.** One immutable intrinsic act has a real extrinsic relation to \(w_0\), claimed not to add an intrinsic component to \(G\).
3. **Primitive agent causation.** A free choice/will primitively selects \(w_0\), without derivation from prior reasons.
4. **Necessary determination.** \(G\) determines \(w_0\) by a bridge \(f\), rather than contingently selecting it.

## Required results

The audit must establish whether each reply satisfies:

- strict simplicity;
- weak NBD;
- strong NBD;
- contingent exclusive actualization;
- absence of a selector/determination debt.

## Conditional lemma target

The intended conditional theorem is:

\[
\mathsf{SSS}(G)
\land
\mathsf{CEA}(G;w_0,w_1)
\land
\mathsf{NBD}_{s}
\Longrightarrow
\bot.
\]

The proof is by case distinction. A differentiator is intrinsic, extrinsic, primitive/circular, or a necessary determination. Respectively it conflicts with SSS, fails strong NBD unless independently grounded, violates the no-primitive condition, or abandons CEA.

The audit must also test the weaker claim. If primitive agency or an extrinsic act is allowed as an ultimate primitive, then the conditional impossibility does not follow:

\[
\mathsf{SSS}(G)
\land
\mathsf{CEA}(G;w_0,w_1)
\land
\mathsf{NBD}_{w}
\not\Longrightarrow
\bot.
\]

## Interpretation rule

A result against CEA under strong NBD does not prove Appearance true. It only establishes a sharply stated conditional debt for contingent-creation models. Necessary-determination and appearance/plenitude then remain distinct rival tasks with their own exact-totality and aspect-realization burdens.
