# AP3P2L Self-Presentation Act-Theorem Audit v1 — Report
## A serious new route, but “act” does not yet derive flow, boundaries, or grammar adequacy

## Verdict

The proposed act theorem targets the correct missing interface: an account of finite self-presentation strong enough to ground flow and grammatical relevance without reducing ontology to a minimality preference.

It is therefore a legitimate next route. But at its present formulation it is not yet a theorem. The word **act** has two non-equivalent readings, and neither on its own supplies the proposed result.

## 1. The act ambiguity

### A. Metaphysical actuality / eternal act

If the ground’s self-knowledge is an atemporal actuality, then it is compatible with no succession. That is stipulated by the proposed ground/mode contrast itself. Hence:
\[
\mathsf{Act}_{\rm eternal}
\not\Rightarrow
\mathsf{TemporalFlow}.
\]

The core identity says that a mode is an aspect/content of self-knowledge. It does not formally say that every finite content is a temporally differentiated act.

### B. Phenomenological temporal act

Husserlian accounts provide a meaningful conditional resource: ongoing experience is structured by primal impression, retention, and protention, with retentional self-manifestation contributing to diachronic coherence. But this does not select a specific finite graph, a \(\mathbb Z\)-ordered successor sequence, a nonterminal flow, IAR alternation, phase-origin gauge, or component identity.

Furthermore, this temporal organization is not simply a self-initiated activity. Husserl distinguishes retention/protention as structural components occurring without active or deliberate contribution; fundamental temporalization is described as passive. Michel Henry likewise treats auto-affection as bound to radical passivity/pathos. Thus “auto-affection is an act” cannot simply mean an active operation from which a sequence of discrete phase transitions follows.

Relevant source-sensitive support and limits are recorded in the SEP entries on [Husserl](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/husserl/), [phenomenological self-consciousness](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/self-consciousness-phenomenological/), and [Michel Henry](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/michel-henry/).

## 2. The load-bearing missing bridge

To obtain the intended result, the proposal needs an independent finite temporalization law:
\[
\mathsf{FT}:
\quad
\mathsf{FiniteSelfPresentation}(m)
\Longrightarrow
\mathsf{TemporallyDifferentiatedAct}(m).
\]

This is stronger than the protected mode/aspect/content identity, but weaker than directly assuming AP3P1. If \(\mathsf{FT}\) can be independently justified, it would be a real advance.

It must then be joined by a representation theorem:
\[
\mathsf{TemporallyDifferentiatedAct}(m)
\Longrightarrow
\mathsf{Successor/Integration/BoundaryStructure}(m).
\]

AP3O is decisive here: static internal order does not yield a successor relation. Calling self-presentation an act does not bridge that gap unless the act structure supplies the missing directional/integrative relation in a noncircular form.

## 3. What an act theorem could support

At a defensible conditional strength, a finite temporal self-presentation act can motivate:

- immanent rather than externally observed continuity;
- retentional/protentional coherence;
- a requirement that a grammar represent whatever act-structure is independently shown necessary;
- a candidate internal integration criterion for a mode.

This is more principled than a bare graph stipulation.

## 4. What it does not automatically support

### Flow

\[
\text{temporal field/coherence}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{AP3P1’s bi-infinite successor grammar}.
\]

A finite temporal act could be recurrent, terminal, branching, bounded in duration, or represented by more than one formal architecture. The intended non-ceasing mode-flow remains a substantive claim requiring its own formal law.

### Boundaries

“The act stops here” cannot be used as a boundary criterion before the act has been individuated. Finite self-presentation may have nested, overlapping, or horizonal articulation. A boundary theorem needs an explicit act-integration or closure relation; otherwise component-to-mode identity is reintroduced in verbal form.

### Partial adequacy and enrichment

An act theorem could establish that certain features are required for every target act. It cannot establish:
\[
\neg\operatorname{RequiredByEveryAct}(x)
\Rightarrow
\neg\operatorname{GenuineContent}(x).
\]

A P/Q comparator or another relation might be genuine in some enriched acts while not constitutive of all finite acts. Thus the theorem can classify **necessary act features**, but not eliminate optional genuine enrichment merely through nonnecessity.

## 5. Correct expiry condition

The route succeeds only if both steps are made independently and survive anti-confirmation testing:

1. **Finite temporalization:** Why must a finite mode, rather than merely some finite modes, be a temporally differentiated self-presentation act?
2. **Act-to-grammar map:** Which successor, integration, and boundary relations are required by that act, and would those conditions still be accepted if they excluded IAR?

If the first is stipulated as “act means temporally flowing,” or the second defines flow in terms of a prior successor graph, the derivation is circular. If neither is independently established, the correct fallback remains the labelled AP3P1 flow grammar and its minimal-description quotient.

## Conclusion

The self-presentation-act proposal is a productive new research target, not an established derivation. It may yield richer conditional constraints than AP3J’s static typing results. But it presently supplies neither the finite temporalization bridge nor the act-to-formal-grammar theorem required to convert AP3P1 flow, component boundaries, and partial adequacy into derived consequences.
