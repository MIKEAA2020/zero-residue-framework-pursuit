# AP3A Perspective-Individuation Test v1: Executed Result

## Question

Can a formal model contain distinct bounded perspectives with shared public records while treating their local states as non-independent restrictions of one global aspect-state?

This test is the first constructive response to the decombination/individuation debt identified in AP2B. It is deliberately only a finite structural consistency diagnostic.

- Specification: `Point-Sphere_Appearance_Plenitude_Perspective_Individuation_Test_v1_Specification.md`
- Script: `point_sphere_appearance_plenitude_perspective_individuation_test_v1.py`
- Machine output: `Point-Sphere_Appearance_Plenitude_Perspective_Individuation_Test_v1.json`

## Frozen formal analogue

The global formal configuration space was
\[
X=\{0,1\}^{\{0,1,2,3,4,5\}},
\]
with 64 configurations. It is explicitly **not** the metaphysical ground. It represents only one finite globally coherent aspect-state in an analogue model.

Two candidate local profiles were restriction maps:

\[
V_P=\{0,1,2,3,4\},\qquad
V_Q=\{0,1,2,3,5\},\qquad
O=\{0,1,2,3\}.
\]

Thus P and Q had a declared shared/public interface \(O\), while each had one private/unavailable coordinate relative to the other.

## Executed findings

All frozen checks passed.

| Requirement | Result |
|---|---|
| Relational identity | The six-vertex global graph had exactly one automorphism: the identity. The two induced carrier graphs had zero isomorphisms. P and Q were therefore not labels attached to structurally interchangeable copies. |
| Bounded access | Each local restriction had 32 local states, each with a two-element fibre in the 64-state global space. Distinct global configurations can look identical to P; likewise to Q. |
| Public compatibility | For every global configuration, P and Q agreed exactly on the four-coordinate overlap \(O\). |
| Non-independent amalgamation | Of \(32\times32=1024\) formal local pairs, exactly 64 agreed on \(O\). Every compatible pair had exactly one global amalgamation; every incompatible pair had none. |

The compatible-pair statement can be written:

\[
\{(p,q):\exists!x\in X\,[\pi_P(x)=p\land\pi_Q(x)=q]\}
=
\{(p,q):p|_O=q|_O\}.
\]

This is the strongest result of the toy: the local profiles are neither two independently chosen complete global states nor free-floating labels. They are compatible partial presentations of one globally specified configuration.

## What AP3A establishes

AP3A establishes a limited formal possibility:

> Non-identical, bounded local access profiles can share public records and be globally constrained restrictions of one relational state rather than independent substances.

This gives the Point–Sphere appearance route a nontrivial formal template for the phrase “real finite modes, not independent beings.” It improves on simply asserting that all perspectives are one.

## What AP3A does not establish

The limits are decisive.

1. \(X\) has six coordinates and graph structure. It cannot be identified with a simple, non-composite ground.
2. A restriction profile is not yet a conscious subject, a person, a phenomenally bounded field, or a free agent.
3. The relational graph and its asymmetry were stipulated. The test does not derive the relevant access structure from awareness, completeness, plenitude, or physical law.
4. Shared overlap is a formal public-record condition, not a derivation of real measurement, objectivity, Born weights, local causation, or time.
5. The global configuration is static. It supplies no temporal becoming, quantum dynamics, spacetime, or Problem-of-Time result.

Therefore AP3A passes as a **structural consistency and typing result**, not a decombination solution.

## Next node: AP3B

AP3B must address the interpretation bridge that AP3A intentionally leaves open. A viable successor needs to define:

\[
\mathfrak M=(X,\mathcal R,\{\pi_i\},\mathcal I,\mathcal P),
\]

where \(\mathcal R\) is relational structure, \(\pi_i\) are perspective restrictions, \(\mathcal I\) is a non-arbitrary identity/boundary criterion, and \(\mathcal P\) is a phenomenality bridge or a clearly stated condition for it. It must show why the global formal state is an aspect/mode of the ground rather than an independent composite substrate, and why a restriction could qualify as a perspective rather than merely an observer-relative data partition.
