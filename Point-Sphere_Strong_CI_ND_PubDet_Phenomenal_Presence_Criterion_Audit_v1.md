# Strong CI Adjudication — ND Public Determination: Phenomenal-Presence Criterion Audit v1
## Can finite phenomenal manifestation identify genuine nondual aspects?

## Status

**Phenomenal presence does not supply a realization-independent all-and-only aspect criterion.** It can constrain phenomenological adequacy, but it neither establishes ground-aspect status nor classifies the full finite domain.

## 1. Predeclared proposal

Let \(\mathsf{Appears}(a)\) mean that finite candidate \(a\) is phenomenally manifest in the minimal sense that there is an occurrence of its appearing. Consider:

\[
\mathsf{PhDet}_G(a):
\quad
\mathsf{FiniteCandidate}(a)
\land
\mathsf{Appears}(a)
\land
\mathsf{NonGauge}(a).
\tag{Ph}
\]

Intended reading:

> A finite candidate is a genuine aspect when it is a non-duplicate finite phenomenal manifestation.

## 2. Predeclared expiry conditions

Ph fails if:

1. \(\mathsf{Appears}(a)\) silently entails actual aspecthood/realization;
2. phenomenal manifestation cannot be connected non-circularly to ground self-knowledge;
3. it cannot distinguish genuine manifestation from error, illusion, or merely intentional/nonexistent content without imported semantics;
4. it excludes unmanifest finite aspects merely because they are not currently appearing; or
5. it provides no compossibility rule for phenomenally incompatible contents.

## 3. Realization dependence

Actual appearing is an occurrence. If it is used to establish that \(a\) is a genuine aspect, the proposed criterion is not realization-independent:

\[
\mathsf{Appears}(a)
\Rightarrow
\text{actual phenomenal occurrence involving \(a\)}.
\]

Replacing actual appearance with “phenomenally possible appearance” only transfers the problem to a modal/possibility semantics:

\[
\mathsf{PhenomenallyPossible}(a)
\not\Rightarrow
\mathsf{GenuineAspect}(a).
\]

## 4. Ground connection gap

Phenomenality supplies an adequacy datum. It does not by itself establish:

\[
\mathsf{Appears}(a)
\Rightarrow
\mathsf{ContentOfSelfKnowledge}_G(a),
\]

nor:

\[
\mathsf{ContentOfSelfKnowledge}_G(a)
\Rightarrow
\mathsf{GenuineAspect}(a)
\]

for independently specified candidates. The latter is the very domain question at issue.

## 5. Error and absent-aspect barriers

A phenomenological criterion requires an account of whether an appearing content can misrepresent, be illusory, or be directed toward a non-genuine candidate. These notions cannot be silently assumed.

Even setting that issue aside, the converse problem remains:

\[
\mathsf{GenuineAspect}(a)
\not\Rightarrow
\mathsf{CurrentlyAppears}(a).
\]

A complete finite domain cannot be identified by whichever contents currently manifest without an unargued restriction of genuine aspecthood to current presentation.

## 6. Case test

| Candidate case | Ph outcome | Failure |
|---|---|---|
| A finite phenomenal content | conditionally included | Its appearance does not establish its ground-aspect status |
| A coherent non-gauge finite candidate not phenomenally manifest | excluded/undetermined | No principled reason it cannot be a genuine unmanifest aspect |
| An apparent but misrepresenting/intentional content | classification unavailable | Requires an unprovided truth/error/intentionality semantics |
| A gauge duplicate of a manifestation | filtered | Non-gauge status is not genuine-aspect determination |

No independent all-and-only positive/negative classification results.

## 7. Compossibility

Phenomenal co-presence or succession does not itself define aspect compossibility:

\[
\mathsf{Appears}(a)
\land
\mathsf{Appears}(b)
\not\Rightarrow
\mathsf{Comp}_G(a,b).
\]

It would be illicit to import temporal succession, subject persistence, or a public object record as the needed compatibility structure.

## 8. Exact result

\[
\boxed{
\mathsf{PhDet}_G
\not\Rightarrow
\mathsf{PubDet}_G.
}
\]

Phenomenal presence is relevant to phenomenological adequacy, but is not a realization-independent public semantics of genuine aspecthood.

## 9. Expiry conclusion

This phenomenal-presence proposal is closed in its stated form. A successor would need a non-circular ground–phenomenality relation, a truth/error and intentionality semantics if applicable, a reason to include unmanifest genuine aspects, and an atemporal compossibility account.

## Nonclaims

This audit does not deny phenomenality, reject prereflective self-awareness, establish illusionism, or deny a relation between nondual self-knowledge and experience. It evaluates only the proposed use of actual phenomenal manifestation as an all-and-only domain criterion.
