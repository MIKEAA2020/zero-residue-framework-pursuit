# Strong CI Adjudication — EDP Typed Possibility: Participation-Semantics Audit v1
## Can a finite participation criterion publicly define real possibility for necessary emanation?

## Status

**The proposed participation semantics has no public typed-possibility pass.** It is a concrete attempt to define the domain prior to realization, but its predicates are either unspecified, encode the desired hierarchy, or reduce to a formal representation. It does not supply DTP’s real-possibility premise.

## 1. The EDP target

Necessary emanation/plenitude requires more than the conditional actualization of an already typed domain:

\[
\mathsf{DTP}:
\quad
\mathsf{NecessarilyDiffusive}(U)
\Rightarrow
\forall a[
\mathsf{RealPossibility}(a)
\Rightarrow
R(a)].
\]

The unresolved prior question is:

\[
\mathsf{RP}_U(a):
\quad
\text{What makes \(a\) a real finite possibility of participation in \(U\),}\
\text{rather than a merely formal candidate?}
\]

A public pass requires typed inclusion/exclusion, positive/negative cases, non-gauge identity, compossibility, and independence from realization.

## 2. Predeclared proposal: limited positive participation

Define the candidate criterion:

\[
\mathsf{PP}_U(a):
\quad
\mathsf{FiniteCandidate}(a)
\land
\exists p[
\mathsf{PositivePerfection}(p,U)
\land
\mathsf{LimitedParticipation}(a,p,U)
\land
\mathsf{NonGauge}(a)
].
\tag{PP}
\]

Intended reading:

> A real finite possibility is a non-duplicate finite, limited participation in a positive perfection of \(U\).

PP contains no \(R(a)\). It is therefore a genuine realization-independent candidate proposal, not an assumed result.

## 3. Predeclared expiry conditions

PP fails if:

1. \(\mathsf{PositivePerfection}\) lacks independent semantics or contains the desired domain by stipulation;
2. \(\mathsf{LimitedParticipation}\) is only metaphorical, a formal degree label, or an unexplained primitive;
3. coherent non-gauge candidates cannot be included/excluded;
4. compossibility is assumed rather than typed; or
5. a claimed hierarchy is inserted as a premise rather than derived.

## 4. Predicate burden: positive perfection

There are three available readings of \(\mathsf{PositivePerfection}(p,U)\):

| Reading | Result |
|---|---|
| Thin goodness/perfection claim | Does not identify finite participable \(p\)'s or their possible limitations |
| Exact classical hierarchy of perfections | Encodes the desired participation domain/hierarchy in the premise |
| Formal quality/order space | Requires the grammar/model-to-ontology bridge |

No reading supplies a public independent set of positive perfections.

## 5. Predicate burden: limited participation

\(\mathsf{LimitedParticipation}(a,p,U)\) must say more than “\(a\) has less of \(p\) than \(U\).” It needs:

1. a relation type: constitutive, analogical, causal, modal, or another kind;
2. a scale/order semantics for “limited”; 
3. a criterion distinguishing a genuine finite participation from a merely described diminution;
4. a non-composition account of source and participant; and
5. positive/negative finite cases.

Without this, PP reads:

\[
\mathsf{RealPossibility}(a)
\Longleftrightarrow
\text{\(a\) is the sort of real participation the theory admits},
\]

which is circular.

## 6. Case test

| Candidate case | PP outcome | Problem |
|---|---|---|
| A finite participant explicitly listed in a classical hierarchy | included only conditionally | Its status is core/premise supplied, not publicly classified |
| A coherent non-gauge finite structure outside that hierarchy | undetermined | PP gives no rule for inclusion/exclusion |
| A formal diminution/degree in a mathematical model | formally classifiable | No bridge to real possibility |
| A contradictory candidate | excluded by logic | Logic excludes inconsistency but does not identify real finite possibility |

No independent positive/negative pair is generated.

## 7. Compossibility and exact hierarchy

Even if individual participation possibilities were identified:

\[
\mathsf{PP}_U(a)
\not\Rightarrow
\mathsf{Comp}_U(a,b).
\]

A plenitude candidate needs a relation or context scheme that determines when limited participations coexist, conflict, are duplicates, or form a hierarchy. A bare all-possibilities thesis does not supply it.

Likewise:

\[
\mathsf{PP}_U(a)
\not\Rightarrow
\mathsf{ExactHierarchy}(U).
\]

## 8. Exact result

\[
\boxed{
\mathsf{PP}_U
\text{ is realization-independent in form but does not determine}\
\mathsf{RP}_U\text{ publicly.}
}
\]

\[
\boxed{
\mathsf{PP}_U
\not\Rightarrow
\mathsf{RealPossibility}(a),
\qquad
\mathsf{PP}_U
\not\Rightarrow
\mathsf{Comp}_U(a,b),
\qquad
\mathsf{PP}_U
\not\Rightarrow
\mathsf{ExactHierarchy}(U).
}
\]

It therefore cannot furnish the domain premise needed by DTP.

## 9. Source note

Medieval transcendental accounts link goodness to actuality/desirability in differing ways, but this does not provide an automatic exact finite creation or participation theorem. See https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/transcendentals-medieval/.

## 10. Expiry conclusion

This participation proposal is closed in its present form. A successor must independently define both \(\mathsf{PositivePerfection}\) and \(\mathsf{LimitedParticipation}\), predeclare positive/negative and compossibility cases, and avoid importing the intended hierarchy in its inputs.

## Nonclaims

This audit does not reject participation metaphysics, deny goodness, rule out a typed possibility domain, or refute necessary emanation. It tests one exact participation criterion and finds that it does not currently supply the real-possibility/compossibility bridge.
