# Creation Architecture Comparison
## C-Nec, C-Cont, and strict numerical non-duality after the repaired foundation stack

## Scope

This comparison begins only with the **conditional** results of the ground stack: an actual externally non-derivative bearer, necessary existence relative to \(R\), and—only under MI\*, EL\*, EC\*, ECmp\*, and RIG—strong aseity and essential closure. It does not assume awareness, personhood, goodness, literal creation, or an exhaustive principle of realisation.

The question is narrower: if a framework adds a relation between a necessary ground and finite reality, where does the determination of the actual finite totality reside? The comparison does not prove that any architecture is true. It identifies what each must supply to count as an explanation rather than a label.

---

## 1. Fixed assessment criteria

Every architecture is assessed under eight non-negotiable questions.

1. **Reality and distinction:** Are finite beings genuinely real and numerically distinct, or only conventionally distinct?
2. **Dependence and causal typing:** What grounds their existence, and does that relation compete with intraworld causes?
3. **Determination:** Why this derivative totality rather than another, or none?
4. **Modal profile:** Is the realised totality necessary, contingent, pluralised, or merely perspectival?
5. **Compatibility with strong aseity:** Does the architecture add an unexplained ultimate residue, or state how its selection/manifestation principle is essence-grounded?
6. **Individuation and agency:** What makes one finite subject or locus distinct; what form of control can it possess?
7. **Public structure:** Why are stable shared laws, records, and disagreement not illusory verbal residues?
8. **Suffering and ethics:** Does the account retain the reality of bad-forness and practical obligation; what additional burden follows if the source is called good?

The criteria are assessment constraints, not a hidden decision rule favouring one outcome.

---

## 2. C-Nec: necessary realisation or plenitude

### Structure

C-Nec adds a necessary realisation rule to a necessary ground. A generic form is
\[
\Box N_G\ \land\ \Box\bigl(N_G\rightarrow \operatorname{Act}(T)\bigr)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\Box\operatorname{Act}(T).
\]
The rule can select one derivative totality, or make every admissible and compossible totality actual. Literal creation is retained if R1–R5 hold: creatures are distinct, existentially dependent, and causally non-competitive with the ground.

### What C-Nec gains

- It avoids a primitive **actual-world selector**: the explanation of realised totality is built into a necessary rule.
- It can preserve real finite causal systems, and therefore leaves ordinary science and reasons-responsive agency structurally intact.
- Its grounding account is maximally unified if the realisation rule is independently motivated and genuinely belongs to the ground’s essence.

### What it owes

- **Rule content.** “Completeness,” “no preference,” or “expression” is not yet an admissibility relation, compossibility relation, realisation rule, or measure.
- **Modal consequence.** If \(T\) is an exact totality, C-Nec makes it necessary relative to \(R\). If \(T\) is only a coarse type, the result is weaker; the exact target must be stated before “modal collapse” or its denial is announced.
- **Plenitude accounting.** A many-totality version needs identity, accessibility, compossibility, measure, and location principles; otherwise it simply repeats “all possibilities.”
- **Suffering.** Necessary realisation cannot appeal to an unnecessitated choice. It therefore bears an especially direct burden for every permitted harm, including animal suffering and unequal distribution.

### Strong-aseity interaction

C-Nec can make selection essence-grounded in the strongest sense, but only at the cost of a clear modal rule. Strong aseity alone does not deliver that rule. The additional principle is **essential productivity/necessitating realisation**.

## 3. C-Cont: non-necessitating creation with a selector

### Structure

C-Cont retains literal creaturely distinction and a necessary ground but denies that the ground’s necessary nature necessitates the realised derivative totality. Across worlds, the same \(G\) may stand in different realisation relations or have different totalities associated with it:
\[
\Box N_G,\qquad \Diamond\operatorname{Act}(T_1),\qquad
\Diamond\operatorname{Act}(T_2),\qquad T_1\neq T_2.
\]

### What C-Cont gains

- It preserves metaphysical contingency of at least some derivative totalities, if the modal semantics is coherent.
- It may represent the actual totality as selected for reasons, values, or a free act without equating those reasons with a necessitating rule.
- It can retain R1–R5 and therefore public causal/scientific structure.

### What it owes

- **Selector location.** The differentiating fact, act, reason, relation, or parameter cannot be hidden behind the phrase “free creation.” It must be either intrinsic, extrinsic, or an explicitly primitive modal feature.
- **Simplicity/immutability.** If there are world-varying intrinsic volitions in \(G\), this conflicts with strong simplicity unless a precise account is provided. If the difference is purely relational, the model must say why the relational fact fully grounds this totality rather than another.
- **Strong-aseity interaction.** If the actual selector belongs to \(\mathcal U_G\), essential closure requires an essence-grounded account of it. That does **not** by itself make the selector necessary: the further claim that essence-grounding is modally invariant or necessitating is exactly what converts C-Cont into C-Nec. C-Cont must therefore deny or restrict that stronger modal-transfer principle while explaining how non-necessitating essence-grounding works.
- **Suffering.** Contingency can make alternative arrangements intelligible, but does not show that this arrangement was justified, necessary for a greater good, or compatible with benevolence.

### Exact status

C-Cont is not incoherent merely because it contains a selector. Its cost is an additional grounding/modal account, not a formal contradiction. It is a serious alternative precisely when it does not disguise this cost.

## 4. ND: strict numerical non-duality

### Structure

Strict ND denies that finite beings are numerically distinct creatures in the R2/R5 sense. Finite plurality, action, and separation are construed as perspectival, conventional, dependent appearances, or internal articulation of one reality.

### What ND gains

- It avoids a creator–creature reception relation and the particular “why did a distinct derivative totality become actual?” selector question.
- It can preserve strong aseity at the fundamental level without an additional act of literal creation.

### What it owes

- **Manifestation/appearance rule.** The need for explanation does not disappear; it becomes a question of why one reality has stable bounded appearances, public law, records, and apparent plurality.
- **Locus of ignorance.** If awareness is fundamental, ND must distinguish a merely formal restriction of information from a subject or locus for whom ignorance, pain, and agency occur.
- **Agency and ethical seriousness.** Calling finite subjects conventional does not licence dismissing their vulnerability. It needs a practical account of consent, repair, reasons, and responsibility that preserves the reality of harm at the relevant level.
- **Empirical compatibility.** It must recover the ordinary causal and intersubjective structure without converting it into an unexplained illusion.

### Strong-aseity interaction

ND eliminates literal-creation selection but not all explanatory burdens. If a manifestation principle is fundamental, it belongs among the ultimate-profile facts and faces the same MI\*/EL\*/EC\*/ECmp\* demand. Naming plurality “appearance” does not make the appearance-rule essence-explained.

## 5. Comparison ledger

| Criterion | C-Nec | C-Cont | Strict ND |
|---|---|---|---|
| Finite numerical reality | Yes, by R1–R5 | Yes, by R1–R5 | Denied or qualified |
| Non-competitive causal compatibility | Available by typed grounding | Available by typed grounding | Must reconstruct at conventional/perspectival level |
| Determination location | Necessary realisation rule | Selector/act/relation/reason | Manifestation/appearance rule |
| Main modal cost | Exact realised totality is necessary if rule is complete | Must model non-necessitating selection | Must model modality of appearances/public structure |
| Strong-aseity pressure | Explain essential productivity | Explain non-necessitating essence-grounding or price primitive selector | Explain manifestation as ultimate-profile fact |
| Finite agency | Often compatibilist/structural | May allow alternatives; no automatic libertarianism | Requires conventional but normatively serious agency |
| Central explanatory debt | Admissibility, compossibility, measure, harm under necessity | Selector, simplicity, and modal transfer | Individuation, ignorance, public plurality, suffering |
| Direct theodicy gain | None | None | None |

No row establishes a winner. C-Nec minimises selector primitives but has the strongest modal and suffering burden. C-Cont preserves robust contingency but owes the clearest selector/simplicity account. ND avoids literal creation’s selection relation but inherits the hardest individuation and public-world burden.

---

## 6. Failure conditions and next research question

Each architecture has a real closure condition.

- **C-Nec fails as an explanatory advance** if it cannot specify its admissibility/realisation rule independently of the actual world, or if it treats every counterexample as “mysteriously compossible.”
- **C-Cont fails as an explanatory advance** if its free selector is indistinguishable from an unexplained contingent primitive, or if it cannot reconcile world-varying selection with its simplicity/immutability claims.
- **ND fails as an explanatory advance** if it cannot state an individuation and public-structure model without reintroducing the real dependent plurality it officially denies.

The next topologically enabled task is not to choose among these labels. It is the intrinsic-nature comparison: whether any awareness-involving ground hypothesis earns abductive advantage over functional identity, neutral/Russellian monism, plural experience, or illusionism once their respective phenomenality and individuation costs are laid out.
