# Appearance/Plenitude Realisation-Independence Test v1
## AP2A — frozen diagnostic before proposing a global admissibility law

## Purpose

AP1 proved conditional orbit completion. AP2 must now determine whether global admissibility plus symmetry can *derive* necessary plenitude, rather than merely make it compatible.

This finite diagnostic isolates the issue. It is not a model of the ground, actual modes, or cosmology. It asks: once a gauge-invariant admissible landscape has been supplied, do gauge invariance and physical-symmetry invariance alone determine a nonempty realisation support?

## Frozen spaces

Use the labelled description space
\[
\Omega=\mathbb Z_4\times\mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_3,
\]
with coordinates \((a,s,u)\). The final coordinate \(u\) is gauge. Quotient by \(K=\mathbb Z_3\) to obtain
\[
\mathcal L=\Omega/K\cong\mathbb Z_4\times\mathbb Z_2.
\]

Let physical symmetry \(H=\mathbb Z_4\) act by
\[
h\cdot(a,s)=(a+h\!\!\pmod4,s).
\]
The landscape therefore has two distinct transitive physical orbits,
\[
O_0=\mathbb Z_4\times\{0\},\qquad O_1=\mathbb Z_4\times\{1\}.
\]

## Frozen admissibility input

For this diagnostic only, take the admissibility support to be
\[
\mathsf A=O_0.
\]
It is stipulated as an already-given global constraint sector. The point is not to justify that stipulation; the point is to test what follows **after** a nonempty, gauge-quotiented, symmetry-invariant admissible domain has been supplied.

A realisation rule is a support \(R\subseteq\mathsf A\). It is acceptable at this formal level if it is invariant under \(H\).

## Registered claims

1. Gauge quotient gives exactly eight physical landscape elements from 24 labelled descriptions.
2. \(\mathsf A\) is nonempty and \(H\)-invariant, and is one full transitive orbit.
3. The only \(H\)-invariant realisation supports contained in \(\mathsf A\) are
\[
R_\varnothing=\varnothing,
\qquad
R_{\rm pl}=\mathsf A.
\]
4. Hence nonemptiness is independent of symmetry and admissibility: both \(R_\varnothing\) and \(R_{\rm pl}\) satisfy those conditions.
5. The plenitude rule \(R=\mathsf A\) is an explicit **realisation axiom**, not a theorem derived from symmetry alone.
6. In a multi-orbit landscape, an admissibility rule must explain why an orbit belongs to \(\mathsf A\). Symmetry within an orbit does not choose among distinct orbits.

## Interpretation rule

If all checks pass, AP2A yields a disciplined result:

> Necessary plenitude can be formulated non-circularly as \(R=\mathsf A\), after \(\mathsf A\) is independently defined on a gauge quotient. But no-preference/symmetry does not derive this axiom or the nonempty admissibility domain.

This is a positive clarification and a negative derivation result. It says exactly what must be elevated to a postulate or derived by a future AP2B global-admissibility theory.
