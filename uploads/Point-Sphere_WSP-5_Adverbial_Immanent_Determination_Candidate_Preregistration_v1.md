# Point–Sphere WSP-5 Adverbial Immanent-Determination Candidate Preregistration v1
## \(F_{\rm WSP\text{-}5}\): fresh nonpartitive-determination candidate with proposed Q2–Q3 defenses

**Predecessor boundary:** \(F_{\rm WSP\text{-}4}\) is frozen at its recorded Q2–Q3 \(\mathsf{EntryIncomplete}\) disposition.  
**Purpose:** register a fresh candidate grammar that supplies proposed, independently stated defenses for (i) nonpartitivity and (ii) a real, subject-bound, nonentity category.  
**Status:** preregistration only. No entry result, Q-gate execution, actual-field bridge, selector discharge, public bridge, or framework-selection operation is authorized.

## 0. Freshness and restricted aim

This is not a repair to WSP-4. It changes the category grammar, the parthood application principle, and the positive account of a subject-bound determination:

\[
F_{\rm WSP\text{-}5}
\neq
F_{\rm WSP\text{-}4},
\qquad
\mathsf{ChangedCore}_{\rm WSP4}
\Longrightarrow
\mathsf{FreshCandidate}(F_{\rm WSP\text{-}5}).
\tag{WSP5-FRESHNESS}
\]

The aim is narrow. WSP-5 proposes that one weakly simple bearer can be really determinate in irreducibly subject-bound manners without those manners being constituent entities or property-objects. It does **not** claim to have shown that every metaphysical theory must recognize this category.

\[
\mathsf{ProposedDefense}_{\rm WSP5}
\not\Rightarrow
\mathsf{Established}(\mathsf{WSP5\text{-}Nonpartitivity})
\land
\mathsf{Established}(\mathsf{WSP5\text{-}RealNonentityCategory}).
\tag{WSP5-NO-PREMATURE-CREDIT}
\]

## 1. Nonspatial Point–Sphere designations

\(G\) is the one ultimate bearer. “Point” designates \(G\), and “Sphere” designates its closed field of intrinsic determinations. Neither designation carries literal spatial, temporal, or container content:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf{PointDesignation}(G)&\not\Rightarrow
\mathsf{SpatialLocation}(G)
\lor\mathsf{TemporalInitialState}(G),\\
\mathsf{SphereDesignation}(G,\Sigma_G)&\not\Rightarrow
\mathsf{SpatialObject}(\Sigma_G)
\lor\mathsf{ExternalContainer}(\Sigma_G,G)\\
&\hspace{56mm}\lor\mathsf{IndependentEntity}(\Sigma_G).
\end{aligned}
\tag{WSP5-NONSPATIAL}
\]

The diagram is not an actuality or world-production claim:

\[
\mathsf{PointSphereDiagram}(G,\Sigma_G)
\not\Rightarrow
\mathsf{ActualFiniteWorld}
\lor
\mathsf{PublicBridgePass}.
\tag{WSP5-DIAGRAM-LIMIT}
\]

## 2. Weak simplicity and typed category grammar

Weak simplicity is retained as a negative unity thesis:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf{WeakSimple}_5(G)
\iff{}&
\mathsf{OneUltimateBearer}(G)
\land
\neg\exists x\,\mathsf{ProperPartOf}(x,G)\\
&\land
\neg\exists x\,\mathsf{IndependentConstituentOf}(x,G)
\land
\neg\mathsf{SpatiallyExtended}(G)\\
&\land
\neg\mathsf{TemporallyProduced}(G).
\end{aligned}
\tag{WSP5-WEAK-SIMPLICITY}
\]

It is not strong simplicity:

\[
\mathsf{WeakSimple}_5(G)
\not\Rightarrow
\neg\exists d\,\mathsf{RealImmanentMode}(d,G).
\tag{WSP5-NOT-STRONG}
\]

WSP-5 distinguishes three primitive types. The distinction is categorical, not a claim that each type denotes an independent ultimate bearer:

\[
\mathbf B\quad\text{(bearers)},
\qquad
\mathbf E\quad\text{(entity-like constituent candidates)},
\qquad
\mathbf M\quad\text{(subject-bound immanent modes)}.
\tag{WSP5-TYPES}
\]

\[
\mathbf B\cap\mathbf E
=
\mathbf B\cap\mathbf M
=
\mathbf E\cap\mathbf M
=\varnothing,
\qquad
G\in\mathbf B.
\tag{WSP5-TYPE-SEPARATION}
\]

An entity-like constituent candidate is a node that can enter a constitution relation, whether or not it is dependent on its bearer:

\[
\mathsf{ConstitutiveNode}(x)
\iff
x\in\mathbf E
\land
\mathsf{NumericallyIndividuable}(x).
\tag{WSP5-CONSTITUTIVE-NODE}
\]

No bearer-independence condition occurs in this definition. Thus a numerically individuable dependent trope or nonseparable constituent, if admitted by a rival ontology, is not excluded from the category of candidate constituent merely because it depends essentially on a bearer.

## 3. Q2 defense: the Constitutive-Node Principle

### 3.1 The independently stated application condition for parthood

The candidate's proposed parthood principle is:

\[
\boxed{
\mathsf{ProperPartOf}(x,y)
\Longrightarrow
\mathsf{ConstitutiveNode}(x)
\land
\mathsf{ConstitutiveWhole}(y)
\land
\mathsf{ConstitutiveContributor}(x,y).
}
\tag{CNP}
\]

The third term is now fixed rather than left undefined:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf{ConstitutiveContributor}(x,y)
\iff{}&
\mathsf{ConstitutiveNode}(x)
\land x\neq y\\
&\land\mathsf{ConstitutionalDependence}(y,x)
\land\mathsf{EligibleForConstitutionalCounting}(x,y).
\end{aligned}
\tag{CONTRIBUTOR}
\]

\(\mathsf{ConstitutionalDependence}(y,x)\) means that \(x\) occurs as a typed relatum in the candidate's constitution profile for \(y\); \(\mathsf{EligibleForConstitutionalCounting}\) means that the profile can count \(x\) as one constituent rather than merely characterize the manner in which \(y\) is.

### 3.2 Rationale independent of the WSP target

The Constitutive-Node Principle is offered as a general application condition for a **constitutive** use of parthood: parthood says that a whole is constituted from units that are available for constitutional counting, not merely that the whole is intrinsically a certain way.

\[
\mathsf{ConstitutiveParthoodQuestion}(x,y)
\Longrightarrow
\mathsf{Askable}
\left(
\text{“Which constituent node is }x\text{ in the constitution of }y\text{?”}
\right).
\tag{CNP-RATIONALE}
\]

This rationale does not start from the desired conclusion that WSP modes are not parts. It applies before WSP is considered, for example to ordinary component/whole cases, to rival trope-like constituents, and to a merely descriptive predicate.

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\textbf{case} & \mathsf{ConstitutiveNode}? & \textbf{CNP consequence}\\
\hline
\text{ordinary component} & \mathsf{yes} & \text{eligible for a parthood question}\\
\text{dependent trope-like item} & \mathsf{yes},\text{ if numerically individuable} & \text{not excluded by dependence alone}\\
\text{mere predicate inscription} & \mathsf{no} & \text{not a part of its subject}\\
\text{subject-bound manner of being} & \text{to be argued in §4} & \text{not settled by dependence alone}
\end{array}
\tag{CNP-RIVAL-CASE-TEST}
\]

The candidate therefore does not rely on the WSP-4 inference that an item lacks objectual identity merely because its identity is tied to a bearer.

\[
\mathsf{EssentialBearerDependence}(x,G)
\not\Rightarrow
\neg\mathsf{ConstitutiveNode}(x).
\tag{NO-DEPENDENCE-SHORTCUT}
\]

### 3.3 Q2 bridge conditional on the mode-category defense

The nonpartitivity conclusion is conditional on the independently specified mode grammar of §4, not on a bare exclusion clause:

\[
\mathsf{RealImmanentMode}(d,G)
\land
\neg\mathsf{ConstitutiveNode}(d)
\Longrightarrow
\neg\mathsf{ProperPartOf}(d,G).
\tag{Q2-BRIDGE}
\]

This makes the burden explicit: the Q2 defense succeeds only if the proposed mode category is shown to be real without becoming a constitution-countable entity.

## 4. Q3 defense: adverbial real-immanent determination

### 4.1 The positive category

A subject-bound mode is not a free-standing property token. It is an irreducible **manner in which the bearer is**, expressed by the typed predication form \(d@G\). The notation does not introduce an additional entity as the bearer of \(d\):

\[
\mathsf{ModeOccurrence}(d@G)
\iff
 d\in\mathbf M
\land G\in\mathbf B
\land\mathsf{Determines}(d,G).
\tag{MODE-OCCURRENCE}
\]

Its reality condition is positive and explicitly non-descriptive:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf{RealImmanentMode}(d,G)
\iff{}&
\mathsf{ModeOccurrence}(d@G)
\land\mathsf{IntrinsicEssenceConstraint}(d,G)\\
&\land\mathsf{GroundsIntrinsicPredication}(G,d)\\
&\land\neg\mathsf{MerelyLinguisticPredicate}(d@G).
\end{aligned}
\tag{ADVERBIAL-REALITY}
\]

\(\mathsf{IntrinsicEssenceConstraint}(d,G)\) means that the manner is constitutive of the specified intrinsic profile of \(G\), not an observed finite effect or a convention for describing \(G\). \(\mathsf{GroundsIntrinsicPredication}(G,d)\) means that the truth of an intrinsic predication of \(G\) is grounded by \(G\)'s being in manner \(d\), rather than by a second object attached to \(G\).

### 4.2 The Adverbial Grounding Principle

The proposed independently stated warrant is:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&\mathsf{IntrinsicPredication}(\varphi,G)
\land
\mathsf{EssentiallyGroundedIn}(\varphi,G,d)\\
&\hspace{13mm}\Longrightarrow
\mathsf{GroundsIntrinsicPredication}(G,d)
\text{ is admissible without positing a further entity }e
\text{ such that }\mathsf{Has}(G,e).
\end{aligned}
}
\tag{AGP}
\]

The principle is not a claim that every intrinsic predication has an adverbial analysis. It states the more limited category option that a real way of being can ground an intrinsic truth through the bearer itself, without automatically adding a property-object to the ontology.

Its rationale is independent of Point–Sphere imagery: a theory of intrinsic predication must distinguish the bearer being a way from the bearer standing in an external possession relation to another item. The distinction is applicable to non-WSP cases, including any putative intrinsic feature of an ordinary bearer.

\[
\mathsf{AGP}\text{ is contestable}
\quad\land\quad
\mathsf{AGP}\text{ is not defined by the desired WSP conclusion}.
\tag{AGP-INDEPENDENCE-CLAIM}
\]

### 4.3 Nonentity test and trope contrast

The category is not merely declared nonentity-like. Its proposed application conditions block the objectual grammar characteristic of a property-instance:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf{RealImmanentMode}(d,G)
\Longrightarrow{}&
\neg\mathsf{ConstitutiveNode}(d)
\land
\neg\mathsf{GroundBearer}(d)\\
&\land
\neg\mathsf{BearerIndependentCounting}(d)
\land
\neg\mathsf{HasOwnConstitutionProfile}(d).
\end{aligned}
\tag{MODE-NONENTITY-TEST}
\]

A trope-like/property-instance rival is not ruled out by fiat. It is classified differently because it is offered as a numerically individuable node capable of entering constitution or possession profiles:

\[
\mathsf{TropeLike}(t,G)
\Longrightarrow
\mathsf{ConstitutiveNode}(t)
\lor
\mathsf{PossessionRelatum}(t,G).
\tag{TROPE-CONTRAST}
\]

Thus WSP-5 draws a positive contrast:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\textbf{category} & \textbf{grounds an intrinsic predication} & \textbf{is a constitution-countable node}\\
\hline
\text{mere description} & \mathsf{no} & \mathsf{no}\\
\text{trope/property-instance} & \text{possibly} & \mathsf{yes}\\
\text{real immanent mode} & \mathsf{yes, through }G & \mathsf{no}
\end{array}
\tag{THREE-WAY-CATEGORY-CONTRAST}
\]

The distinction needs later scrutiny. WSP-5 does not claim that a formal type declaration alone proves the metaphysical category.

### 4.4 Subject-bound identity without a transferable property object

The candidate distinguishes a mode-form from a free property object. Its identity rule is typed at the level of determinate manners-of-being:

\[
\mathsf{SameModeOccurrence}(d@G,e@H)
\iff
G=H
\land
\mathsf{SameModeForm}(d,e;G).
\tag{MODE-IDENTITY}
\]

This says that the occurrence is inseparable from the bearer whose intrinsic manner it is. It does not infer that all bearer-dependent items lack objectual identity; that question is decided by the constitutive-node conditions, not by nontransfer alone.

\[
\mathsf{SameModeOccurrence}(d@G,e@H)
\not\Rightarrow
\neg\mathsf{ConstitutiveNode}(d)
\quad\text{without \((\mathrm{MODE\text{-}NONENTITY\text{-}TEST})\).}
\tag{NO-NONTRANSFER-SHORTCUT}
\]

## 5. Positive formal field and non-outcome-loaded discernibility

The Sphere is a closed typed field of real immanent modes:

\[
\Sigma_G=\langle D_G,R_G\rangle,
\qquad
D_G\subseteq\mathbf M,
\qquad
R_G\subseteq D_G\times\mathsf{RelType}\times D_G.
\tag{WSP5-FIELD}
\]

\[
\mathsf{SphereDesignation}(G,\Sigma_G)
\iff
\mathsf{WeakSimple}_5(G)
\land
D_G\neq\varnothing
\land
\forall d\in D_G\,\mathsf{RealImmanentMode}(d,G)
\land
\mathsf{Closed}(D_G,R_G).
\tag{WSP5-SPHERE}
\]

Closure is endpoint closure only:

\[
\mathsf{Closed}(D_G,R_G)
\iff
\forall\langle d,\ell,e\rangle\in R_G
[d\in D_G\land e\in D_G\land\ell\in\mathsf{RelType}].
\tag{WSP5-CLOSURE}
\]

The formal discernibility rule is fixed by incidence position, not by actual finite effects, experiences, observations, or desired Point–Sphere outcomes:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf{FormallyDiscernible}_{R_G}(d,e)
\iff{}&
 d\neq e
\land\exists\ell,z\,[
 (d,\ell,z)\in R_G\not\leftrightarrow(e,\ell,z)\in R_G\\
&\hspace{37mm}\lor
 (z,\ell,d)\in R_G\not\leftrightarrow(z,\ell,e)\in R_G].
\end{aligned}
\tag{WSP5-DISCERNIBILITY}
\]

\[
\mathsf{FormallyDiscernible}_{R_G}(d,e)
\not\iff
\mathsf{DifferentFiniteEffects}(d,e)
\lor
\mathsf{ActualAppearance}(d)
\lor
\mathsf{ActualAppearance}(e).
\tag{WSP5-NO-OUTCOME-LOADING}
\]

A finite field exemplar, if later supplied, must use only this typed incidence grammar to establish formal difference. Its contents are not presently selected.
