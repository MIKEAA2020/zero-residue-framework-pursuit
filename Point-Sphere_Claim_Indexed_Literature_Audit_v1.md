# Point–Sphere Claim-Indexed Literature Audit v1
## Ground stack, modality, consciousness, creation, and ethical burden

## Method

Each entry identifies a framework claim, its current status, a source-backed reason for keeping the distinction live, and the limit of what the source supplies. Literature can establish that a premise, objection, or family of views is real and contested. It cannot turn an optional premise into a theorem.

This is the first claim-indexed stage promised by the orientation audit. It focuses on the core premises that bear the most downstream weight; AQFT, quantum interpretation, empirical consciousness science, and detailed creation/concurrence scholarship remain later modules.

## A. Grounding and aseity stack

| Framework claim | Status after audit | Source-backed relevance | What remains framework-specific or open |
|---|---|---|---|
| **F0: grounding needs explicit semantics** | Retained | The grounding literature distinguishes explanation-based and determination-based conceptions and distinguishes partial from full ground. See Bliss & Trogdon, “[Metaphysical Grounding](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/grounding/)”. | The choice of fact domain, full-ground substitution, bearer semantics, and modal range is not settled by this taxonomy. |
| **F1: derivative ancestry has a local root** | Conditional theorem | Well-foundedness is a substantive issue in grounding theory, not a notational consequence. The same SEP survey treats strict ordering and well-foundedness as debated theoretical commitments. | The framework’s local-root theorem requires its own asymmetric/well-founded/dependence-composition premises. No source proves those premises universally. |
| **F2: one global foundation fact** | Conditional theorem | The cosmological/PSR tradition documents strengthened explanatory demands reaching beyond local causal series; see “[Principle of Sufficient Reason](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/sufficient-reason/)” and “[Cosmological Argument](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/cosmological-argument/)”. | Totality closure, full-ground substitution, and the single-foundation axiom are stronger than a bare PSR and remain explicit framework commitments. |
| **F3: fact-to-bearer transfer** | Open bridge | Grounding discussions commonly distinguish facts and objects and warn that grounding claims need regimentation. | TB1–TB4, exact-bearer uniqueness, and composition reflection are bespoke bridge principles. They require focused truthmaker/bearer literature and formal countermodels, not historical citation alone. |
| **F4: external non-derivativeness yields necessary existence** | Conditional theorem | Modal sources distinguish necessity, possibility, and contingency; see “[The Epistemology of Modality](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/modality-epistemology/)”. | MD/explanatory contingency is not a logical truth. It is the framework’s stated premise excluding an externally ungrounded but modally optional bearer. |
| **F5: strong aseity / essential closure** | Conditional theorem, repaired | Grounding literature recognises proposed links between essence and ground while also stressing that essence is not merely modality; see “[Metaphysical Grounding](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/grounding/)” and Fine, “[Unified Foundations for Essence and Ground](https://doi.org/10.1017/apa.2014.26)”. | MI*, exhaustive explanatory location, hybrid-explanation exclusion, essence completion, and rigid de re identity remain substantive. No cited source proves that an actual ground’s essence explains its necessary existence. |

### Audit conclusion for the stack

The literature reinforces the master’s premise pricing. It does **not** reduce the price. In particular, the PSR is not a theorem of logic: the SEP characterises unrestricted PSR as rejecting brute facts, which is precisely the substantive governing commitment the framework must label. Nor does standard grounding theory turn a unique foundation fact into a unique concrete bearer, or modal necessity into essence-based self-explanation.

## B. Ontological arguments and actualisation

**Framework claim:** a coherent or complete concept of a self-existent being does not establish an actual concrete bearer.

**Audit result:** retained and sharpened. The SEP’s “[Ontological Arguments](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ontological-arguments/)” makes explicit that modal ontological arguments need at least a possibility premise, a necessity premise, and an appropriate modal logic. The core issue is not whether an opponent can display a contradiction in a description; it is whether objective metaphysical possibility and the relevant modal principles have been independently justified.

**Integration consequence:** the master’s distinction is correct. The F1–F5 route begins, conditionally, with actual concrete facts and a bearer bridge. A modal ontological argument begins with modal/possibility claims. Neither route may borrow its conclusion from the other without stating the connection.

## D. Creation, simplicity, and finite reality

**Framework claim:** necessary existence and strong aseity do not decide C-Nec, C-Cont, or strict ND.

**Audit result:** retained. The SEP’s “[Divine Simplicity](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/divine-simplicity/)” documents why essence/existence identity, attribute identity, divine freedom, and modal status generate major coherence and modal-creation questions. “[God and Other Necessary Beings](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/god-necessary-being/)” further shows why necessary existence is not by itself a unique personal-creator identifier.

**Integration consequence:** C-Nec still needs a realisation rule and inherits its exact modal cost; C-Cont still needs a selector/non-necessitating-grounding account; ND still needs a manifestation and individuation account. These are framework comparison results, not settled by appeals to classical theism.

**Historical restraint:** Leibniz is not evidence that finite monads are merely divine thoughts. “[Leibniz on Causation](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/leibniz-causation/)” presents created substances as dependent on God while also assigning them activity. Modern perspectival-monist reconstruction must therefore be labelled reconstruction, not textual report.

## E. Ethics and suffering

**Framework claim:** sourcehood, necessity, non-duality, or privation talk does not itself defeat the reality of suffering or produce a theodicy.

**Audit result:** retained. “[The Problem of Evil](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/evil/)” treats undesirable states of affairs as potential evidential challenge to theism; “[Theodicies](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/theodicies/)” identifies theodicies as attempts to reconcile divine justice/goodness with concrete harms. “[Divine Providence](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/providence-divine/)” distinguishes logical consistency questions from further justificatory claims.

**Integration consequence:** the master’s permission/burden conditions are appropriate as candidate constraints. They are not refuted by simply describing evil as privation, and they are not satisfied merely by invoking unknown goods, free will, natural law, or future repair.
