# Probability and Constants Bridge v1
## A conditional Born-weight result and a non-vacuous selection protocol

## Result in one sentence

A branch/plenitude proposal obtains Born weights only after it supplies a normalised additive measure on decoherent alternatives; and a constants/observer-selection proposal has no predictive content until its base measure and selection function are fixed independently, since an unconstrained selection weight can reproduce any target distribution.

---

## 1. Two liabilities that must not be merged

The Point–Sphere programme invokes grades, relational records, and possible plenitude. None of these, by itself, supplies either:

1. a probability rule for alternatives within the actual physical regime; or
2. a probability distribution over possible laws and constants.

The first is a quantum-probability problem. The second is a theory-space measure problem. A branch count, a complexity weight, an observer-selection factor, and a Born measure answer different questions and cannot be exchanged without an argument.

---

## 2. Conditional Born-weight theorem

Consider a declared decoherent event algebra with mutually exclusive coarse-grained alternatives \(i\). Let \(x_i=|c_i|^2\) be the squared amplitude associated with an alternative in a specified orthogonal branch decomposition. Suppose its credence or chance weight is \(w(x_i)\).

### Assumptions

- **B1 — Normalisation:** \(w(1)=1\) and \(w(0)=0\).
- **B2 — Decoherent coarse-graining additivity:** for mutually exclusive alternatives whose squared amplitudes are \(x,y\),
  \[
  w(x+y)=w(x)+w(y)\qquad (x,y\geq0,\ x+y\leq1).
  \]
- **B3 — Continuity:** \(w\) is continuous on \([0,1]\).
- **B4 — State-description invariance:** weights depend only on the squared-amplitude weight of a declared decoherent event, not on a relabelling or a physically irrelevant refinement of its record description.

### Theorem — Conditional uniqueness

Under B1–B4,
\[
w(x)=x\quad\text{for every }x\in[0,1].
\]

#### Proof

B2 gives \(w(m/n)=m w(1/n)\). Since \(1=n(1/n)\), B1 yields \(w(1/n)=1/n\), hence \(w(m/n)=m/n\) for rational \(m/n\in[0,1]\). B3 extends the equality from the rationals to all reals in the interval. \(\square\)

The theorem is useful because it locates the burden exactly. It does **not** derive B2 or B4 from branching, decoherence, observer counting, perspectivality, or non-dual metaphysics. A programme that rejects Born weighting must reject at least one stated premise and provide a rival measure with empirical consequences.

## 3. A measure-dependence theorem for constants and plenitude

Let \(\Lambda\) be a specified measurable space of candidate low-energy theories or dimensionless-constant vectors \(\lambda\). Let \(\mu\) be a base measure representing the proposal's pre-selection weight, and let \(a(\lambda)\geq 0\) be an observer/admissibility/realisation selection factor. Where finite and nonzero, the conditioned prediction is
\[
\pi_{\mu,a}(A)=
\frac{\int_A a(\lambda)\,d\mu(\lambda)}{\int_\Lambda a(\lambda)\,d\mu(\lambda)}.
\]

### Theorem — Reweighting flexibility

Suppose \(\mu\) has support covering a target probability distribution \(q\) on \(\Lambda\), with \(q\ll\mu\). Then an admissible selection weight can reproduce \(q\): take
\[
a(\lambda)=\frac{dq}{d\mu}(\lambda)
\]
(up to an arbitrary positive normalising constant). It follows that \(\pi_{\mu,a}=q\).

#### Proof

Substitution gives \(a\,d\mu=dq\); normalisation yields \(q\). \(\square\)

An unconstrained observer-selection or plenitude factor therefore has enough freedom to fit any distribution absolutely continuous with the chosen base measure. This is not a defect of selection reasoning as such; it is a proof that unfixed selection reasoning is not yet predictive.

## 4. Non-vacuous selection protocol

A proposed derivation or explanation of constants must state, before confronting the target values:

1. **Theory space \(\Lambda\):** the exact mathematical family, coordinate chart, allowed effective theories, and cutoffs.
2. **Base measure \(\mu\):** its formula, normalisation or regularisation, symmetry rationale, and treatment of reparameterisation.
3. **Selection factor \(a\):** a physical or explicitly observer-theoretic derivation; it cannot be chosen after seeing the constants.
4. **Conditioning information \(C\):** precisely which facts are conditioned upon. “Observers exist” is inadequate unless operationalised without coding the target constants.
5. **Observable map:** the renormalisation/low-energy map from \(\lambda\) to the reported dimensionless constants \(O(\lambda)\).
6. **Prior-predictive regions:** declared measurement regions \(R_j\) and their probabilities
   \[
   \Pi_T(R_j\mid C)=\pi_{\mu,a}(O^{-1}(R_j)\mid C).
   \]
7. **Comparator models:** the same items for at least one non-plenitude or alternative-measure competitor.

Exact observed real numbers ordinarily have zero measure. Tests must therefore use a declared finite-precision region, an independently motivated relation, or a future observable not used in fixing \(\mu\), \(a\), or \(C\).

### Prospective endpoints

A proposal earns empirical content only if it fixes a future discriminator. Examples include:

- a sign, ordering, or exact relation among not-yet-measured dimensionless observables;
- a predeclared interval with stated prior-predictive mass;
- an out-of-sample Bayes factor against named comparator measures;
- a low-energy relation recovered from the restricted mathematical family without fitted selection parameters.

Failure occurs when the observed future result lies in a preregistered low-probability region, the selection function must be modified after disclosure, or an equally simple reweighting of a rival measure yields the same apparent success.

## 5. Relation to creation, plenitude, and non-duality

A metaphysical plenitude claim must be disambiguated:

- **Admissibility:** a mode is internally coherent or allowed by a framework.
- **Realisation:** the mode is concrete in some world/grade.
- **Actuality:** the mode obtains in this physical history.
- **Weight:** the mode receives a probability, measure, or explanatory priority.

No implication from admissibility to realisation, or from realisation to equal weight, is logically automatic. In a literal-creation model, God may actualise a selected totality without making every admissible mode concrete. In a non-dual model, derivative modes may be conventionally or dependently real without constituting an equally weighted multiverse. Either architecture needs a distinct selection principle if it makes numerical or probabilistic claims.

The bridge therefore preserves the symbolic thought of plenitude while blocking an illicit inference from “the ground is inexhaustible” to “every describable possibility exists with a useful probability.”

---

## 6. What this achieves and what it does not

### Achieved

- A short conditional uniqueness proof that identifies the exact assumptions yielding Born weights.
- A general reweighting theorem showing why an unfixed observer/selection function can fit arbitrary constant distributions.
- A concrete preregistration protocol by which a landscape, plenitude, or global-selection theory could become empirically discriminating.

### Not achieved

- A derivation of B2/B4, Hilbert-space quantum theory, or the Born rule from the Point–Sphere metaphysics.
- A preferred measure on theory space.
- A derivation of the observed constants.
- Evidence that all admissible modes are real, or that any one metaphysical architecture is selected.

The legitimate advance is constraint: it identifies the additional mathematics and prospective risk needed before probability or plenitude claims count as physical explanation.
