## Conditional identity theorem

The valid result remains:

\[
C(G)\land W(G)\land E(G,\mathcal A)\land I(G,\mathcal A)
\Rightarrow
\forall a\in\mathcal A\;R(a).
\]

Here \(I\) is the crucial non-representational identity bridge: genuine self-knowledge of \(a\) is its actual self-presentation as a mode. Once that is explicit, realization follows without a subsequent selector.

This is P-internal immanent plenitude, not an inference that every coherent formula, mathematical object, possible world, or formal grammar is real.

## Exact results

### 1. Complete self-presence does not by itself give finite aspects

The bare-self-presence model preserves \(C(G)\) and \(W(G)\), but contains neither a nonempty finite \(\mathcal A\), exhaustivity over such a domain, nor non-representational identity.

Thus:

\[
C(G)\land W(G)
\not\models
\mathcal A\neq\varnothing,
\]

\[
C(G)\land W(G)
\not\models E(G,\mathcal A),
\]

\[
C(G)\land W(G)
\not\models I(G,\mathcal A).
\]

This is the exact answer to the claim that simplicity/completeness alone forces finite articulation. A further bridge is needed.

### 2. Exhaustive representation is not realization

The representational model knows every item in a declared domain while realizing only one. Therefore:

\[
E(G,\mathcal A)
\not\models
\mathsf{MEC}(\mathcal A)
\]

without \(I\).

The Point–Sphere response is not to deny this model exists as a rival form. It is to deny that it captures the intended nondual identity of self-knowledge and modehood. That denial is precisely the substantive bridge \(I\), not a consequence of the bare word “knowledge.”

### 3. Full realization can conceal a domain restriction

The restricted-domain model satisfies MEC relative to its declared one-element domain, but that domain has been selected from a larger candidate \(\Omega\). Hence:

\[
\mathsf{MEC}(\mathcal A)
\not\models
\mathcal A\text{ is independently genuine or maximally inclusive}.
\]

A proof of plenitude needs an independent criterion for genuine aspect-content. It cannot define “genuine aspect” as “whatever the ground realizes.”

### 4. Realization does not solve compossibility

Even a full realization support needs a context rule for incompatible finite contents. The prior maximal-proper-context discipline is relevant but does not itself determine which contents are genuine aspects or how all contexts are typed.

### 5. Non-composition remains a typing constraint

The formal model can only record the constraint:

\[
	ext{finite aspect articulation}
\neq
	ext{parts, properties, or changing states of }G\text{ qua }G.
\]

It cannot prove this metaphysical claim by treating a finite set as a literal model of the ground. Any positive account must preserve the user’s correction: there are not two ontological levels; ground-description and finite-aspect description concern one reality without converting finite relations into intrinsic ground structure.
