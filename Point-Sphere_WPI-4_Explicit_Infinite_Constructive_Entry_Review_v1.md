## 2. Model-regime coherence

### 2.1 Global infinitude is now explicit rather than contradicted

The WPI-3 defect was that an infinite code/constructor universe and unbounded first-stage closure were paired with a full finite-model certificate. WPI-4 instead declares:

\[
\mathbf C=\{0,1\}^{*},
\qquad
\mathbf A=\{a_p:p\in\mathbf C\},
\qquad
\mathbf B=\{G\}\cup\{b_{p,i}:p\in\mathbf C,i\in\mathbb N\},
\tag{Infinite-Sorts}
\]

within an explicitly infinite regime, and no longer demands enumeration of the entire global closure in a finite structure.

\[
\mathsf{FiniteFull}
\not=
\mathsf{ExplicitInfinite}.
\tag{No-Regime-Conflation}
\]

The packet also identifies the intended global closure:

\[
H_\omega=\bigcup_{n<\omega}H_n,
\tag{Global-Closure}
\]

and declares, rather than assumes, its intended countable cardinality.

### 2.2 Finite slices do not replace the global model

For every finite fixture \(p\), the packet defines a dependency closure \(D(p)\) using only finite branching and strictly shorter predecessor/content/successor codes. The proposed test slice is:

\[
L(p)
=
\langle H_0,D(p),\text{associated finite constructors and decoded data}\rangle.
\tag{Slice}
\]

The necessary local/global claim is stated explicitly:

\[
\mathsf{Verdict}_{U3}(L(p))
\Longleftrightarrow
\mathsf{Verdict}_{U3}(\mathfrak M_\infty),
\tag{Slice-Bridge}
\]

with analogous stated equivalences for actual articulation, finite entityhood, and actual presentation.

This is not yet proved. It is correctly registered as the U0 burden, not smuggled in as an accomplished result.

\[

=

\tag{MR-Entry}
