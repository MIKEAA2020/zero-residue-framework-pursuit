# Point–Sphere Foundation Stress Test and Execution Order
## Dependency-first, feasibility-prioritised work package

## Purpose

This work package executes the first remaining priority in the master: stress-test the priced ground stack before attempting downstream claims about creation, awareness, phenomenality, plenitude, or physics. It also fixes the exact work order. A downstream bridge may be explored conditionally, but it cannot be counted as support for its prerequisites.

The main result is a repair to the strong-aseity/essential-closure bridge. The prior version correctly distinguished external non-derivativeness from strong aseity. Its compact formulation, however, could leave a **mixed explanation** unaddressed: an explanation partly located in the ground’s nature and partly in an external fact. The repair makes explanatory location exhaustive and excludes externally located explanations, not only a single wholly external ground.

## 2. Formal stress test of the ground stack

### 2.1 F0: stipulated base

The stack must declare:

1. an actual fact domain \(F\), its closure conditions, and whether it includes modal, grounding, and bearer facts;
2. a proper-grounding relation \(\prec\) that is asymmetric and well-founded on the relevant ancestry;
3. what a complete immediate ground-set is and the substitution principle that permits transitive complete grounding;
4. a modal range \(R\) and cross-world identity/rigidity conditions; and
5. whether a full explanation may be mixed—partly intrinsic to \(G\), partly not.

Without these, terms such as “ultimate,” “complete explanation,” and “necessary” change meaning between premises.

### 2.2 F1: local root

**Result.** If the ancestry of an actually derivative item \(d\) is non-empty, well-founded under proper grounding, and dependence-composites have a proper ground, there is a minimal non-derivative item in that ancestry. With the additional composition principle, that item is non-composite in the dependence-generating sense.

**Countermodel if well-foundedness is removed.** Let \(\cdots \prec d_3\prec d_2\prec d_1\prec d\). Every item has a proper ground. There is no minimal ancestor. Local explanation may be accepted link-by-link, but no local non-derivative root follows.

**Limit.** F1 neither globalises the root nor yields a bearer, simplicity of an entity, necessary existence, or awareness.

### 2.3 F2: global unique foundation fact

Let \(M\) be the facts in \(F\) with no \(\prec\)-predecessor. Well-foundedness and non-emptiness give at least one member of \(M\). The single-foundation axiom
\[
|M|>1\;\Longrightarrow\;\exists m\in M\,\exists z\in F\;(z\prec m)
\]
therefore makes \(M\) a singleton. Under full-ground substitution, its sole member can be a transitive complete ground for the stipulated fact domain.

**What this really costs.** The axiom is a direct anti-plural-root commitment. It does not follow from the ordinary thought that no two roots are wholly independent: two roots may be primitively related or co-foundational. The “global” result also depends on the domain closure and on the exact kind of full-ground substitution used.

**Countermodel if the single-foundation axiom is weakened.** Let two root facts \(r_1,r_2\) have no proper grounds and jointly full-ground all other facts. They may be connected by a primitive symmetric relation without either grounding the other. Well-foundedness holds, yet global uniqueness fails.

### 2.4 F3: from fact to actual bearer

The exact-bearer semantics must supply TB1–TB4: an actual exact bearer of the foundation fact, bearer uniqueness, transfer from fact-level to entity-level grounding, and no distinct entity externally grounds the bearer’s actuality.

**Countermodel without composition reflection.** A primitive composite entity \(x=(a,b,u)\) exactly bears a simple fact \(f\), while its part/unity structure is not represented in the admitted fact-grounding relation. \(f\) may be non-composite although \(x\) is not. Hence non-composition of the foundation fact does not imply entity simplicity.

**Countermodel without exactness.** Distinct partial truthmakers \(x\) and \(y\) bear different aspects of \(f_\bullet\). A generic bearer-existence claim gives no unique foundation entity.

### 2.5 F4: necessary existence

With actual external non-derivativeness, the MD premise says that an actual entity that could fail to exist somewhere in \(R\) has a distinct external ground of its actual existence. The contradiction with TB4 yields \(\Box_R\operatorname{Ex}(G)\).

**Countermodel without MD.** In each world of \(R\) where \(G\) exists, no distinct entity grounds its existence; in another accessible world \(G\) does not exist. This is an externally non-derivative but modally optional ground. No contradiction follows until MD or a stronger fact-first modal premise is adopted.

**Limit.** Necessary existence in \(R\) does not give a unique metaphysically absolute necessity claim until \(R\) is motivated, and it does not give strong aseity.

## 3. Repairing F5: strong aseity and essential closure

### 3.1 The mixed-explanation gap

The abbreviated premises “every explanation is external or intrinsic” and “no external fact fully grounds \(p\)” can be read too weakly. Consider:

\[
\{\mathcal E_G,q\}\mathrel{\mathsf{FG}}\Box_R\operatorname{Ex}(G),
\]

where \(q\) is not internally grounded in \(\mathcal E_G\), but neither \(q\) alone nor \(\mathcal E_G\) alone fully grounds the modal fact. No single external fact fully grounds it. Thus the old weak reading of external closure would not exclude a hybrid explanation, while the conclusion that \(\mathcal E_G\) alone fully grounds necessity would be invalid.

### 3.2 Corrected location and closure premises

Fix a complete explanation \(X\) of an ultimate fact \(p\). Define:

- \(\operatorname{Int}_G(X)\): every member of \(X\) is intrinsic to \(G\) or fully grounded by \(\mathcal E_G\).
- \(\operatorname{Ext}_G(X)\): at least one indispensable member of \(X\) is not intrinsic to \(G\) and not fully grounded by \(\mathcal E_G\).

The required premises are then:

- **MI\***: every \(p\in\mathcal U_G\) has a non-brute full explanation.
- **EL\***: each such explanation is either internally located or externally located in the above exhaustive sense.
- **EC\***: no full explanation of a member of \(\mathcal U_G\) is externally located; this excludes wholly external **and hybrid** explanations.
- **ECmp\***: if a full explanation is internally located, \(\mathcal E_G\) fully grounds every intrinsic explanatory constituent, and full-ground substitution holds.
- **RIG**: \(\mathcal E_G\) rigidly denotes the complete de re intrinsic nature of this very \(G\) throughout the stipulated range \(R\). This makes the modal target and its alleged essence-grounding about the same bearer.

### 3.3 Corrected conditional theorem

**Theorem.** Assume F3–F4 and MI\*, EL\*, EC\*, ECmp\*, and RIG. Then:
\[
\mathcal E_G\mathrel{\mathsf{FG}}\Box_R\operatorname{Ex}(G).
\]
Further, for every \(p\in\mathcal U_G\),
\[
\mathcal E_G\mathrel{\mathsf{FG}^{*}}p.
\]

**Proof.** Let \(p=\Box_R\operatorname{Ex}(G)\). By MI\*, \(p\) has a non-brute full explanation \(X\). EL\* classifies \(X\) as internally or externally located. EC\* eliminates the second option, including a mixed explanation. Thus \(X\) is internally located. ECmp\* and substitution yield \(\mathcal E_G\mathrel{\mathsf{FG}}p\). The same proof schema applies to every stipulated \(p\in\mathcal U_G\). RIG fixes the de re target across \(R\). \(\square\)

### 3.4 Limits that remain after the repair

1. **No premise-free proof.** MI\* is a substantive complete-intelligibility norm; it is not logic.
2. **No concept-to-actuality inference.** The theorem applies to a bearer made actual by F1–F4. It does not instantiate a merely described essence.
3. **No automatic modal collapse.** Actual grounding of \(p\) in \(\mathcal E_G\) does not yield \(\Box_R p\) without an additional modal-invariance or necessitation principle for that grounding relation.
4. **No necessary creation.** \(\mathcal U_G\) must be limited to genuinely ultimate-profile facts. If an actual derivative totality is included, its necessity still needs the further modal-transfer/realisation premises; it cannot be smuggled in through “essential closure.”
5. **No content derivation.** The theorem does not identify the essence or derive awareness, goodness, a selection law, quantum structure, or physical constants.

### Rule for future updates

A result may move from “open” to “conditional theorem” only when every non-logical premise is listed and a countermodel without it is given. It may move from “conditional theorem” to “supported theory” only with independent argument or evidence for those premises. It may never be upgraded merely because it is congenial to a faith-held core, metaphorically attractive, or compatible with an existing empirical model.
