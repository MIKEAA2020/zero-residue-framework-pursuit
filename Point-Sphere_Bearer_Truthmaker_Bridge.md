# Bearer–Truthmaker Bridge v1
## From a unique foundation fact to a unique ground entity—only under explicit bridging premises

## Result in one sentence

The global unique-foundation theorem yields a unique non-composite **fact** under its stated grounding premises. A unique actual **ground entity** follows only after adding an exact-bearer/truthmaker principle, an entity-to-fact grounding-transfer principle, and a non-derivative-bearer principle; neither simplicity, necessity, awareness, agency, nor creation follows without further premises.

---

## 1. The gap that the prior grounding theorem leaves open

The global theorem establishes, conditionally, an actual fact \(F_\bullet\) such that
\[
\forall d\in\mathcal D\quad F_\bullet\;\mathrel{\mathsf{FG}^{*}}\;d,
\]
where \(\mathcal D\) is the stipulated concrete fact-domain and \(F_\bullet\) is non-composite in the theorem's fact-level sense.

That result does **not** establish that \(F_\bullet\) is:

- the existence or nature of an entity;
- borne by one entity rather than a plurality, a world-totality, or a primitive fact;
- a simple entity rather than a fact about a complex entity;
- necessary, aware, personal, good, or creative.

A fact-first ontology can accept the global theorem while denying all entity talk. The following bridge makes the additional commitments visible.

---

## 2. Two-sorted bearer semantics

Use two primitive sorts:

- \(\mathsf{Ent}(x)\): \(x\) is an entity;
- \(\mathsf{Fact}(f)\): \(f\) is an actual or possible fact.

Let \(\mathsf{Act}(f)\) mean that \(f\) obtains, \(\mathsf{FG}^{*}(f,d)\) mean that \(f\) transitively fully grounds \(d\), \(\mathsf{Ex}(x)\) be the fact that \(x\) is actual, and \(\mathsf{B}(x,f)\) mean that \(x\) is the **exact bearer** of \(f\).

“Exact bearer” is deliberately stronger than weak truthmaking. It means that the actuality and nature of \(x\), in the relevant respect, are sufficient for \(f\), while no proper independent plurality is required to make \(f\) obtain. A table may weakly truthmake “something is in the room,” but it is not automatically the exact bearer of a fundamental totality fact.

Define entity-level grounding only through an explicit bridge:
\[
\mathsf{EG}(x,d)\;:\Longleftrightarrow\; \text{the being/nature of }x\text{ is an ontological ground of }d.
\]
It is not a mere causal relation and it is not stipulated by calling \(x\) a truthmaker.

---

## 3. The bearer bridge axioms

Assume the preceding global result:

\[
\tag{GF}
\mathsf{Act}(F_\bullet)\ \land\ \mathsf{Fund}(F_\bullet)\ \land\ \forall d\in\mathcal D\;\mathsf{FG}^{*}(F_\bullet,d).
\]

Here \(\mathsf{Fund}(f)\) says that \(f\) has no further full ground in the stipulated domain.

Add the following new premises.

### TB1 — Exact bearer existence
\[
\mathsf{Act}(F_\bullet)\land\mathsf{Fund}(F_\bullet)
\ \Rightarrow\ 
\exists x\,[\mathsf{Ent}(x)\land\mathsf{ActEnt}(x)\land\mathsf{B}(x,F_\bullet)].
\]

A fundamental actual foundation fact is borne by at least one actual entity. This rejects primitive-fact ontology for facts of this kind.

### TB2 — Bearer unity
\[
\mathsf{B}(x,F_\bullet)\land\mathsf{B}(y,F_\bullet)
\ \Rightarrow\ x=y.
\]

The foundation fact has one exact entity-bearer, not merely several partial truthmakers or a plural bearer. This is an entity-level anti-plurality premise.

### TB3 — Grounding transfer
\[
\mathsf{B}(x,F_\bullet)\land\mathsf{FG}^{*}(F_\bullet,d)
\ \Rightarrow\ \mathsf{EG}(x,d).
\]

Where the foundation fact fully grounds a concrete fact, its exact bearer grounds that fact in virtue of its being or nature. TB3 blocks a merely extensional “the whole world makes the totality fact true” move: an entity qualifies only if its being is genuinely explanatory rather than a summary of the facts to be explained.

### TB4 — Non-derivative bearer
\[
\mathsf{B}(x,F_\bullet)\land\mathsf{Fund}(F_\bullet)
\ \Rightarrow\ \neg\exists y\,[\mathsf{Ent}(y)\land y\ne x\land\mathsf{EG}(y,\mathsf{Ex}(x))].
\]

The exact bearer of a fundamental foundation fact has no **distinct external** entity-ground of its actuality. This permits an immanent account in terms of the bearer's own nature, while ruling out identification of the ground bearer with a mere totality or aggregate constituted by what it is supposed to explain.

TB4 need not say that \(x\) is necessary. It says only that it is non-derivative relative to \(\mathcal D\).

---

## 4. Conditional ground-entity theorem

### Theorem — Unique actual non-derivative ground entity

From GF and TB1–TB4, there exists exactly one actual entity \(g\) such that:
\[
\forall d\in\mathcal D\;\mathsf{EG}(g,d),
\]
and \(g\) is non-derivative relative to \(\mathcal D\).

#### Proof

GF and TB1 yield an actual entity \(g\) with \(\mathsf{B}(g,F_\bullet)\). For arbitrary \(d\in\mathcal D\), GF supplies \(\mathsf{FG}^{*}(F_\bullet,d)\); TB3 therefore gives \(\mathsf{EG}(g,d)\). TB4 supplies non-derivativeness relative to \(\mathcal D\). If \(h\) is another exact entity-bearer of \(F_\bullet\), TB2 gives \(h=g\). Thus there is exactly one such foundation-fact bearer, and it grounds every fact in the stipulated domain. \(\square\)

The conclusion is intentionally indexed to \(\mathcal D\) and to the foundation fact. It does not say that no other entities exist; it says that exactly one entity bears the unique foundation fact and is non-derivative relative to the domain it grounds.

## 5. Further conclusions require further bridges

### Simplicity

Fact-level non-composition does not entail entity-level simplicity. To infer it, add a **composition reflection** premise:
\[
\tag{CR}
\mathsf{Composite}(x)\land\mathsf{B}(x,f)
\Rightarrow
f\text{ has a non-trivial full-ground decomposition representing }x\text{'s parts and unification.}
\]

Then the non-composition of \(F_\bullet\) and \(\mathsf B(g,F_\bullet)\) imply that \(g\) is non-composite. CR is controversial: a theorist may hold that a simple fact can concern a complex entity, or that part-whole composition is not mirrored by fact-ground structure.

### Necessity and aseity

To infer \(\Box\mathsf{ActEnt}(g)\), add a modal premise such as the necessary actuality of \(F_\bullet\), plus necessary versions of TB1–TB2. The global grounding theorem gives neither. To infer aseity in the stronger sense—existence through itself rather than merely no ground in \(\mathcal D\)—one needs a separate essence/explanation principle.

### Awareness, agency, goodness, and creation

Even a simple, necessary ground entity need not be conscious, personal, morally good, or free. Each conclusion needs a further argument: an intrinsic-nature bridge for awareness; an intellect/will bridge for agency; an axiological bridge for goodness; and a modal/production bridge for literal creation. The bearer theorem does not collapse those steps.

## 6. Countermodels and what each premise excludes

| Model | GF true? | Failed bridge premise | Lesson |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Primitive foundation fact with no bearer | Yes | TB1 | A unique foundation fact need not be an entity. |
| Two co-fundamental entities jointly bearing one fact | Yes | TB2 | Entity uniqueness is not inherited from fact uniqueness. |
| The concrete world-totality as a summary bearer | Potentially | TB3 or TB4 | Truthmaking must be explanatory, not mere extensional inclusion. |
| Simple fact about a composite fundamental object | Yes | CR | Fact non-composition alone does not establish entity simplicity. |
| Actual but contingent foundational entity | Yes | Modal bridge | Grounding does not yield necessary existence. |

These are not mere verbal possibilities; they identify the cost of every transition from a fact-level to an entity-level conclusion.

---

## 7. Assessment

The bridge upgrades the result from a unique foundation fact to a unique ground entity only conditionally. Its gain is a precise theorem and a clear diagnosis of the remaining costs:

- **TB1** chooses bearer/truthmaker ontology over fact primitivism.
- **TB2** rules out plural exact bearers.
- **TB3** converts truthmaking into genuine ontological explanation.
- **TB4** rules out a derivative world-totality bearer.
- **CR** is additionally required for entity simplicity.

This is the strongest honest form of the desired inference. It supplies a separate bearer route to a ground entity without disguising entity, simplicity, aseity, awareness, or creatorhood as conclusions of the original grounding theorem.
