## 1. Improvements over WPI-1

WPI-2 directly addresses several deficiencies recorded in the WPI-1 review.

### 1.1 Typed noncomposition is sharper

The packet now states:

\[
\mathsf{WeakSimple}_2(G)
\iff
\neg\exists b:\mathbf B\,\mathsf{BPart}(b,G)
\land
\neg\exists f:\mathbf F\,\mathsf{FPart}(f,G).
\tag{WPI2-WS}
\]

This excludes inseparable as well as separable parts in the two declared ontic categories. It does not establish that positive formal distinction is metaphysically nonpartitive, but it makes the candidate's intended criterion explicit.

### 1.2 Grammar and closure are materially more operational

The packet supplies a partial recursive decoder, finite C1–C15 admissibility conditions, a strictly shorter-code recursion, and explicit set-builder closure clauses:

\[
A_H,
\qquad
E_H,
qquad
S_H,
qquad
H_{n+1}=H_n\cup\mathcal R_{\rm WPI2}(H).
\tag{WPI2-Closure}
\]

This is a real advance over the license-condition-only rules of WPI-1.

### 1.3 Anchor and match are improved

WPI-2 supplies an actual-host anchoring requirement and separates claim-independent matching from claim-relative fit:

\[
\mathsf{Matches}(\lambda,a_p,e_{p,i})
\iff
\lambda\approx_{\rm WPI2}\mathsf{ObsProj}(p,i),
\tag{Match}
\]

followed by a claim-relative \(\mathsf{Fit}\) rule. It also supplies canonicalization and an actual-articulation key-injectivity condition.

### 1.4 Phenomenal and normative operations are no longer bare labels

The packet specifies finite graph conditions for \(\mathsf{Integrated}_\Theta\), finite action-successor coding, a veto test, lexicographic values, and an exact modular physical update. These are candidate primitives, not established adequacy results, but they are substantially more articulated than their WPI-1 predecessors.

These advances do not cure the entry-blocking defects below.

## 2. Entry-blocking type error: finite entities occur in incompatible sorts

The packet declares:

\[
\mathbf B
=\text{beings: }G\text{ and dependent finite entities},
\tag{B-Sort}
\]

and types its ontological predicates as:

\[
\mathsf{DependsOn}\subseteq\mathbf B\times\mathbf B,
\qquad
\mathsf{BPart}\subseteq\mathbf B\times\mathbf B,
\qquad
\mathsf{DifferenceMaking}\subseteq\mathbf B.
\tag{B-Predicates}
\]

But it separately declares:

\[
\mathbf E
=
\{e_{p,i}:p\in\mathbf C,\ i\in\mathbb N\}
\quad\text{(finite-entity-token constructors)},
\tag{E-Sort}
\]

and defines:

\[
\mathsf{FiniteEnt}(e_{p,i})
\iff
 a_p\in H_\omega
\land\cdots.
\tag{Real2}
\]

Thus \(\mathsf{FiniteEnt}\) is applied to an \(\mathbf E\)-sorted term. Yet ontology clause O4 applies the \(\mathbf B\)-sorted predicates to every \(\mathsf{FiniteEnt}(e)\):

\[
\mathsf{FiniteEnt}(e)
\to
[
 e\neq G
\land
\mathsf{DependsOn}(e,G)
\land
\neg\mathsf{BPart}(e,G)
\land
\mathsf{DifferenceMaking}(e)
].
\tag{O4}
\]

In a many-sorted language with distinct disjoint sorts, the following terms are ill typed:

\[
e_{p,i}\neq G,
\qquad
\mathsf{DependsOn}(e_{p,i},G),
\qquad
\mathsf{BPart}(e_{p,i},G),
\qquad
\mathsf{DifferenceMaking}(e_{p,i}).
\tag{Type-Conflict}
\]

The packet provides neither an injection nor a realization map such as:

\[
\iota_{\rm ent}:\mathbf E\to\mathbf B,
\tag{Missing-Realizer}
\]

nor an identification of \(\mathbf E\) with the finite-entity subsort of \(\mathbf B\). Either choice would be a new primitive and a new realization bridge.

Therefore the proposed Z0 typed witness cannot satisfy the ontology and realization clauses as frozen:

\[
\boxed{
\mathsf{TypeConflict}_{\rm entity/being}.
}
\tag{I1}
\]

## 3. Entry-blocking finite-code problem: self-reference by literal code inclusion

A presentation claim node has a finite record field:

\[
\gamma(c).\mathrm{reference}\in\mathbf C=\{0,1\}^*.
\tag{Ref-Field}
\]

All record fields are said to use length-delimited canonical binary encoding. Actual anchoring requires:

\[
\mathsf{RefCode}(s_{p,j},\operatorname{can}(p)),
\tag{Anchor-Reference}
\]

and actual articulations can enter the closure only where:

\[
p=\operatorname{can}(p).
\tag{Canonical-Actuality}
\]

Thus an actual presentation within a canonical actual code requires its finite claim record literally to contain its own entire containing code:

\[
\gamma(c).\mathrm{reference}=p.
\tag{Self-Code}
\]

Under ordinary finite length-delimited embedding, a proper field carrying a literal finite string is shorter than the enclosing record. Hence:

\[
|\gamma(c).\mathrm{reference}|<|p|,
\tag{Length}
\]

whereas \((\mathrm{Self\text{-}Code})\) requires equality of the two strings. No finite record has both properties.

The packet does not define reference codes as finite pointers into a separately typed key table; it defines them as members of the finite-string code sort \(\mathbf C\), and it supplies no pointer grammar or external lookup relation. Consequently its own Z2 actual correct/error cases cannot be encoded by a finite actual presentation profile.

A repair would require, for example, a distinct key/pointer sort, an external finite reference table, or a nonliteral recursive coding convention. Each would change the signature, grammar, anchor rule, and anti-decoder audit.

\[
\boxed{
\mathsf{FiniteActualClaimCode}
\text{ is not supplied by the frozen packet.}
}
\tag{I2}
\]

## 5. Supplementary operational ambiguities

The following are not needed for the type-incoherence result, but would also require resolution before a full battery.

### 5.1 Claim-host lookup is not globally unique

\(\mathsf{TruthScore}\) refers to “the unique entity host of the presentation node that points to claim node \(c\).” C6 gives each presentation one claim; it does not constrain a claim node to be pointed to by only one presentation. Multiple presentations with different entity hosts can share one claim node.

### 5.2 Recursive equivalence and reference fields

Content and successor codes are well founded by strict length decrease, but reference codes are unconstrained and may be self-referential. Therefore the stated recursive preservation clause for canonicalization/equivalence is not fully specified for references.

### 5.3 Physical seed does not furnish an empirical recovery criterion

The seed, energy diagnostic, and translation test make an exact internal finite physical test. They do not provide the missing physical adequacy bridge demanded by Z3.d.
