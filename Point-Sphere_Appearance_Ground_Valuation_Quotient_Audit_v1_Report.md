# AP3P2E Ground–Appearance Valuation Quotient Audit v1 — Report
## One ground does not by itself entail one valuation-description class

## Evaluation of the proposed reframing

The reframing identifies an important and legitimate methodological pressure: do not reify an appearance-level coordinate multiplicity as a plurality of independent grounds or substances. That is fully compatible with the programme.

But its stronger conclusion does **not** follow from the present protected core:
\[
\text{all 16 lifts have the one ground }G
\quad\not\Rightarrow\quad
\text{all 16 lifts are one descriptive class}.
\]

One must distinguish:

\[
\boxed{\text{one ground}}
\qquad\text{from}\qquad
\boxed{\text{one aspect/content}}.
\]

The protected identity permits genuine modes to be aspects/contents *of* one ground. If sharing a ground automatically made two contents merely one description, then every finite aspect distinction would collapse — including the programme's own protected conditional domain of genuine aspects.

## Formal countermodel to the claimed derivation

Grant the strongest relevant premise:
\[
\operatorname{Ground}(\ell)=G
\qquad\text{for each of the 16 lifts }\ell.
\]

The same constant grounding map is compatible with all three quotient relations:

| Equivalence on the 16 lifts | Classes | Compatible with one common ground? |
|---|---:|---|
| Identity only | 16 | Yes |
| Uniform Q complement | 8 | Yes |
| Full sector-dependent Q redefinition | 1 | Yes |

Therefore common grounding supplies a vertical dependence relation; it does not supply the horizontal equivalence relation that a gauge quotient needs.

All executable checks pass.

## Why the examples do not yet bridge the gap

### Ground timelessness and mode-flow

The intended claim is:
\[
\operatorname{Time}(m)=\operatorname{Flow}(m),
\]
while the ground is not an externally timed object. This is a relation between levels. It does not define an \((\mathbb Z_2)^4\) transformation on Q valuations or prove that Q relations have no content. Indeed, it is an example of a ground-level nonmultiplicity coexisting with a mode-level articulated feature.

### Coordinate/frame analogies

A coordinate quotient is established only when a transformation group and its invariant observables have been independently specified. That is exactly the pending issue here: the relational quantities
\[
I_{s,t}=d(s)\oplus d(t)
\]
are invariant under uniform complement but not under independent sector flips. Calling them appearance-only assumes, rather than proves, full gauge.

This does not reject the analogy. It specifies what is needed to use it: a warrant that all Q-bit changes, including cross-sector ones, are merely changes of presentation.

### Will and necessity

An asserted ground-level collapse of will/necessity may be an interpretive analogy, but it is not presently an AP3 theorem, and it provides no valuation map or preservation criterion. It cannot by itself determine the Q-coordinate quotient.

## What *can* be adopted honestly

The fibre-local position can be adopted as a clearly named additional bridge:
\[
\mathsf{GAQ}_{\rm trace}:
\quad
\operatorname{Trace}(\ell)=\operatorname{Trace}(\ell')
\Longrightarrow
\ell\sim_{\rm desc}\ell'.
\]

Because all AP3P2 lifts preserve the trace, this postulate yields:
\[
H_{\rm full}\cong(\mathbb Z_2)^4
\quad\text{as gauge, hence one lift class.}
\]

This is not arbitrary canonical-lift selection. It is a uniform, fully symmetric **minimal fibre-local grammar convention**. It is compatible with the appearance/ground reading **provided** it is stated as a reductive bridge: no Q-bit or cross-sector Q relation counts as an aspect-content over and above the ordered trace.

Its cost is correspondingly clear: it rules out the relational valuation extension as additional content within this grammar. It cannot be reported as following from nonduality, one ground, no external time, or no-preference alone.

## Corrected one-line conclusion

> The 16 lifts may be treated as 16 appearance descriptions of one trace structure **if the programme adopts the ground–appearance trace-quotient bridge**. The bridge is a coherent, symmetric minimal-grammar convention motivated by the appearance/ground pattern; it is not derived from ground unity alone.

## Programme consequence

The proposed reframing corrects a real risk of reifying formal multiplicity. It does not make the relational branch logically inconsistent: that branch can describe multiple genuine aspects of one ground, not multiple grounds. The decision is now transparent:

- retain the valuation-content/aspect-typing question open; or
- explicitly adopt \(\mathsf{GAQ}_{\rm trace}\) as a revisable fibre-local grammar bridge and obtain full gauge in that branch.

Either path must preserve the distinction between a common ground and an equality criterion for its self-presented contents.
