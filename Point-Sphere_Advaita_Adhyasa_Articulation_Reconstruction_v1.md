# Advaita Adhyāsa Articulation Reconstruction v1
## Śaṅkara-oriented source test of superimposition, sublation, and indeterminacy

## Status

This is a bounded source reconstruction, not a proof that Advaita passes A0, a reconstruction of every Advaita sub-school, or a rescue of the earlier nondual/Appearance proposal.

It is also not yet evidence that “two source traditions fail at the same point.” The Proclean work is one source-grounded reconstruction; the earlier nondual \(\mathsf{SF}_0\) exercise was a constructed articulation attempt, not a second traditional source reconstruction. A shared failure pattern remains a hypothesis to test, not an established induction.

## 1. Source base and historical boundary

### Primary text and translation access

- Śaṅkara, *Adhyāsabhāṣya*, introduction to the *Brahma Sūtra* commentary; accessible translation/lecture material: https://sanskritdocuments.org/sites/snsastri/vedaadhyasa.html
- Supplementary translation: https://sanskritdocuments.org/sites/snsastri/adhyasabhashya.pdf

### Interpretive controls

- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, “Śaṅkara,” especially §§2.1–2.2 and 3.4: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/shankara/
- Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy, “Vedanta, Advaita”: https://iep.utm.edu/advaita-vedanta/

The dossier distinguishes Śaṅkara’s treatment of \(\mathsf{adhyāsa}\) from later, disputed developments concerning cosmic \(\mathsf{māyā}\), \(\mathsf{avidyā}\), and fully elaborated \(\mathsf{anirvacanīya}\) theory. It will not attribute a later doctrine to Śaṅkara merely because it is familiar under the label “Advaita.”

## 2. Candidate architecture extracted from the sources

The primary definition treats \(\mathsf{adhyāsa}\) as an appearance in a different locus of something previously experienced—paradigmatically mistaking a rope for a snake. The analysis includes mutual superimposition of self and non-self: bodily/mental predicates are attributed to the self, and consciousness is attributed to the body-mind complex.

Let \(s\) be a present locus, \(a\) an appearing content, and \(k\) a correcting cognition. A cautious reconstruction is:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf{Adhyāsa}_{\rm A}(a,s)\Rightarrow
&\mathsf{Appears}(a,s)\land
\mathsf{Mislocated}(a,s)\\
&\land \exists k\,\mathsf{Sublates}(k,\mathsf{Appears}(a,s)).
\end{aligned}
\]

At the local error level, the intended contrast is:

\[
\begin{array}{rcl}
\text{absolutely unreal} &:& \text{cannot appear as an experienced object};\\
\text{empirical appearance} &:& \text{appears but can be sublated};\\
\text{absolute reality} &:& \text{is not sublated.}
\end{array}
\]

The later technical characterization \(\mathsf{anirvacanīya}\) is therefore not merely an evocative third word. It marks the claim that what is experienced yet sublated cannot be classified as absolutely real or as simple non-being.

## 3. The strongest candidate public criterion

The best charitable generalization supplied by this source family is:

\[
\mathsf{Mithyā}_{\rm A}(x)
\iff
\mathsf{Cognized}(x)
\land
\mathsf{Sublatable}(x)
\land
\mathsf{DependentOnSubstratum}(x).
\]

This is not presented as an unrestricted theorem of logic. Its classification is conditional on a theory of valid cognition, the relevant sublating cognition, and the alleged substratum. For the rope-snake case, ordinary corrective perception is meant to supply those elements. For the empirical world as a whole, the claim that Brahman-knowledge sublates the world is a distinctive Advaita commitment.

## 4. What the reconstruction genuinely supplies

The source offers more than a bare assertion that the finite is “neither real nor unreal.” It distinguishes at least three questions:

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
E_1 &: \text{Does something appear or function in experience?}\\
E_2 &: \text{Can that presentation be corrected/sublated?}\\
E_3 &: \text{Does the presentation have independent ultimate reality?}
\end{array}
\]

For ordinary superimposition, \(E_1\) and a specified corrective test can be intersubjectively examined without first accepting the complete Advaita cosmology. This is a real contribution to an error/appearance semantics.

## 5. Structural-battery test

| Case | Advaita reconstruction result | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| T0: no finite candidate | The framework permits absolute reality without finite independently real entities. | Does not derive any finite inventory. |
| T1: one finite candidate | It may be empirically cognized and, if sublated, \(\mathsf{mithyā}\). | Classifies epistemic/ontological status, not modehood. |
| T2: symmetric pair | Equal sublatability does not individuate the two items. | No particularity rule. |
| T3: property candidate | \(\mathsf{adhyāsa}\) can involve an object or an attribute, but its criterion does not distinguish a genuine mode from a property. | Mode/property boundary remains open. |
| T4: independent candidate | The candidate may be denied independent ultimate reality if dependent/sublatable. | This does not thereby make it a non-substantial mode; it may be an appearance, an empirical object, or a different ontological category. |
| T5: re-encoding | A judgment of sublation is invariant only if appearances, correcting cognition, and substratum are preserved under a common profile. | No source-supplied profile extractor or invariance proof. |
| T6: token added without semantic resource | The criterion can ask whether it appears and is sublated; it cannot derive its presence or determine a complete roster. | Exact-profile burden remains. |

## 6. Main limitation: wrong target for A3

\(\mathsf{Mithyā}_{\rm A}\) is principally a criterion for the ontological/epistemic status of an appearance, not a criterion for a genuine finite mode:

\[
\mathsf{Mithyā}_{\rm A}(x)
\not\Rightarrow
\mathsf{GenuineMode}(x).
\]

Indeed, the most natural Advaita result may reject the target demand that finite particulars be real modes in the relevant robust sense. A finite item could be empirically operative yet ultimately sublatable; it need not be a property of Brahman, an independent substance, or a genuine finite articulation of a simple ground.

The reconstruction therefore may illuminate an Appearance-style alternative, but it cannot be assimilated without argument to the protected claim that finite real modes are core under \(P_{\rm ND}\).

## 7. Gate assessment

The A0 gate is not a demand for a framework-neutral ontology. It permits framework-internal vocabulary, provided that truth conditions do not use the desired inventory as their only warrant, introduce an uncharged selector, or vary under declared conservative reformulation.

Replacing framework-neutral publicness with “checkability only by those who already accept the framework” would defeat the gate: it would license the very stipulative membership rules that A0 excludes.

The Advaita result does show a narrower limitation:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Public sublation may classify a presentation as corrigible/appearance-like,}\
\text{without deciding whether it is a genuine mode, property, or substance.}
}
\]

That is not evidence that A0 is unsatisfiable. It is evidence that a public error criterion and a public mode-membership criterion have different explanatory tasks.

## 8. Articulation verdict

The source earns a limited partial pass only for local error/appearance semantics:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Advaita supplies a substantive, partially public schema for superimposition and sublation.}
}
\]

It fails the stronger A0 articulation criterion because it does not provide:

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
L_1 &: \text{a genuine-mode condition independent of the Advaita inventory};\\
L_2 &: \text{a particularity criterion for finite symmetric items};\\
L_3 &: \text{a mode/property distinction};\\
L_4 &: \text{a profile extractor and invariance proof};\\
L_5 &: \text{a derivation of an exact finite domain};\\
L_6 &: \text{a phenomenal or physical adequacy bridge.}
\end{array}
\]

## 9. Consequence

Advaita is not a failed duplication of Proclus. It supplies a different kind of argument: a theory of cognitively operative but ultimately corrigible appearance. Its failure at A3 is a target mismatch, not merely an inability to find better wording.

A subsequent global assessment of the A0 gate would require a genuine range of candidate types: participation, appearance/error, whole-first, structural/mathematical, and any other serious articulation mechanism. It cannot be responsibly based on the present small and non-homogeneous sample.

## Nonclaim

This reconstruction does not show Advaita false, that the world is illusory, that ordinary empirical knowledge is worthless, that the A0 gate is unsatisfiable, or that no correct whole-reality framework can be identified.
