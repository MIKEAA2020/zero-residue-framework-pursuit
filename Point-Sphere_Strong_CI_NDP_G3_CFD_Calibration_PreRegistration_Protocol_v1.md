# Strong CI Adjudication — \(\mathcal G_3\)/CFD Phenomenal Calibration Pre-Registration v1
## Protocol only: no validation result is claimed

## Status

**Pre-registered protocol draft. No corpus has been collected, coded, or adjudicated under it. This document cannot validate \(\mathsf{CFD}^{\rm cmp,\Phi}_{G}\); it only fixes what a non-post-hoc restricted phenomenal test would require.**

The protocol tests phenomenal adequacy of the proposed profile criterion:

\[
\mathsf{ProfileCore}(\Pi)
\Longleftrightarrow
\text{complete profile has universal positive support and one connected field graph}.
\]

It does not test global aspecthood, nondual identity, plenitude, physical reality, or framework truth.

## 1. Frozen objects under test

### 1.1 Candidate classifier

The candidate classifier is the complete-mode profile test from \(\mathcal G_3\):

\[
\mathsf{Core}^{\rm cmp}_{A}(m)
\Longleftrightarrow
\mathsf{Complete}_{A}(m)
\land
\mathsf{Supported}^{\rm cmp}_{\Phi}(m)
\land
\mathsf{Unified}^{\rm cmp}_{\Phi}(m).
\]

Only complete finite diagrams or their declared profile-extraction equivalents may be evaluated. A partial report that cannot be completed by the fixed coding rules is recorded as **out of scope**, not as a negative aspect case.

### 1.2 Fixed rivals

The protocol compares the classifier to two rivals:

\[
R_{\rm syn}(m)
\Longleftrightarrow
\mathsf{Gen}_{\mathcal G_3}(m),
\]

and:

\[
R_{\rm atom}(c)
\Longleftrightarrow
\mathsf{FormalCtx}(c)
\land
\forall j\,\mathsf{Core}^{\rm cmp}(m_j).
\]

The atomistic rival omits global field unity. The realization-dependent rival is excluded: under the restricted coverage/plenitude/transfer package it is extensionally coextensive with aspecthood and supplies no case discriminator.

### 1.3 Permitted encoding equivalence

A reified or alternative diagram language may be used only with a frozen profile extractor:

\[
\eta_L:
\mathsf{CompleteModes}(L)
\to
\langle\bar S,\bar E,Q,H,B\rangle.
\]

The extractor must be declared before coding a case and must be profile-conservative under the G3 representation-invariance condition. A case with no declared extractor is out of scope.

## 2. Data and independence boundary

A calibration item is:

\[
d=\langle\alpha,r,\kappa,\pi\rangle,
\]

where:

- \(\alpha\) is a locally indexed reported episode;
- \(r\) is a raw first-person description captured before formal coding;
- \(\kappa\) is a non-CFD phenomenal classification record;
- \(\pi\) is a complete diagram/profile coded from \(r\) using the frozen manual.

This protocol does not assume that reports are incorrigible, publicly objective, or metaphysically truth-making. They are defeasible evidence inputs. No public witness code, decoder, or persistence ontology is inferred merely from the existence of a report.

### 2.1 Independence separation

The protocol uses two separated tasks:

1. **Phenomenal classifier:** obtains \(\kappa\) from fixed questions about the episode and may not inspect \(\pi\), graph connectivity, \(\mathsf{Core}\), \(\mathsf{CFD}\), or rival verdicts.
2. **Diagram coder:** produces \(\pi\) from \(r\), using a frozen syntax manual, and may not inspect \(\kappa\), the proposed core verdict, or rival verdicts.

The same person may not perform both roles for a single item. If genuinely separate coders are unavailable, the item is marked **non-independent** and may illustrate but cannot count toward calibration.

## 3. Frozen phenomenal classification record

The phenomenal classifier records answers without using the terms “support,” “graph,” “connectivity,” “aspect,” “mode,” “nondual,” or “field profile.”

For each episode, record the following predeclared answers:

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
K1 &: \text{Was there an experienced qualitative presentation?}\
K2 &: \text{Was it reported as one local presentation rather than a mere list of separately described items?}\
K3 &: \text{If there were distinguishable moments/items, were they reported as jointly presented within that one episode?}\
K4 &: \text{Was the report too indeterminate to answer K1--K3?}
\end{array}
\]

Set:

\[
\mathsf{CertField}_{\alpha}(d)=+
\
\Longleftrightarrow
K1=\mathrm{yes}\land K2=\mathrm{yes}\land K3\in\{\mathrm{yes},\mathrm{not\ applicable}\}\land K4=\mathrm{no}.
\]

Set:

\[
\mathsf{CertField}_{\alpha}(d)=-
\
\Longleftrightarrow
K1=\mathrm{no}
\lor
(K2=\mathrm{no}\land K4=\mathrm{no}).
\]

All other responses are **indeterminate**, not negative.

This certification is a calibration target for phenomenal adequacy. It is not a definition of \(\mathsf{Aspect}^{\Phi}_{G}\), and it cannot by itself refute or establish \(P_{\rm ND}\).

## 4. Diagram coding manual

Before any item is coded, the manual fixes:

1. how report-roles map to active subject, phase, and rigid-quality slots;
2. when two terms are coded as equal versus distinct;
3. which qualitative attributions and phase orders are entered;
4. the required complete active atom base; and
5. the treatment of unreported material.

**Completion rule:** unreported material may not be arbitrarily inserted to make a diagram connected. If the report does not support a unique complete coding under the manual, it is marked **incomplete/out of scope** rather than completed by a favourable choice.

**No-rescue rule:** a coding may not be revised after viewing \(\mathsf{Core}\), \(R_{\rm syn}\), or \(R_{\rm atom}\) verdicts. Corrections are allowed only for a predeclared syntax/manual error and must be versioned.

## 5. Required corpus composition

No items are yet enrolled. For the eventual corpus to count, it must include before verdict computation:

\[
\mathcal D_\star=
\mathcal D_{+}\cup\mathcal D_{-}\cup\mathcal D_{\Delta}.
\]

- \(\mathcal D_{+}\): at least three independently certified local-presentation items.
- \(\mathcal D_{-}\): at least three independently certified non-field/list-or-no-presentation items.
- \(\mathcal D_{\Delta}\): at least two complete items in a predeclared disagreement class.

The disagreement classes are:

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\Delta_{\rm syn}: & \text{generated but Core-rejected complete diagrams};\\
\Delta_{\rm atom}: & \text{same local core occurrences under distinct explicit global gluing diagrams.}
\end{array}
\]

If a candidate item fails completeness or independent-role separation, it is not silently reassigned to a favourable class; it is excluded and logged.

## 6. Verdict and mismatch rules

For every enrolled item, compute only after certification and coding are frozen:

\[
\mathsf{Core}^{\rm cmp}(\pi),
\qquad
R_{\rm syn}(\pi),
\qquad
R_{\rm atom}(\pi)\ \text{where applicable}.
\]

### 6.1 Candidate adequacy failure

The restricted phenomenal adequacy claim for CFD is defeated if either condition occurs in an enrolled, independently coded, in-scope item:

\[
\mathsf{CertField}_{\alpha}(d)=+
\land
\neg\mathsf{Core}^{\rm cmp}(\pi),
\tag{F+}
\]

or:

\[
\mathsf{CertField}_{\alpha}(d)=-
\land
\mathsf{Core}^{\rm cmp}(\pi).
\tag{F-}
\]

This defeats the protocol's claim that \(\mathsf{Core}^{\rm cmp}\) tracks the independently classified restricted phenomenal target. It does not deductively prove:

\[
\neg\mathsf{CFD}^{\rm cmp,\Phi}_{G},
\]

because the metaphysical biconditional could be retained only by rejecting the protocol's adequacy target. Such a move must be entered as a new cost, not counted as a pass.

### 6.2 Rival outcome

A rival is not counted as defeated merely by disagreement. A comparative result requires the rival to classify the independently certified corpus worse under the same frozen coding and completeness conditions.

If neither rival is worse, the protocol returns:

\[
\text{no selector advantage established}.
\]

## 7. Expiry

This protocol is pre-registered on **2026-10-21**. Its public-selector entitlement expires on:

\[
 d_\star=\text{2026-12-31}.
\]

It expires if, by \(d_\star\), either:

\[
\text{(i) no corpus satisfying §5 has been independently enrolled and frozen,}
\]

or:

\[
\text{(ii) no item in either disagreement class }\Delta_{\rm syn}\text{ or }\Delta_{\rm atom}

Expiry means only:

\[
\text{CFD may no longer be presented as an empirically/phenomenologically warranted public selector.}
\]

It does not prove the metaphysical postulate false.
