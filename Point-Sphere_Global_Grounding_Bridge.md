# Global Grounding Bridge v1
## A valid conditional theorem from a total concrete domain to one simple foundation fact

## Result in one sentence

This document strengthens the local-foundation result into a global theorem, but only by making every required premise visible. Given a total domain of actual concrete facts, a well-founded full-grounding relation, dependence-composition, transitivity of full grounding, and an explicit anti-primitive-plurality axiom, there is exactly one non-composite foundation fact that fully grounds every fact in the domain.

The theorem is valid. Its premises are substantive. It does **not** by itself establish necessary existence across worlds, awareness, personhood, goodness, or literal creation.

---

## 1. Why the local theorem is not enough

A local theorem begins with one derivative fact \(d\) and yields some minimal ancestor. It leaves open:

- whether other concrete facts have different minimal ancestors;
- whether the local root grounds the whole concrete domain;
- whether multiple roots are jointly primitive;
- whether the root is simple;
- whether the root exists necessarily rather than only actually;
- whether a foundation fact is the fact *that a ground exists* rather than the ground itself.

A global argument has to earn each further conclusion. The right procedure is not to call “the totality” self-explanatory. It is to define the total domain and state the closure, transitivity, composition, and unity principles needed to obtain one root.

---

## 2. Grounding framework

Let \(F\) be a non-empty set of all **actual concrete facts** in the intended domain. The phrase “all” is a totality premise: it excludes neither further concrete facts nor grounding facts required by the theory. Logical truths and abstract mathematical truths may be handled separately; the theorem is about the concrete grounding domain.

Write
\[
S\triangleright f
\]
for “the non-empty set of facts \(S\subseteq F\) is a complete immediate ground of \(f\).” Complete means sufficient within the specified grounding theory, not merely one partial contributor.

Define the predecessor relation
\[
x\prec f
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\exists S\subseteq F\,[S\triangleright f\ \land\ x\in S].
\]

A fact \(m\in F\) is a **foundation fact** when it has no proper predecessor:
\[
\operatorname{Root}(m)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\neg\exists x\in F\,(x\prec m).
\]

Let
\[
M=\{m\in F:\operatorname{Root}(m)\}
\]
be the foundation set.

We use \(S\triangleright^* f\) for the transitive closure of complete grounding, allowing \(\{f\}\triangleright^*f\) reflexively.

---

## 3. Premises

### T — Totality closure

\(F\) contains every actual concrete fact relevant to the grounding explanation and every concrete fact used as an immediate ground in that explanation.

This prevents a superficial “global” theorem that silently excludes the unifying fact, modal condition, law, or selector on which the argument relies.

### W — Well-founded proper grounding

The predecessor relation \(\prec\) is well-founded:
\[
\forall X\subseteq F\,
\bigl(X\neq\varnothing\rightarrow
\exists x\in X\;\neg\exists y\in X\,(y\prec x)\bigr).
\]

This rules out an endless descending chain of proper full grounds within the specified domain. It is the anti-regress premise. It is not a theorem of logic.

### C — Dependence-composition

Every fact that is composite in the **dependence-generating** sense has a proper full ground:
\[
\operatorname{Comp}(f)ightarrow\exists S\subseteq F\,
(S\triangleright f\land f\notin S).
\]

The restriction matters. C does not assert that every mathematical aggregate or arbitrary collection needs an external cause. It asserts that if a fact’s unity depends on constituents and a unifying condition, that dependence must appear in its grounding profile.

### X — Full-ground transitivity and substitution closure

If \(S\triangleright f\), and each \(s\in S\) is fully grounded, directly or transitively, by \(T_s\), then the union of those ground sets fully grounds \(f\):
\[
S\triangleright f\ \land\ \forall s\in S\,(T_s\triangleright^*s)
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\bigcup_{s\in S}T_s\triangleright^*f.
\]
The closure is assumed to apply along every well-founded ancestry tree generated by the specified grounding sets, including any set-indexed substitutions that the theory permits.

This is stronger than the weak claim that grounding is merely explanatory ancestry. Without X, all grounding chains may terminate in the same root while the root still fails to be a full ground of the facts higher in the chain.

### U — Anti-primitive plurality

There cannot be two or more jointly actual, wholly independent foundation facts in the total concrete domain:
\[
|M|>1\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\exists m\in M\,\exists z\in F\;(z\prec m).
\]

Since the consequent contradicts \(m\in M\), U implies \(|M|\le 1\).

U is an explicit unity premise. It should not be disguised as a consequence of the word “ultimate.” Its philosophical motivation is that a plurality of jointly actual ultimate facts contains a unity/distinction structure that calls for a ground. Its opponent may deny this and accept an irreducibly plural foundation. The theorem records, rather than hides, that disagreement.

### B — Optional bearer bridge

The theorem yields a foundation **fact**. To infer a ground **entity**, add:

> Every foundational existential fact has a unique ontological bearer, and facts it fully grounds depend existentially on that bearer.

B is not part of the grounding theorem. It is a reification or truthmaker-style bridge and needs separate defence.

## 4. The theorem

### Theorem — Global unique simple foundation

Assume T, W, C, X, and U. Then there exists exactly one foundation fact \(g\in F\) such that:

1. \(g\) has no proper full ground;
2. \(g\) is non-composite in the dependence-generating sense;
3. for every \(f\in F\),
   \[
   \{g\}\triangleright^* f.
   \]

If B is also assumed, \(g\) is borne by a unique ground entity that existentially underwrites every fact in \(F\).

#### Proof

**Existence.** Since \(F\) is non-empty and \(\prec\) is well-founded by W, \(F\) has a \(\prec\)-minimal element. Hence \(M\neq\varnothing\).

**Uniqueness.** If \(|M|>1\), U yields \(m\in M\) and \(z\in F\) with \(z\prec m\). This contradicts the definition of \(M\). Hence \(|M|\le1\). With non-emptiness, \(M=\{g\}\) for one fact \(g\).

**Simplicity in the dependence-generating sense.** Suppose \(\operatorname{Comp}(g)\). By C, some proper ground set \(S\) satisfies \(S\triangleright g\) with \(g\notin S\). Taking any \(s\in S\) gives \(s\prec g\), contradicting \(g\in M\). Therefore \(g\) is not composite in the stipulated sense.

**Global full grounding.** Fix any \(f\in F\). Repeatedly replace non-foundational members of a full ground set for \(f\) by their complete grounds. W guarantees that this descent reaches only minimal facts; since \(M=\{g\}\), every terminal ground is \(g\). By X, the substitutions compose into \(\{g\}\triangleright^*f\). \(\square\)

---

## 5. What the theorem earns

The theorem improves the earlier local result in three precise ways.

1. It reaches the stipulated **entire concrete domain**, not merely one ancestry tree.
2. It derives **one** foundation fact rather than an unspecified set of roots.
3. It derives **non-composition in the dependence-generating sense**, not geometric point-likeness or every classical attribute of simplicity.

It therefore supports this exact conditional conclusion:

> If the entire actual concrete domain is closed under full grounding, proper grounding is well-founded, dependence-composites need grounds, full grounding is transitive, and primitive plurality is excluded, then the actual concrete domain has one non-composite foundation fact from which every actual concrete fact is fully grounded.

That is a valid global grounding argument. It is stronger than an appeal to brute fact or a merely local minimal ancestor.

## 6. Premise defence and price

The theorem is only as strong as its assumptions. Their status should be argued rather than concealed.

### T: why a total domain?

A global conclusion cannot be reached from an arbitrarily selected local chain. T is motivated by the aim of an ultimate explanation: all concrete facts relevant to actuality and dependence must be in view. T faces familiar objections about unrestricted totalities, fusions, and whether facts form a set. A theory may use a proper class or typed hierarchy instead; it must then formulate an analogue of closure.

### W: why well-foundedness?

W is the formal expression of the claim that full dependence cannot consist solely of borrowed grounding. It is not established by the impossibility of an infinite temporal past; it concerns asymmetric ontological dependence. A defender of an infinite grounding chain can reject W. The theorem then does not apply, which is exactly as it should be.

### C: why does composition require a ground?

C is limited to dependence-composites. It says that when constituents and their unity are ontologically prior to a whole, they must figure in a proper ground. It does not refute every conception of a necessarily composite object. Its opponent can posit primitive composition or a priority monism in which the whole is prior to parts. Those positions reject C or change the fact domain; they are rival metaphysics, not counterexamples to the theorem.

### X: why full-ground transitivity?

X is required to upgrade shared ancestry into a single full ground. Some accounts of grounding endorse only weak or mediate transitivity; on those accounts the global-full-ground conclusion must be weakened to root reachability. The theorem makes this choice public.

### U: why one root rather than many?

U is the most controversial premise. It encodes the intuition that an irreducible plurality of actual foundations leaves their joint actuality, distinction, and unity unexplained. A pluralist can reject U and accept a fundamental plurality. That does not invalidate the proof; it identifies the exact disagreement.

The crucial improvement is that the framework no longer claims to deduce unity from well-foundedness alone. Unity comes only from U.

## 7. Countermodels that price the premises

Each premise does genuine work.

1. **Without W:** take facts \(f_n\) for integers \(n\), with \(f_{n+1}\prec f_n\). There is no minimal foundation fact.
2. **Without U:** take two root facts \(a,b\), neither grounded, with \(a\) fully grounding one family of facts and \(b\) another. All other assumptions may hold, but there are two roots.
3. **Without C:** stipulate a primitive composite root \(c\) with no proper ground. The root exists and may be unique, but its non-composition does not follow.
4. **Without X:** let every chain terminate at \(g\), while a higher fact \(f\) requires both a derivative mediator and an irreducible relational fact not fully reducible to \(g\). Root reachability remains, but \(\{g\}\triangleright^*f\) fails.
5. **Without B:** the theorem yields one foundation fact, not yet a personal, mental, or even object-like entity.

These countermodels prevent a false sense of inevitability. The global theorem is real precisely because its failure modes are explicit.

---

## 8. Modal limits

An actual unique foundation does not yet yield a necessary foundation.

A simple countermodel has two possible worlds. In \(w_1\), the theorem’s premises hold with unique root \(g_1\). In \(w_2\), a different total concrete domain has root \(g_2\), or no corresponding root because W fails. Nothing in T, W, C, X, or U at \(w_1\) alone rules this out.

To derive cross-world necessity, add a separate modal premise, for example:

- the grounding laws and totality domain are necessary across worlds;
- a foundation fact cannot fail to obtain without a further ground;
- or the bearer of \(g\) has existence entailed by its essence.

Each is a substantive modal thesis. The global theorem earns **actual global foundation**, not essence–existence identity or necessary aseity.

Likewise, it does not establish awareness. A neutral, physical, structural, plural-at-a-different-level, or awareness-based bearer theory remains possible unless further intrinsic-nature arguments are supplied.

## 9. Integration with the wider programme

The theorem sharpens the dependency ledger.

\[
\text{T,W,C,X,U}
\Longrightarrow
\text{one actual non-composite foundation fact}
\]
\[
\text{B}
\Longrightarrow
\text{a ground entity bearing that fact}
\]
\[
\text{additional modal bridge}
\Longrightarrow
\text{necessary aseity}
\]
\[
\text{intrinsic-nature bridge}
\Longrightarrow
\text{awareness-ground hypothesis}
\]
\[
\text{creation bridge}
\Longrightarrow
\text{C-Nec, C-Cont, or non-dual alternative}
\]

This ordering matters. It prevents the framework from attributing awareness, personal agency, plenitude, or physics-generating power to the result of a grounding theorem that has not yet reached those conclusions.

---

## 10. Completion tests

The global grounding track advances only if it can defend or responsibly delimit its premises.

1. **Formal test:** implement T, W, C, X, and U in a grounding or hyperintensional modal logic; verify the theorem and construct the countermodels explicitly.
2. **Metaphysical test:** compare the strongest rival rejection of each premise—infinitism against W, priority monism against C, plural foundations against U, weak grounding against X, anti-reification against B.
3. **Scope test:** specify whether the fact domain includes laws, modal facts, mathematical truths, consciousness facts, and grounding facts themselves; do not change the domain midway through an argument.
4. **Modal test:** provide a non-question-begging bridge from actual foundation to necessary aseity, or retain the more modest actual conclusion.

The correct achievement is not an unqualified proof of God. It is a valid, global, premise-priced foundation theorem and a clear map of what must be added to reach stronger metaphysical claims.
