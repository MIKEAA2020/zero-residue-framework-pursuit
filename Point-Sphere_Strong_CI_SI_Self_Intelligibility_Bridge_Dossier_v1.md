## The common terminal burden every candidate must meet

**Self-intelligibility is required by strongest CI but not yet supported by a publicly examinable non-circular terminal-closure argument that any candidate demonstrably satisfies.** This is a common burden, not a refutation of all candidates.

## 1. The terminal question

Every non-brute architecture eventually claims that its ultimate closure \(U\), nature \(\mathcal E_U\), or global structure is intelligible through itself rather than through a further external ultimate source:

\[
\mathsf{SelfInt}(U):
\quad
\mathsf{Ess}(U)
\text{ makes the existence and exact ultimate profile of \(U\) intelligible without a further ultimate explainer.}
\]

Strong CI requires some closure of this kind. Otherwise the terminal nature/law/whole is simply brute.

But the phrase “self-intelligible” cannot mean any of the following without more content:

\[
U\text{ exists because }U\text{ exists},
\qquad
U\prec U,
\qquad
U\text{ is necessary because it is necessary}.
\]

These are labels or circles, not explanations.

## 2. Candidate self-intelligibility strategies

| Strategy | Proposed source of closure | Exact SCI problem |
|---|---|---|
| Formal/axiomatic | \(U\) is derivable from a fundamental formal system | Why that system/axioms, and why formal derivability is ontological explanation |
| Bare modal necessity | \(\Box_R\operatorname{Ex}(U)\) | Why \(U\) is necessary rather than simply asserted necessary |
| Essence–existence identity | \(\mathsf{Ess}(U)\equiv\operatorname{Ex}(U)\) | Coherence of the identity and why it explains rather than redescribes existence |
| Conceptual/analytic necessity | Denial of \(U\) is contradictory | Whether the concept is uniquely fixed and whether conceptual necessity yields reality |
| Whole-first structural closure | The whole structure intrinsically determines its own profile | Why the exact whole is not a primitive totality or self-description |
| Nonrepresentational self-presence | Awareness/self-knowledge has no knower–known gap | Why awareness is ultimate and why finite articulation follows, not merely assumed |
| Deflationary stopping point | No further explanation is needed | Rejects strongest CI unless “no need” is independently explicated |

None is rejected merely by appearing in this list. Each must supply an explanatory relation that differs from a terminal renaming.

## 3. Public SI pass condition

A candidate earns a public self-intelligibility pass only if it supplies:

\[
\mathsf{SIpass}(U)
\Rightarrow
\begin{cases}
\text{a typed essence/profile \(\mathsf{Ess}(U)\);}\
\text{a stated explanatory relation from that essence to existence/profile;}\\
\text{a non-circular criterion distinguishing this relation from brute assertion;}\\
\text{a negative case: a necessary-looking or terminal-looking rival that fails;}\\
\text{a reason the explanatory relation itself is not an ungrounded addition.}
\end{cases}
\tag{SI-P}
\]

A candidate may offer an internal/core-relative account. That establishes only a conditional framework unless it meets the public negative-case and non-circularity tests.

## 4. Common failure patterns

### Necessity label

\[
\Box_Rp
\not\Rightarrow
\mathsf{Expl}(\mathsf{Ess}(U),p).
\]

A necessity claim may be true, but without an account it relocates the terminal fact.

### Identity label

\[
\mathsf{Ess}(U)=\operatorname{Ex}(U)
\not\Rightarrow
\mathsf{Expl}(
\mathsf{Ess}(U),
\operatorname{Ex}(U)).
\]

An identity can be an explanatory ground only given a theory of essence, existence, and explanation that makes the identity informative rather than stipulative.

### Whole label

\[
\text{“the whole explains its parts”}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{“the whole explains why it is this exact whole.”}
\]

Whole-first explanation may succeed, but it must reach the whole’s exact profile rather than merely reverse explanatory direction.

### Self-knowledge label

\[
\text{“self-knowledge is actual self-presentation”}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{“all finite contents are genuine self-knowledge contents.”}
\]

This preserves the distinction between the valid nondual omission theorem and the public aspect-domain problem.

## 6. Exact result

\[
\boxed{
\mathsf{SCI}
\Rightarrow
\text{a non-brute closure claim is required.}
}
\]

But:

\[
\boxed{
\mathsf{SCI}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{a public criterion that certifies any named self-intelligibility strategy.}
}
\]

The latter gap is not an argument for brute facts. It records the deepest unresolved explanatory burden for every candidate.

## 7. Research consequence

The search for the correct framework cannot be completed by selecting a derivative relation—creation, emanation, aspect, or totality—before its terminal closure strategy is articulated. A candidate that cannot meet SI-P cannot pass strongest CI, however elegant its downstream account.

## 8. Expiry condition

The SI route advances only with a public explanation account that gives positive and negative cases for self-intelligibility and survives the four failure patterns above. A mere declaration that an ultimate is “necessary,” “self-grounding,” “pure act,” “the whole,” or “self-aware” does not reopen the generic question.

## Nonclaims

This dossier does not prove that strongest CI is unsatisfiable, that self-intelligibility is impossible, or that all candidate frameworks fail. It prevents a terminal label from being counted as an achieved explanation.
