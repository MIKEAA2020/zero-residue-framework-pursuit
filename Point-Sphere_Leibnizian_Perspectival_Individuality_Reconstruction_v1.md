# Leibnizian Perspectival Individuality Reconstruction v1
## Source-grounded test of complete concepts, PII, and unique perspectival expression

## Status

This dossier tests the strongest Leibnizian candidate for a public criterion of finite particularity. It does not assume that a “unique perspective” is a free-standing mode criterion, or that it can be detached from monad substance, complete individual concepts, God, compossibility, and pre-established harmony without loss.

## 2. Candidate architecture

The relevant Leibnizian claims are:

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
L_1 &: \text{a monad is a simple substance};\\
L_2 &: \text{an individual substance has a complete individual concept (CIC)};\\
L_3 &: \text{no two substances differ merely numerically while sharing every property (PII)};\\
L_4 &: \text{each monad expresses the same universe from its own point of view};\\
L_5 &: \text{the actual coordinated domain involves compossibility and pre-established harmony.}
\end{array}
\]

The primary text’s “same town from different sides” analogy concerns the perspectival expression of one universe by many simple substances. It is not, by itself, a definition of non-substantial modes.

## 3. Strongest charitable criterion

Let \(\mathsf{CIC}_L(x)\) be a complete individual concept and \(\Pi_L(x)\) its total perspectival/perceptual expression. The identity rule is:

\[
\mathsf{PII}_{L}(x,y)
\iff
\bigl[
\forall F\,(F(x)\leftrightarrow F(y))
\bigr]
\rightarrow x=y.
\]

The proposed particularity rule is:

\[
\mathsf{Distinct}_{L}(x,y)
\rightarrow
\Pi_L(x)\ne\Pi_L(y),
\]

where a different perspective is understood as a difference in the complete pattern or relative distinctness of the monad’s perceptions of the one universe.

This is a substantive identity/individuation proposal, not merely a relation-to-ground slogan.

## 4. What the proposal genuinely achieves

Leibniz supplies a principled response to bare numerical difference:

\[
\text{distinctness}
\not\equiv
\text{a merely external label, coordinate, or haecceity}.
\]

If the complete qualitative profile of a candidate is fully specified and two candidates share it, PII directs us to treat them as one. This directly targets the T2 symmetry/particularity problem more closely than the previous dossiers did.

## 5. Why it is not yet a public genuine-mode criterion

PII is an **identity condition**, not an **existence or membership condition**:

\[
\mathsf{PII}_{L}(x,y)
\not\Rightarrow
\mathsf{Exists}(x),
\qquad
\mathsf{PII}_{L}(x,y)
\not\Rightarrow
\mathsf{Monad}(x),
\qquad
\mathsf{PII}_{L}(x,y)
\not\Rightarrow
\mathsf{Mode}(x).
\]

It says when two already admitted candidates are one, conditional on a completed profile. It does not say which candidates exist, which complete profiles are genuine, or why a candidate is a monad rather than a property, aggregate, fiction, or merely possible individual.

Moreover, a complete individual concept contains all predicates true of the substance, including its relations to the entire world. Thus the purported local perspectival test invokes the full world profile:

\[
\mathsf{CIC}_L(x)
\rightsquigarrow
\text{exact totality/compossibility burden}.
\]

## 6. Structural-battery test

| Case | Leibnizian reconstruction result | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| T0: no finite candidate | PII is compatible with an empty or singleton candidate domain. | It derives no finite population. |
| T1: one candidate | A fully specified CIC can provide identity through time and across predicates. | Conditional on an already supplied complete concept and substance classification. |
| T2: symmetric pair | PII says qualitatively indiscernible candidates are one; if distinct, some intrinsic difference must be found. | A genuine particularity rule, but not an effective/public way to find the difference; arguments from CIC to PII require additional premises. |
| T3: property candidate | A property may enter a CIC without itself being a substance/monad. | PII does not supply the mode/property boundary. |
| T4: independent candidate | A Leibnizian monad is explicitly a simple substance, not a non-substantial mode. | Fails the target non-substantiality condition if used directly. |
| T5: re-encoding | PII is invariant only under translations preserving all profile-relevant predicates and their truth conditions. | No finite common profile extractor is supplied; “all predicates” cannot be silently treated as one. |
| T6: additional token | A new CIC may be possible only if compossible with the others; actualization invokes the chosen harmonious world. | Selector/exact-world burden remains. |

## 7. The inseparability problem

The proposed “unique perspective” cannot simply be detached from the monad metaphysics:

\[
\text{perspective}
\longleftrightarrow
\text{representation by a simple substance}
\longleftrightarrow
\text{CIC and world-relative harmony}.
\]

If one retains only “every real item has a unique structural profile,” the result is a generic individuation axiom. It no longer determines whether the item is a monad, mode, property, physical individual, or appearance.

If one retains the full Leibnizian account, then the candidate introduces simple substances, whole-world profiles, compossibility, and divine selection. These may be defensible commitments, but they are not a detached framework-neutral criterion.

## 8. Articulation verdict

\[
\boxed{
\text{Leibniz supplies a substantive conditional principle of identity/individuation,}\
\text{not a public criterion of genuine finite modehood or actuality.}
}
\]

This is an advance over a merely relational grammar, but it does not pass A0. Its unclosed burdens are:

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
L_1 &: \text{what makes a candidate a simple substance rather than another ontological type};\\
L_2 &: \text{how a complete profile is identified without presupposing the exact world};\\
L_3 &: \text{why PII follows rather than being added as an individuation premise};\\
L_4 &: \text{how perspective is representation-invariant under a shared extractor};\\
L_5 &: \text{which compossible collection becomes actual and why};\\
L_6 &: \text{phenomenal and physical adequacy.}
\end{array}
\]

## 9. Consequence

The Leibnizian case refines the comparative diagnosis. The earlier traditions did not simply lack all criteria: Advaita gives a local sublation criterion, Hegel a logical-derivation criterion, and Leibniz an identity criterion. The unresolved common issue is more precise:

\[
\boxed{
\text{A criterion restricted to status, derivation, or identity has not yet yielded}\
\text{a public bridge to finite metaphysical actuality/mode membership.}
}
\]

This is a live research pattern, not a no-go theorem.

## Nonclaim

This reconstruction does not show that PII is false, that monads are incoherent, that Leibniz’s best-world theory is false, that perspectival uniqueness is useless, or that the A0 gate is unsatisfiable.
