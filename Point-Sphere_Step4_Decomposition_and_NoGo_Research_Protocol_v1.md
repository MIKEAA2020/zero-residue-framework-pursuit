# Step 4 Decomposition and No-Go Research Protocol v1
## Constructive and negative tracks for the whole-reality gate

## Status and discipline

This is a research protocol, not a proof that a framework passes Step 4, a proof that strongest complete intelligibility is impossible, or a ranking of frameworks. No framework is presently identified as correct.

The target remains:

\[
\exists![F]_{\simeq}\bigl[
\mathsf{SCI}(F)\land \mathsf{E4}(F)\land
\mathsf{Adeq}_{\Phi}(F)\land\mathsf{Adeq}_{P}(F)
\bigr].
\]

The protocol adopts two parallel tracks:

\[
\text{constructive decomposition}
\qquad\Vert\qquad
\text{scoped no-go attempt}.
\]

It does **not** infer a global impossibility theorem from the repeated failure of existing candidates.

## 1. Correction to the proposed decomposition

Articulation, particularity, dependence, compossibility, and exact domain are distinguishable questions, but they are not wholly independent. In particular, a criterion of compossibility or dependence stated over “modes” already presupposes enough modal grammar to say what a mode is.

The right aim is therefore conditional modularity:

\[
\text{solve each subproblem at its own type, state every inherited assumption,}\
\text{and do not treat a conditional result as an integrated framework result.}
\]

## 2. Dependency map

Let the work packages be:

\[
\begin{array}{ll}
M_0 &: \text{background logic, semantic resources, and representation-invariance policy};\\
A &: \text{articulation: simple ground, determinate modes, and their relation};\\
P &: \text{particularity: identity/difference conditions for a mode};\\
D &: \text{dependence: a non-temporal constitutive/explanatory relation};\\
C &: \text{compossibility: a criterion for joint admissibility of modes};\\
X &: \text{exact domain/profile: why this complete modal profile obtains};\\
B_\Phi &: \text{phenomenal bridge};\\
B_P &: \text{physical bridge}.
\end{array}
\]

The minimum dependency order is:

\[
M_0\longrightarrow A\longrightarrow\{P,D\},
\qquad
\{A,P\}\longrightarrow C,
\qquad
\{A,P,D,C\}\longrightarrow X.
\]

The adequacy bridges are additional, not replacements:

\[
\{A,P,D,C,X\}\not\Rightarrow B_\Phi
\quad\text{and}\quad
\{A,P,D,C,X\}\not\Rightarrow B_P.
\]

Thus an isolated result about compossibility or dependence can be useful, but cannot by itself satisfy \(\mathsf{SCI}\), \(\mathsf{E4}\), phenomenal adequacy, or physical adequacy.

## 3. Constructive track: success conditions

### 3.1 \(M_0\): public semantic frame

A result must state the logic, identity resources, modal resources, explanation/grounding relation, and allowed representational transformations. It must not silently import factorisation, external time, public objectivity, a witness, decoder, phase ontology, persistence, or injectivity convention.

**Pass condition:** two formulations judged conservative under the declared profile extractor agree on the stated result.

**Failure condition:** a claimed result changes merely through unacknowledged coding or representation choices.

### 3.2 \(A\): articulation

A candidate articulation theory must supply truth conditions for:

\[
\mathsf{Mode}(m),\qquad
\mathsf{Of}(m,G),\qquad
\mathsf{Determinate}(m),\qquad
\mathsf{Simple}(G).
\]

It must show, rather than merely label, how determinate finite modes neither compose \(G\), nor float as independent substances, nor reduce without remainder to predicates of \(G\). “Eminent containment,” “distinction of reason,” “self-articulation,” and “manifestation” count as prompts for a semantics, not as a semantics.

**Pass condition:** a noncircular interpretation supplies at least one finite determinate modal case, distinguishes it from a property and an independent substance, and preserves simplicity under the declared semantics.

**Failure condition:** the exact mode inventory or mode-to-ground relation is stipulated, shifted into an unanalysed primitive, or obtains only through a hidden selector.

### 3.3 \(P\): particularity

A particularity account must distinguish numerical/qualitative/structural difference as applicable, and state when two presentations are one mode, two modes, or alternative descriptions. It must not assume distinctness by a coding convention.

**Pass condition:** the account decides at least predeclared test pairs without appeal to an external individuator or bare haecceity.

**Failure condition:** “particular” only means “some determinate property,” or individuation is delegated to unexplained coordinates, time, material parts, or an external observer.

### 3.4 \(D\): dependence

A dependence account must type the relation without treating it as temporal production, spatial containment, ordinary causation, or a second independently existing realm.

**Pass condition:** it identifies the explanatory direction, relata, and counterfactual/modal role, and shows why this relation is not merely coextensive restatement of \(\mathsf{Of}(m,G)\).

**Failure condition:** “depends on” has no content beyond “is associated with,” or requires the very individuation/domain result it claims to explain without declaring that dependency.

### 3.5 \(C\): compossibility

A compossibility account must state a checkable condition \(\mathsf{Comp}(S)\) for a finite set/profile of modes and show how it relates to the articulation semantics.

**Pass condition:** it handles predeclared compatible, incompatible, and representation-equivalent cases, and proves that the criterion is invariant under permitted reformulation.

**Failure condition:** compossibility means only “belongs to the actual totality,” “is mutually consistent” with no defined consistency relation, or “is divinely/intelligibly possible” with the deciding resource left untyped.

### 3.6 \(X\): exact domain/profile

An exact-profile account must answer why the full admissible/actual domain is \(D\) rather than a distinct candidate domain \(D'\), without treating bare possibility, coherence, goodness, or a maximisation label as an automatic actuality rule.

**Pass condition:** the candidate yields a noncircular domain-selection or domain-nonselection theorem with an explicit modal semantics, and makes clear whether its result is restricted coverage or global plenitude.

**Failure condition:** it simply re-describes \(D\) as the ground’s exact nature, or presupposes the mode-membership fact it was meant to determine.

## 4. Integration discipline

A constructive Step 4 package may be considered for the shared substantive gate only if it contains:

\[
M_0\land A\land P\land D\land C\land X
\]

with an explicit bridge ledger. Every result must be labelled either:

\[
\text{unconditional},\quad
\text{conditional on named premises},\quad
\text{or exploratory proposal}.
\]

No successful subpackage licenses a conclusion about \(B_\Phi\), \(B_P\), or framework uniqueness.

## 5. Negative track: how a no-go result could be valid

A global assertion that no framework can satisfy \(\mathsf{SCI}\) is not currently licensed. A valid negative result first needs a formally declared scope class \(\mathcal C\), background logic \(L\), and a non-question-begging specification of \(\mathsf{SCI}_L\).

The appropriate target shape is:

\[
\forall F\in\mathcal C\;\Bigl[
\mathsf{Ultimate}_L(F)\land
\mathsf{SCI}_L(F)\land
\mathsf{Req}(F)
\;\Rightarrow\;
\bot
\Bigr].
\]

A result of that form excludes only the specified class \(\mathcal C\). It does not automatically establish:

\[
\forall F\;\neg\mathsf{SCI}(F).
\]

### 5.1 Required no-go components

1. **Scope:** State exactly which frameworks are in \(\mathcal C\), including whether classical, whole-first, mathematical, nondual, structural, and unknown future candidates are included.
2. **Explanatory demand:** Specify what counts as a residue, explanation, circularity, regress, and admissible self-explanation.
3. **Exhaustiveness lemma:** Prove—not merely assert—that every member of \(\mathcal C\) must fall into the declared cases (for example, regress, brute termination, vicious circle, or satisfactory self-grounding).
4. **Exclusion lemmas:** Show why each disallowed case violates the explicit demand.
5. **Escape audit:** Test the theorem against re-description, primitive explanation relations, non-well-founded explanation, grounding pluralism, and alternative logics, marking all exclusions overtly.

### 5.2 Invalid no-go moves

The following do not establish impossibility:

\[
\text{all reviewed candidates fail};
\qquad
\text{a final explanation sounds brute};
\qquad
\text{a definition cannot be further explained};
\qquad
\text{self-explanation is stipulated circular}.
\]

Those may motivate a scoped research question but are not proofs of a global theorem.

## 6. Fallback discipline: least-residue without disguise

Least-residue is a permissible fallback only if it is explicitly a comparative, defeasible ranking rather than a proof of truth or \(\mathsf{SCI}\).

Before ranking, define a profile-neutral residue ledger with typed entries, such as:

\[
\langle
\text{semantic primitive},
\text{modal selector},
\text{individuation primitive},
\text{bridge gap},
\text{physical mismatch}
\rangle.
\]

A scalar sum may not be introduced without independently justified weights. The default output is a Pareto or lexicographic partial order:

\[
F_1\preceq_R F_2,
\]

which may leave multiple incomparable candidates. Incomparability is a valid result; it may not be converted into a winner by elegance intuition.

## 7. First work products and stopping rules

### First constructive product

Produce an articulation dossier for one explicitly named candidate tradition or candidate family, but only after declaring its modal vocabulary, conserved profile, predeclared test cases, and expiry condition. This is not a seventeenth reformulation of the CFD selector.

### First negative product

Produce a scope audit deciding whether a nontrivial \(\mathcal C\), \(L\), and \(\mathsf{SCI}_L\) can be specified without building the desired negative result into the definitions.

### Stopping rule

If either first product cannot state its scope, semantic resources, test cases, and disconfirming outcome, record it as an inadmissible research proposal rather than repeatedly rebuilding it. If it can, proceed only to its declared finite test battery.

## Nonclaim

This protocol neither identifies a correct framework nor shows that no framework can be correct. It replaces an undifferentiated Step 4 impasse with auditable constructive and negative research obligations.
