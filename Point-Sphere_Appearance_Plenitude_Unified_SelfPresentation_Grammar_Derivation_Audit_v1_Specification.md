# AP3P2K Unified Self-Presentation Grammar-Derivation Audit v1
## Do reflexivity, retentional thickness, boundedness, and maximality jointly discharge grammar adequacy, enrichment, and component-boundary debts?

## Proposal tested

The proposal correctly identifies a shared interface question: how a finite formal grammar is related to genuine self-presenting mode-content. It proposes four finite-mode features:

1. immanent reflexivity;
2. retentional thickness;
3. boundedness;
4. maximality.

It then proposes deriving a minimal grammar from those features, thereby settling adequacy, enrichments, and boundaries together.

## Resource audit

The four features do not all have the claimed same status:

- **Reflexivity/immanence:** AP3J supplies a conditional phenomenological typing constraint; it does not select a graph.
- **Retentional thickness:** AP3J motivates temporal articulation weakly; AP3N adds an internal retentional order, and AP3O proves that it does not yield successor/continuation. It is not a theorem of the circular core alone.
- **Boundedness:** real finite appearance excludes universal compatibility only within the proper-maximal-context implementation.
- **Maximality:** maximal contexts are an AP3G implementation of finite modes, not a grammar-independent definition. AP3P1 successor components are separately stipulated.

## Already-executed family test

AP3L applied immanence, boundedness/properness, nonobjectification, and real-boundary quotienting. It reduced 37 labelled grammars to five structural families, not one grammar. AP3M's horizon constraints and AP3N's retentional order leave all five viable. AP3O then shows that a successor flow is a new premise, not a consequence of static retentional order.

Thus the four-feature programme has already produced its honest result:
\[
\mathcal F_{\rm self-presenting}\neq\{\text{unique grammar}\}.
\]

## Two non-implications

### Grammar adequacy

Even a derived family of grammars gives necessary structural conditions, not:
\[
\operatorname{Content}(x)\Rightarrow\operatorname{Represented}_{\Sigma}(x)
\]
for one chosen member \(\Sigma\). That is a grammar-adequacy bridge.

### Enrichment necessity

An enrichment not required by every finite self-presentation need not be non-content. A base grammar \(M\) can satisfy all four features, and an enriched grammar \(M+J\) can also satisfy them with \(J\) genuine only in that enriched mode. Therefore:
\[
\neg\operatorname{RequiredForAllModes}(J)
\not\Rightarrow
\neg\operatorname{GenuineContent}(J).
\]
Excluding optional enrichments requires a **minimal-grammar policy**, not merely the four features.

## Boundary result

If a successor relation is supplied, connected components are graph-theoretically maximal. But equating them with modes requires both:

1. a successor/continuation law; and
2. a component-to-mode identity bridge.

AP3L maximal phase contexts do not supply either. The proposed maximality connection is therefore a well-posed later task, but not an existing derivation.

## Correct unified target

The proper integrated theorem target is weaker and noncircular:
\[
\text{finite self-presentation features}
\Longrightarrow
\text{a family of admissible grammar constraints}.
\]

For each explicitly chosen member, one can then determine its description quotients. A claim that one member is exhaustive of genuine content, or that its components are genuine modes, requires an extra adequacy/identity bridge.

## Registered conclusion

The three debts share a common **interface**, but not one automatically dischargeable premise. The proposed four-feature derivation is valuable as a family-constraining programme already partially executed; it cannot make adequacy, enrichment exclusion, or component-to-mode identity theorems without adding the very bridges at issue.
