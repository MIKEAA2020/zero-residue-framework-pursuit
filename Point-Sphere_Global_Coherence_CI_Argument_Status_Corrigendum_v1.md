# GC4 Corrigendum v1 — Complete Intelligibility Is Argument-Supported, Not a Bare Choice

## Correction

The GC4 audit correctly found no formal theorem:

\[
\mathsf{RN}\not\models\mathsf{OCI}.
\]

But its language should not reduce the complete-intelligibility (CI) principle to a merely arbitrary “choice.” CI is a substantive metaphysical commitment supported by two serious argument families. Neither currently proves CI from neutral logic alone.

## 1. Parity argument: limited but real support

The parity argument proposes that explanation/grounding is not an alien addition to rationality but belongs with the intelligibility sought in demonstrative inquiry. It supports a broad conception of reason in which truth-preservation without an account of why is incomplete.

Its valid contribution is:

\[
\text{rational intelligibility plausibly includes explanatory/grounding demands,}
\]

not:

\[
\text{metaphysical grounding is identical to formal logical consequence.}
\]

The latter identity is false or at least unsupported. Logical consequence is normally reflexive and monotonic; grounding is often treated as irreflexive/asymmetric and hyperintensional/non-monotonic. Transitivity of grounding is also debated. The argument therefore pressures a narrow account of rationality, but does not show that defining formal logic narrowly is incoherent.

Aristotelian demonstration offers historical support for the broader ideal: scientific knowledge requires grasping why a conclusion holds, not merely a valid deduction. It does not establish that all metaphysical grounding belongs to modern formal logic.

## 2. Self-referential argument: exact scope

The self-referential argument blocks the position:

\[
\text{Nothing whatsoever is intelligible.}
\]

An asserted truth to that effect would itself be an intelligible claim about reality.

It does **not** by itself block:

\[
\text{Some ultimate fact(s) are brute.}
\]

A deflationary opponent may treat the claim that a brute remainder exists as itself part of that remainder, or as a locally intelligible assertion whose object contains an unexplained terminus. That reply is explanatorily costly under CI, but it is not formally self-contradictory.

Likewise, denying a single composite “whole” does not by itself settle the issue. CI can be formulated without a totality object:

\[
\forall p\,[\operatorname{Ultimate}(p)\Rightarrow\neg\operatorname{Brute}(p)].
\]

A pluralist may reject this schema, but then accepts primitive ultimate facts rather than defeating the CI demand by mereological deflation.

## 3. Correct status

\[
\boxed{
\text{CI is an argument-supported substantive commitment, not a bare preference and not a neutral logical theorem.}
}
\]

The two live coherent replies are:

1. **Formalist restriction:** logic concerns truth-preserving inference; grounding is a distinct metaphysical enterprise.
2. **Deflationary/pluralist remainder:** local intelligibility is retained while some ultimate fact or plurality is allowed to lack further explanation.

These replies bear costs relative to CI but are not refuted by the arguments above. The GC4 non-theorem conclusion therefore stands; its previous “choice” vocabulary is withdrawn.

## 4. Relation to the omission theorem

The internal omission theorem is unaffected. It is not derived from CI alone. It follows conditionally from the nondual identity package plus a non-circular genuine-aspect domain:

\[
\operatorname{GenuineAspect}(a)
\Rightarrow
\operatorname{ContentOfSelfKnowledge}_G(a)
\Rightarrow
R(a).
\]

The remaining open issue is \(\mathcal A_{\rm genuine}\). Calling it a “specification problem” is justified only provisionally: it has a real failure condition. If no non-circular, non-reifying criterion can be supplied, it becomes a substantive objection rather than a mere drafting task.

## 5. Revised global verdict

The correct calibrated conclusion is:

> Under an argument-supported CI methodology, Appearance plus the protected nondual identity package has a strong conditional advantage over contingent creator–creation selection. It is not yet a neutral theorem or a unique truth claim, because CI has coherent rivals and \(\mathcal A_{\rm genuine}\) remains open.
