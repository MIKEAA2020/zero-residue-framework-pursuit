# Strong CI Adjudication — Elegance Audit Protocol v1
## Elegance as a constrained comparison method, not an ontological oracle

## Status

**Adopted as a revisable comparison protocol conditional on strongest CI.** It does not rank any current candidate, because none currently passes the common substantive gate.

## 1. Lexicographic rule

Elegance enters only after substantive adequacy. Let:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf{Gate}(F):={}&
\mathsf{SCI}(F)
\land
\mathsf{Consistent}_{\rm typed}(F)
\land
\mathsf{TypedPossibility}(F)\\
&\land
\mathsf{Compossibility}(F)
\land
\mathsf{ExactDomain}(F)
\land
\mathsf{ExplainsActuality}(F)\\
&\land
\mathsf{Adeq}_{\Phi}(F)
\land
\mathsf{Adeq}_{P}(F).
\end{aligned}
\tag{Gate}
\]

\(\mathsf{SCI}\) remains an argument-supported substantive commitment, not a neutral theorem. The protocol compares candidates conditional on that commitment; it does not silently eliminate formalist or deflationary rivals from the larger methodology question.

A candidate that fails any Gate item is recorded as incomplete. It receives no elegance offset for compactness, symmetry, or historical pedigree.

## 2. Typed meanings of the gate

- \(\mathsf{TypedPossibility}\): real possibility is specified without defining it as whatever the theory already admits.
- \(\mathsf{Compossibility}\): coexistence/incompatibility is given an explicit relation or context semantics.
- \(\mathsf{ExactDomain}\): all-and-only domain membership is accounted for, not merely asserted.
- \(\mathsf{ExplainsActuality}\): the relation from possibility/domain to realization has no unexplained later selector; this is distinct from a mere necessity label.
- \(\mathsf{Adeq}_{\Phi}\) and \(\mathsf{Adeq}_{P}\): phenomenal and physical adequacy retain their separate interfaces. Physical adequacy requires actual recovery/discriminator work, not metaphysical imagery.

## 3. Commitment ledger and concealment test

For each gated candidate, record:

\[
\mathsf{Ledger}(F)=
\langle
\text{independent primitives},
\text{unearned bridges},
\text{selectors},
\text{exceptions},
\text{residues}
\rangle.
\]

The ledger is explanatory, not a word count. Every entry must state its type, justification status, and whether it is core, a revisable bridge, or an external rival premise.

### Concealment test

A compressed formulation earns no simplicity credit if its expansion introduces unacknowledged distinctions or rules needed for its explanatory work.

\[
\text{verbal/formal compression}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{ontological or explanatory simplicity}.
\]

For example, “all genuine aspects are self-known” is not a low-cost explanation unless genuine aspecthood, self-knowledge, exact domain, and their relation are independently specified.

## 4. Representation invariance

Elegance comparisons are made only modulo target-relative commitment-preserving equivalence:

\[
F\simeq_{\rm task}F'
\Rightarrow
\mathsf{Elegance}(F)=\mathsf{Elegance}(F').
\]

\(\simeq_{\rm task}\) is not bare syntactic equivalence. It requires preservation of relevant ontology, grounding/explanation claims, domain, realization rule, and adequacy commitments. This avoids treating formal intertranslatability as automatic ontological identity.

No candidate gains credit by introducing a primitive that merely abbreviates a larger unearned bundle of commitments.

## 5. Pareto comparison

After candidates pass Gate and their ledgers are expanded under the same representational discipline, define:

\[
\mathsf{Cost}(F)=
\langle
P_F,B_F,S_F,X_F,R_F
\rangle,
\]

where the components track independently justified primitives, unearned bridges, selectors, exceptions, and residues. The components are typed and comparative; they are not presumed to be mechanically cardinal.

\[
F_1\prec_{\rm el}F_2
\]

only if:

\[
\mathsf{Gate}(F_1)
\land
\mathsf{Gate}(F_2)
\land
\mathsf{Cost}(F_1)\preceq\mathsf{Cost}(F_2)
\land
\mathsf{Cost}(F_1)\ne\mathsf{Cost}(F_2).
\]

If each candidate is better on a different dimension, the protocol returns an unresolved trade-off, not a forced winner. Any weighted total score requires weights declared and defended before candidate-specific scoring.

## 6. Anti-bias controls

1. Apply the same gate and ledger expansion to every live candidate.
2. Preserve protected commitments, revisable bridges, and external rivals as distinct statuses.
3. Do not call an unresolved bridge a cost only for a disfavoured candidate.
4. Do not turn explanatory dissatisfaction into formal impossibility without an explicit premise.
5. A candidate’s apparent simplicity cannot compensate for failure of phenomenal or physical adequacy.

## 8. Uniqueness non-entailment

Let:

\[
Q(F):=
\mathsf{SCI}(F)
\land
\mathsf{E4}(F)
\land
\mathsf{Adeq}_{\Phi}(F)
\land
\mathsf{Adeq}_{P}(F).
\]

Even if one adds the conditional premise:

\[
\forall F[\mathsf{SCI}(F)\Rightarrow\mathsf{E4}(F)],
\]

it does not entail:

\[
\exists![F]_{\simeq}Q(F).
\]

There are interpretations of that conditional premise with no qualifying candidate, exactly one qualifying equivalence class, or multiple inequivalent qualifying candidates. Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\forall F[\mathsf{SCI}(F)\Rightarrow\mathsf{E4}(F)]
\not\models
\exists![F]_{\simeq}Q(F).
}
\]

This is a schema-level underdetermination result. It does not prove that uniqueness is false; it shows that existence, uniqueness, and identification need further defended premises.

## 9. Exact result

\[
\boxed{
\text{Elegance is an admissibility constraint and Pareto tie-breaker after adequacy,}\
\text{not evidence that a preferred ontology is true.}
}
\]

\[
\boxed{
\text{The current elegant outcome is an unresolved comparison, not a manufactured winner.}
}

## Nonclaims

This protocol does not establish that SCI is true, reduce metaphysics to formal complexity, provide an objective scalar simplicity measure, select \(P_{\rm ND}\) or any rival, or relax exact-domain/physical adequacy demands.
