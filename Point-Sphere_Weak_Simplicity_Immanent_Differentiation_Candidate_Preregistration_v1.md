## 1. Nonspatial Point and Sphere designations

The Point is a designation of the one ultimate bearer \(G\). It is not a geometrical point, spatial location, temporal initial state, or a small object:

\[
\mathsf{PointDesignation}(G)
\not\Rightarrow
\mathsf{LocatedInSpace}(G)
\lor
\mathsf{Extended}(G)
\lor
\mathsf{EarlierThan}(G,x).
\tag{Nonspatial-Point}
\]

The Sphere is a designation of a closed immanent formal field \(\Sigma_G\), not a spatial surface, external container, second realm, or independent being:

\[
\mathsf{SphereDesignation}(G,\Sigma_G)
\not\Rightarrow
\mathsf{SpatialObject}(\Sigma_G)
\lor
\mathsf{ExternalContainer}(\Sigma_G,G)
\lor
\mathsf{IndependentEntity}(\Sigma_G).
\tag{Nonspatial-Sphere}

## 2. Weak simplicity: fixed noncircular core grammar

Weak simplicity is not defined by its ability to produce a desired world, appearance, relation, or finite mode. It is the following negative unity condition:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf{WeakSimple}(G)
\iff{}&
\mathsf{OneUltimateBearer}(G)
\land
\neg\exists x\,[\mathsf{ProperPartOf}(x,G)]\\
&\land
\neg\exists x\,[\mathsf{IndependentConstituentOf}(x,G)]
\land
\neg\mathsf{SpatiallyExtended}(G)\\
&\land
\neg\mathsf{TemporallyProduced}(G).
\end{aligned}
\tag{Weak-Simplicity}
\]

It does **not** include the stronger prohibition on every real or formal intrinsic distinction:

\[
\mathsf{WeakSimple}(G)
\not\Rightarrow
\neg\exists D,R\,
\mathsf{PosImDiff}(G,D,R).
\tag{Weak-Not-Strong}
\]

This is the exact point of departure from the strong-simplicity reading:

\[
\mathsf{StrongSimple}^{\sharp}(G)
\Longrightarrow
\neg\exists D,R\,
\mathsf{PosImDiff}(G,D,R)
\quad
\text{(candidate-core reading)},
\tag{Strong-Firewall}
\]

whereas \(F_{\rm WSP\text{-}1}\) does not make that implication part of its core.

## 3. Positive immanent formal differentiation

Let \(D_G\) be a typed domain of formal roles of \(G\), and let \(R_G\) be a typed incidence/dependence relation on those roles. The roles are not independent entities, parts, spatial locations, or temporal stages:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf{FormalRoleOf}(d,G)
\Longrightarrow{}&
\neg\mathsf{IndependentEntity}(d)
\land
\neg\mathsf{ProperPartOf}(d,G)\\
&\land
\neg\mathsf{SpatialLocation}(d)
\land
\neg\mathsf{TemporalStage}(d).
\end{aligned}
\tag{Role-Type}
\]

The candidate's primitive differentiation grammar is:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf{PosImDiff}(G,D_G,R_G)

\iff{}&
D_G\neq\varnothing
\land
\forall d\in D_G\,\mathsf{FormalRoleOf}(d,G)\\
&\land
R_G\subseteq D_G\times D_G
\land
\mathsf{Closed}(D_G,R_G)\\
&\land
\exists d,e\in D_G\,[d\neq e\land\mathsf{FormallyDiscernible}_{R_G}(d,e)].
\end{aligned}
\tag{Positive-Immanent-Difference}
\]

`Closed` means every incidence endpoint is a role of \(G\):

\[
\mathsf{Closed}(D_G,R_G)
\iff
\forall\langle d,e\rangle\in R_G\,[d\in D_G\land e\in D_G].
\tag{Closed-Immanence}
\]

`FormallyDiscernible` must be settled by the typed incidence grammar alone, not by an appeal to actual finite modes, empirical observations, phenomenal labels, or the desired Point–Sphere result:

\[
\mathsf{FormallyDiscernible}_{R_G}(d,e)
\not\iff
\mathsf{DifferentFiniteEffects}(d,e)
\lor
\mathsf{ActualAppearance}(d)
\lor
\mathsf{ActualAppearance}(e).
\tag{No-Outcome-Loaded-Difference}
\]

## 4. Sphere as immanent closed formal field

The Sphere designation is fixed by the formal field, not by literal geometry:

\[
\mathsf{SphereDesignation}(G,\Sigma_G)
\iff
\Sigma_G=\langle D_G,R_G\rangle
\land
\mathsf{PosImDiff}(G,D_G,R_G)
\land
\mathsf{Closed}(D_G,R_G).
\tag{Sphere-Definition}
\]

The core does not say that \(\Sigma_G\) is another ontological bearer. It says that one bearer may be intrinsically/formally articulate while lacking parts and independent constituents:

\[
\mathsf{SphereDesignation}(G,\Sigma_G)
\land
\mathsf{WeakSimple}(G)
\not\Rightarrow
\mathsf{TwoUltimateBearers}
\lor
\mathsf{PartComposition}(G).
\tag{Nonpartitive-Claim}
\]

This is a candidate commitment that requires defense. It is not a theorem extracted from the weak-simplicity definition alone.
