# Point–Sphere Physics-Bridge Landscape
## What existing emergent-spacetime programmes show about the next truth-directed step

**Status:** Research map only. This is not PS-LP-3, not a reformulation of Point–Sphere monism, and not a derivation of physics. It neither selects weak nor strong simplicity, nor treats any surveyed physics programme as true.

**Question:** After PS-LP-2W and PS-LP-2S failed to reach even a determinate prephysical output, where should a truth-directed Point–Sphere project look for a genuine physics bridge rather than a new verbal restatement?

---

## 1. Core result of the survey

Established emergent-spacetime programmes do not begin with literal space and time. But neither do they begin with a contentless unity. Each starts with a rich, explicit, nonspatiotemporal mathematical structure and then faces a difficult emergence/recovery problem.

\[
\mathsf{NonSpatiotemporal}
\notightsquigarrow
\mathsf{Structureless}.
\]

The three useful comparison families are:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{family} & \text{fundamental mathematical resource} & \text{intended recovered structure}\\
\hline
\text{causal sets} & \text{locally finite partial order} & \text{Lorentzian spacetime approximation}\\
\text{tensor/entanglement} & \text{quantum state, factorization/network, entanglement} & \text{geometry and gravity under additional assumptions}\\
\text{group field theory} & \text{fields, group data, combinatorial quanta, dynamics} & \text{effective cosmological/geometric behaviour}
\end{array}
\]

The lesson for Point–Sphere is methodological:

\[
\mathsf{Point}
\xrightarrow{\text{only a slogan of limitation}}
\mathsf{Physics}
\]

has no analogue in these programmes. Every programme specifies an intermediate formal resource, a dynamics or limiting/coarse-graining regime, and a recovery map.

## 2. Causal-set route

### 2.1 What is actually primitive

Causal-set theory replaces continuum spacetime at the fundamental level with a locally finite partially ordered set. The order is interpreted as proto-causal and local finiteness as intrinsic discreteness; in a suitable continuum approximation, order corresponds to causal structure and cardinality to spacetime volume.

A schematic causal-set starting package is:

\[
\mathcal C
=
\langle C,\prec,\mathsf{LF},\mathcal A,\mathcal E\rangle,
\]

where \(C\) is a plurality of elements, \(\prec\) is a partial order, \(\mathsf{LF}\) is local finiteness, \(\mathcal A\) represents a dynamics/action or measure, and \(\mathcal E\) is an emergence/approximation relation.

It is not a derivation of all spacetime from a point-like simple bearer. It explicitly posits plurality, relation, discreteness, and a dynamical or measure problem.

### 2.2 Relevance to the Point–Sphere project

Causal sets show one precise shape a physics bridge can take:

\[
\text{nonspatial relational structure}
\longrightarrow
\text{continuum approximation}
\longrightarrow
\text{spacetime geometry}.
\]

They do **not** license the inference:

\[
\mathsf{NoSpatialLocation}(P)
\Longrightarrow
\mathsf{LocallyFinitePoset}.
\]

For a future Point–Sphere candidate to use this route, it would have to derive—not merely borrow—a plurality of relation-positions, an order, finiteness, a dynamics/measure, and a recovery criterion.

### 2.3 Simplicity pressure

- A strong-simplicity/privative candidate cannot simply install \(C\) and \(\prec\) as intrinsic positive structure without changing its core.
- A weak-simplicity candidate may propose immanent formal relational structure, but must meet the unresolved nonpartitivity, nonindependence, actualization, selector, and formal-to-ontological burdens.

No general refutation follows in either direction.

## 3. Tensor-network and entanglement route

### 3.1 What is actually primitive

Tensor-network approaches begin with quantum degrees of freedom organized in a state/network and with an entanglement structure. A discrete geometry can encode that entanglement, and a continuum limit is an additional condition in the claimed emergence of gravitational geometry.

A schematic starting package is:

\[
\mathcal T
=
\langle
\mathcal H,
\Psi,
\mathsf{Factorization},
\mathsf{Network},
\mathsf{Entanglement},
\mathsf{ContinuumLimit}
\rangle.
\]

The Hilbert space, its subsystem structure or suitable algebraic replacement, a state, and a network are not generated merely by calling a source limitless. They are substantive mathematical postulates.

### 3.2 Relevance to Point–Sphere

This route is especially helpful as a warning against an ambiguous “view” relation. It distinguishes:

\[
\text{a formal information structure}
\quad\text{from}\quad
\text{an emergent geometry encoded by that structure}.
\]

Thus even a successful limit grammar would still require an explicit map from its output to state/algebra/network data, and then an independently specified geometry and continuum bridge.

### 3.3 Simplicity pressure

The route has an immediate composition concern: familiar tensor factorizations are expressed in terms of subsystems and tensor products.

\[
\mathsf{TensorFactorization}
\not\Rightarrow
\mathsf{CompatibilityWithWeakSimplicity},
\]

and it conflicts more directly with a strong-simplicity reading that excludes real internal formal differentiation. Algebraic approaches that avoid naïve factorization may alter the issue, but would be a separate fresh candidate and cannot be assumed to solve it.

## 4. Group-field-theory and quantum-geometry-condensate route

### 4.1 What is actually primitive

Group field theory uses combinatorial structures with further group or geometric/pregeometric data, plus quantum dynamics. In condensate cosmology, effective cosmological behaviour is sought from collective states of many quantum-geometric degrees of freedom.

A schematic starting package is:

\[
\mathcal G
=
\langle
\mathsf{GroupData},
\mathsf{FieldOrQuanta},
\mathsf{Combinatorics},
\mathsf{Action},
\mathsf{State},
\mathsf{CoarseGraining}
\rangle.
\]

The approach is valuable because it makes the missing ingredients visible: a state, a law, a many-degree-of-freedom regime, an approximation, and a comparison target. It does not present those as consequences of a bare unity claim.

### 4.2 Relevance to Point–Sphere

A Point–Sphere limit proposal could not identify the Sphere with a GFT condensate without adding the entire package above. That would be a new substantive physical theory, not an unpacking of “Sphere is Point under limits.”

\[
\mathsf{SphereAsGFTCondensate}
\Longrightarrow
\mathsf{NewPhysicsPostulates}.
\]

### 4.3 Simplicity pressure

The many quanta, group labels, graphs, and field dynamics are plainly positive formal resources. A weak-simplicity approach would require a new account of their status as nonpartitive and nonindependent immanent determination. A strong-simplicity/privative approach cannot simply relabel them privations.

## 5. The common pattern

The survey makes four requirements unavoidable for any physics-facing candidate, even though different programmes meet them differently:

\[
\mathcal B
=
\langle
\mathsf{PrimitiveStructure},
\mathsf{RuleOrDynamics},
\mathsf{RegimeOrLimit},
\mathsf{RecoveryMap}
\rangle.
\]

1. **Primitive structure:** an exact nonspatiotemporal mathematical resource.
2. **Rule or dynamics:** a determinate operation, action, or evolution/selection principle.
3. **Regime or limit:** a precise coarse-graining, scaling, continuum, or collective regime.
4. **Recovery map:** criteria connecting the output to geometry, physical dynamics, and eventually empirical quantities.

PS-LP-1 and PS-LP-2 lacked the first two items in a usable form:

\[
\mathsf{PS\text{-}LP\text{-}1/2}
\not\vdash
\mathcal B.
\]

## 6. Consequence for weak and strong simplicity

### 6.1 Weak simplicity

Weak simplicity has a potential route only if it carries real explanatory weight:

\[
\mathsf{WeakSimple}(P)
+
\mathsf{SpecifiedImmanentStructure}
+
\mathsf{Bridge}
\longrightarrow
\mathcal B.
\]

But it must independently establish that the structure is positive, nonpartitive, nonindependent, nontemporal, nonspatial, nonbrute, and physically recoverable. The existing physics programmes cannot simply be called “the Point’s formal profiles” to satisfy these conditions.

### 6.2 Strong simplicity and privation

Strong simplicity with merely privative limitation faces the sharper constructive burden:

\[
\mathsf{PrivationOnly}
\longrightarrow
\mathsf{DeterminatePrimitiveStructure}
\]

has not been supplied. The surveyed programmes all use positive mathematical resources. That does not prove that a purely privative route cannot work; it shows that no surveyed programme furnishes it ready-made.

## 7. Truth-directed next move

The next useful move is not a new unrestricted Point–Sphere revision. It is to choose one **independent mathematical bridge target** and declare, in advance, whether that target is:

\[
\begin{array}{c|l}
\text{derived} & \text{must follow from the Point–Sphere core without being presupposed}\\
\text{postulated} & \text{is an added physical hypothesis, not a deduction from the core}\\
\text{instrumental} & \text{is a comparison model used only to state what physics recovery demands}
\end{array}
\]

Three possible targets are:

\[
\begin{array}{c|l|l}
\text{target} & \text{first nonspatial output} & \text{principal risk}\\
\hline
\text{causal-set-like} & \text{order plus discreteness} & \text{plurality/relation and dynamics are added}\\
\text{entanglement-like} & \text{state/algebra/network} & \text{subsystems and factorization are added}\\
\text{GFT-like} & \text{quanta, group data, collective state} & \text{large positive physical package is added}
\end{array}
\]

Only after one target is selected can a fresh candidate honestly test whether Point–Sphere provides a derivation, merely coexists with the mathematics, or is being supplemented by a new physical theory.
