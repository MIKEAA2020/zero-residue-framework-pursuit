# AP3I Formal Self-Presentation Grammar Test v1: Executed Result

## Correction implemented

AP3H’s corrected task is not to search for a new ontological source of modes. The core identity is held fixed:

\[
\text{mode}=	ext{aspect}=	ext{content of self-knowledge}.
\]

The formal question is the relation \(\smile_{\rm SP}\): which contents are jointly self-presentable as one bounded finite mode?

## Test

All \(2^6=64\) reflexive symmetric compatibility grammars on four content positions were enumerated. Modes were maximal proper compatible contexts.

The test applied four core-motivated requirements:

1. bounded real appearance: each content appears in at least one proper maximal context;
2. plural appearance: at least two proper contexts exist;
3. nonexclusive co-presentation: the context-overlap graph is connected; and
4. reflexivity, implicit in the relation representation.

It then added one explicitly **candidate**, not yet derived, formal condition:

5. nonatomic relational self-presentation: every maximal proper context has at least two contents.

## Executed results

All checks passed.

| Grammar | Result |
|---|---|
| Universal compatibility | Fails bounded appearance: its only maximal context is the whole, so no proper finite mode occurs in this formalisation |
| Discrete compatibility | Fails nonatomic relational self-presentation: it yields only singleton contexts |
| IAR-like two-overlapping-triple grammar | Passes all five conditions |
| Full relation family | **37 of 64** grammars pass all five conditions |

The core appearance commitment therefore performs real work: universal compatibility is excluded **within this maximal-proper-context implementation**. If finite self-presentation must be relational rather than a bare singleton, the discrete grammar is also excluded.

But the result remains underdetermined:

\[
\#\{\smile:\mathrm{F1}\text{--}\mathrm{F5}\}=37.
\]

## Interpretation

This is progress, not a renewed foundational debt.

- The core identity provides the intended ontological reading of qualified modes.
- The core reality of finite appearance excludes a grammar with no proper finite context.
- The candidate relationality condition rules out purely atomic “modes,” but its warrant still belongs to the phenomenological or formal route.
- Many grammars remain. Neither symmetry nor the finite enumeration selects IAR.

The grammar question is now sharply localized: which further constraint captures self-presentation rather than merely an arbitrary compatibility graph?

## Next route

AP3J is the phenomenological audit. It will test whether prereflective self-presence, bounded/horizonal access, and non-objectifying mineness support a further grammar constraint. It must not demand that every finite mode explicitly recognise itself as the ground; that would confuse structural self-presence with reflective metaphysical knowledge.

The physical route remains separately downstream: it must derive or constrain grammars through records, locality, integration, or dynamics without importing the desired partition.
