## Frozen conditions

- **SSS:** strict simplicity—no independently variable intrinsic differentiating component/property in the ground.
- **CEA:** contingent exclusive actualization—one of genuinely open non-equivalent totalities is actual relative to the same complete intrinsic ground.
- **Weak NBD:** some declared difference-maker exists.
- **Strong NBD:** the difference-maker must be non-circular and non-primitive relative to the asymmetry it explains.

Strong NBD is the relevant condition if “complete intelligibility” means no unexplained terminal selection. It is not bare logic; GC1 proved that it must be explicitly defended.

## Main conditional theorem

Within the declared taxonomy:

\[
\boxed{
\mathsf{SSS}(G)
\land
\mathsf{CEA}(G;w_0,w_1)
\land
\mathsf{NBD}_{s}
\Longrightarrow
\bot.
}
\]

The proof is not that contingent creation is syntactically contradictory. It is a case analysis:

1. An intrinsic non-primitive differentiator violates the frozen strong reading of simplicity.
2. An extrinsic single-act relation can name the actual effect, but without an independent account of why the act terminates in this effect rather than its alternative, it is circular or primitive at the relevant point.
3. Primitive free agency can be retained, but precisely as a terminal selector. It passes weak NBD only because the primitive is allowed to count as a difference-maker; it fails strong NBD.
4. Necessary determination can eliminate the contingent-selection asymmetry, but it becomes a determinate-necessary realization rule and owes the R1 exact-totality bridge.

Thus a strictly simple, globally intelligible ground cannot be both strongly non-brute in its differentiations and libertarianly contingent in exclusive creation **under these frozen definitions**.

## What the result does not establish

The stronger classical reply remains formally available:

> an ultimate agent’s primitive free act is intelligible even though it is not derivable from an antecedent differentiating reason.

GC2 does not show that this reply is a logical contradiction. It establishes its exact price:

\[
\mathsf{SSS}
+
\mathsf{CEA}
+
\mathsf{NBD}_{w}
+
\mathsf{primitive\ agency}
\

\text{is formally coherent at the toy level, but not strongly globally intelligible.}
\]

A defender may reject strong NBD or argue that a free act is non-primitive in a way not captured by the audit. Either response must now supply a positive, non-circular account rather than merely invoke divine freedom.

## Consequence for the Appearance comparison

This result confirms the important asymmetry without prematurely declaring victory:

- **Contingent creation** incurs a structural selector debt under strong NBD.
- **Necessary determination** avoids that debt only by moving to a different realization-rule family and acquiring the exact-totality-bridge debt already exposed in R1.
- **Appearance plus plenitude** may avoid external selection, but it still owes GC3: an independently typed genuine-aspect domain and a maximal-expression theorem.

Therefore:

\[
\text{failure of contingent-creation selection under strong NBD}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{truth of Appearance}.
\]

It does establish that the global-coherence argument is no longer merely rhetorical. It has a sharp conditional result against one rival architecture.
