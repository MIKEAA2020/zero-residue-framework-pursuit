## 1. The strongest available formulation

The proposal can be stated without relying on the unstable phrases “simulation,” “all coherent things,” or a literal geometry claim:

1. **Ground:** there is one non-derivative, simple, non-spatiotemporal, intrinsically aware reality \(G\).
2. **Non-dual appearance:** finite perspectives are not separate substances produced alongside \(G\). They are real *as modes/aspects of* \(G\), while the inference from a bounded perspective to a numerically independent self is an error.
3. **Finitude:** a mode is structurally bounded access to the whole, not a piece cut out of the ground. Limited knowledge, self-location, and the sense of separateness are features of the mode; they do not make \(G\) ignorant or incomplete.
4. **Necessary plenitude:** every globally admissible mode/aspect is realised. There is no ultimate selection of one mode over equally admissible alternatives, no free-standing exclusion, and no second substantive reality competing with the ground.
5. **Physics:** physics describes the stable structural grammar internal to modes; it does not directly describe the simple ground. Quantum branches, records, spacetime, and agents are to be treated as mode-level structures, not as independent ontological competitors.

This is stronger than crude “the world is illusion.” It insists that the experienced order, suffering, records, and physics are real at the modal level, while denying that their apparent boundedness is the boundary of an independently existing substance.

## 2. Why this package is genuinely attractive

The latest continuation identifies real explanatory attractions, not merely rhetorical ones.

### 2.1 It avoids a creator–creation production relation

Literal creation must explain how a simple complete ground stands in a real producing/dependence relation to an order numerically distinct from it, while remaining unaltered and non-composite. Appearance replaces a two-term production picture with an aspect/whole picture. This can remove the particular burdens called reception, concurrence, and an apparently new relation in the ground.

### 2.2 It reframes ignorance without locating a defect in the ground

The cake/slice line of thought is strongest when stated carefully: a perspective can have partial access without the whole itself lacking anything. The felt sense of boundedness is real as experience; the extra inference—“therefore I am a separate self whose being ends at this boundary”—is the proposed illusion. That avoids the crude trilemma “either the Absolute is ignorant, or a second substance is ignorant, or nothing is ignorant.”

### 2.3 It removes a selector from contrastive questions

If all admissible aspects are realised, questions of the form “why did the ground pick *this* world/branch/harm rather than another?” do not apply at the ultimate level. This is a genuine advantage over a theory that posits a selector yet provides no non-arbitrary selection principle.

### 2.4 It gives a unified reading of finite privation

On the strongest version, pain, ignorance, and non-remembrance are not positive defects in the ground. They are modal limitations associated with bounded access. This does not deny lived suffering; it explains why an unbounded ground need not itself be damaged by the limits displayed in a finite mode.

### 2.5 It has a promising formal shape

The best formal seed is not “all imaginable things are real.” It is an **orbit-completion** principle:

\[
R(g\!\cdot\!a)=R(a),\qquad
G\curvearrowright\mathcal A\text{ transitively},\qquad
\exists a\,R(a)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\forall b\in\mathcal A\,R(b).
\]

If \(\mathcal A\) is a physically substantive—not merely gauge—transitive orbit of admissible aspects, and realisation \(R\) is equivariant, then the realisation of one member entails realisation of the full orbit. This is the cleanest steelman of “no preference, hence no arbitrary exclusion.” It also respects the completed T2 result: invariant data cannot uniquely select one member of a transitive orbit.

This could make plenitude a precise belt hypothesis: not an unrestricted assertion that every describable object exists, but a claim that full non-gauge symmetry orbits of a specified admissible structure are realised.

## 3. What the line-level source does **not** establish

Several late claims are forceful but are not carried by their arguments. They should not be promoted to core merely because they are attractive.

### 3.1 No-preference does not by itself entail all-aspects-realised

The move

\[
\text{no preference}\;\Rightarrow\;\text{no exclusion}\;\Rightarrow\;\text{all aspects actual}
\]

needs further premises. Symmetry of a law, a state, or an admissibility relation does not automatically determine an ontology of realisation. It can support equal treatment, equivariance, or an invariant measure; it does not alone show that every member of a candidate space exists.

The orbit-completion theorem above works only after the following are supplied: a defined aspect space \(\mathcal A\), a substantive group action, a non-gauge criterion, an actual realisation predicate, transitivity, and the existence of one realised member. It establishes the completion of a registered orbit—not all mathematically coherent structures, all physical theories, or every imaginable suffering.

### 3.2 The point/sphere image is a heuristic, not an exact derivation

The appended dialogue repeatedly calls point-to-sphere “exact.” At line level this cannot be sustained literally:

- A point at the origin is fixed by rotations; its orbit is a point, not a sphere.
- The orbit of a *nonzero* point in a pre-existing \(\mathbb R^3\) under \(SO(3)\) is a sphere. This already assumes ambient dimension, norm, radius, and group action—the very structural data that the proposed metaphor seeks to derive.
- \(S_r^2\) for \(r>0\) is not identical to its \(r=0\) degeneration \(\{0\}\). The latter does not mathematically “contain every radius” or “hold everything at once.”
- A surface fraction is not the whole sphere, and a spatial slice is not literally identical with its whole. The analogies can illuminate aspecthood but cannot prove identity of ground and mode.

The image survives as a productive design constraint: any formal model should make spacetime, direction, locality, and bounded perspective derivative from a non-spatiotemporal description. It cannot presently show that a dimensionless simple ground is a sphere, that all aspects coexist, or that the Standard Model/GR follow.

### 3.3 Appearance changes rather than automatically solves the perspective problem

The later “partial aspect” account is a major improvement over a dismissive illusionism. But a physics-capable version still must state what a mode is, what individuates modes, what makes records public among modes, and why bounded access has the particular stable form observed. Saying “the whole under an aspect” is a metaphysical schema, not yet the required individuation map.

The issue is no longer crudely “who is ignorant?” It is: what structure makes a limited perspective real as a mode without turning it into either an independent substance or an unexplicated label? This is the same bridge that the prior programme calls decombination/perspective individuation.

### 3.4 Plenitude does not dissolve quantum probability

The source correctly later retracts the claim that indexical location alone dissolves the Born problem. In Everettian and self-locating settings, all branches may be real while rational credence, observed relative frequencies, and empirical predictions still require a measure or probability rule.

The status is therefore:

- Declining weights yields a metaphysical plurality with no quantum-probabilistic prediction.
- Adding weights requires an extra principle. Symmetry alone on projective Hilbert space does not straightforwardly give Born outcome weights; a measure over states is not the same object as a conditional measure over outcomes for a fixed state.
- Decision-theoretic, non-contextual, envariance, or other derivations each add substantive assumptions.

Thus plenitude can remove an ultimate selection story, but it cannot make probability, typicality, or the empirical content of quantum theory disappear.

### 3.5 Dissolving a contrastive evil question leaves an axiological question

Necessary plenitude can remove “why was this harm selected instead of a harmless alternative?” if there is no selector. That is a genuine structural gain. It does not settle the non-contrastive question: why does a perfect ground necessarily have/appear through pain-bearing modes at all, and how are the reality and moral urgency of their suffering to be understood? A response that suffering is modal privation may locate the phenomenon, but it does not by itself establish that no further ethical explanation is owed.

This is not an aggregate-count objection. Each mode’s suffering matters within that mode whether there is one such mode or many.

## 5. Honest comparative assessment

### Within the strengthened non-dual premises

If one accepts all of the following:

1. a simple, non-derivative ground;
2. awareness as its intrinsic nature;
3. no numerically distinct created order;
4. modes as real aspects rather than substances; and
5. an equivariant realisation principle completing every admissible aspect orbit,

then non-dual appearance with necessary plenitude is exceptionally unified. It removes an arbitrary selector, avoids a literal ground-to-creature production relation, and gives a coherent way to regard branch-relative/public reality as real at a modal level.

### Under neutral public comparison

The package is not yet entitled to “most likely ultimate truth.” Its load-bearing claims remain unproved: the ground-to-awareness bridge; the aspect/individuation map; the move from no preference to realisation; the scope of admissibility; the treatment of probability; and the recovery of GR/QM. Real dependent creation, neutral/Russellian monism, and other non-dual accounts are not refuted merely by being less minimal under the preferred metric.

The fairest verdict is:

> Simple-ground, non-dual appearance, and necessary plenitude is now one of the framework’s most elegant and serious architectures. It is a powerful research hypothesis, not a closed deduction or a physics solution.

## 6. Productive next formal moves

If this architecture is to be developed rather than merely admired, the next work must be mathematical and comparative:

1. **Specify an aspect model.** Define a derivative state/algebra/category \(\mathcal A\), a perspective construction, and exact equivalence relations. Do not identify the simple ground itself with a geometric point in the model.
2. **State the realisation axiom.** Specify what counts as a physical/non-gauge orbit, why its action is substantive, and whether orbit completion—not unrestricted plenitude—is the intended rule.
3. **Define individuation and records.** Recover the T1–T5 requirements: internal clocks, record robustness, clock-change coherence, and local proper-time physics.
4. **Confront probability.** Give a Born-rule theorem or state explicitly that the architecture is non-empirical metaphysics. Indexicality alone is insufficient.
5. **Compare creation fairly.** Formalise the alleged production-relation primitive and test whether a classical participation/concurrence account necessarily introduces it, rather than assuming it does.
6. **Preserve ethical reality.** State how real modal suffering grounds practical obligations without dismissing it as a merely verbal appearance.

Until these are done, the architecture should guide the belt as a powerful hypothesis and a source of precise tasks, not be announced as a final resolution.
