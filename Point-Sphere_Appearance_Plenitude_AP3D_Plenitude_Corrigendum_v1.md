# Corrigendum to AP3D: Non-unique Boundaries Are Not Automatically a Failure Under Plenitude

## Correction

AP3D correctly found 14 inequivalent decompositions satisfying the current structural/information candidate conditions on a rigid graph. Its suggestion that a successor must therefore select **one** preferred boundary was too strong for the conditional necessary-plenitude architecture.

Under the architecture now being tested, the relevant question is not:

> Which one candidate boundary is the uniquely real perspective?

It is:

> Which candidate profiles are genuine non-gauge modes, and if they are genuine, may all of them coexist as actual nondual aspects?

The conditional plenitude route says:

\[
\forall m\in\mathcal M\,[\operatorname{GenuineMode}(m)\Rightarrow R(m)],
\]

not that a final selector chooses exactly one \(m\).

## What AP3D still establishes

AP3D establishes that the present graph/information gate defines a **candidate family**, not a unique perspective identity. Its 14 decompositions are not related by a graph automorphism, so they cannot be dismissed as label copies.

It does **not** establish:

\[
\operatorname{Candidate}(m)\Rightarrow\operatorname{GenuineMode}(m).
\]

That membership bridge—not unique selection—is the remaining debt. Treating every mathematically available restriction as phenomenal would be an uncontrolled proliferation thesis, not a consequence of plenitude.

## Corrected programme consequence

AP3E is redirected to a nonexclusive coexistence and gauge audit of the AP3D candidate family. It asks whether all candidates can jointly be restrictions of one global support, and identifies the exact condition still needed before conditional plenitude can apply.

A later continuation needs a justified \(\operatorname{GenuineMode}\) criterion, such as a physical record/locality closure law, a stability/integration law, or a formally defined intrinsic-aspect relation. It need not supply a unique selector among already-genuine modes.
