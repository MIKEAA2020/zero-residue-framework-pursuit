## Results

### C-Nec-D: unique determination does not eliminate the determination bridge

The toy family realizes one displayed element, \(\{0\}\). But relabelling \(\Omega\) maps that displayed element to 2. Thus the numeral “0” has no invariant privilege. The unique result follows only after a map \(f(E)\) has been supplied.

**Surviving lesson:** C-Nec-D does not need a selector among several realized worlds, but it does need an exact-totality/determination bridge. Calling the chosen output “the necessary one” cannot replace an account of \(f\).

### C-Nec-O: finite invariant measure exists, but plurality needs more

For the finite cyclic action \(C_4\curvearrowright\Omega\), the orbit of 0 is all four elements and the uniform \(1/4\) distribution is exactly invariant. Yet the same orbit has:

- one member in the gauge quotient, if all members are descriptions of one totality; and
- four members only under a distinct substantive-totality reading.

**Surviving lesson:** a group orbit alone does not establish a plurality of created orders. R-O needs an independent non-gauge distinctness criterion in addition to an action and equivariance bridge.

### Non-compact warning

For translation action of \(\mathbb Z\) on itself, no countably additive normalized equal-weight invariant probability exists: a common point mass is either positive and sums without bound, or zero and sums to zero.

**Surviving lesson:** finite-group uniformity/Haar-style language cannot be promoted into a general probability solution. A physically relevant non-compact orbit needs its own normalisation and observable construction.

### C-Cont: selector indispensability is explicit

Holding \(E\) fixed while varying \(q\in\Omega\) realizes four different singleton outputs. The selector parameter is indispensable in this family.

**Surviving lesson:** C-Cont is not refuted by containing an extra selector; that is its defining resource. Its live debt is to state how the selector is neither an arbitrary primitive nor fully entailed by \(E\), in which case it would reduce to C-Nec-D.

1. C-Nec-D cannot call an unnamed \(f\) “no extra structure.”
2. C-Nec-O cannot call a gauge orbit many real totalities or assume a finite-orbit measure generalises.
3. C-Cont cannot hide \(q\) while claiming that \(E\) alone fixes the result.
